## Course Preface & Mental Model

**Control of Autonomous Systems** (**5 ECTS**, Semester 1) bridges Bachelor control theory directly into autonomous vehicles and mobile/aerial robots. In your Bachelor's linear systems class, transfer functions and state matrices $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}$ lived cleanly on paper. Here, those exact equations govern vehicles whose actuators saturate, whose sensors carry noise and latency, and whose control loops run digitally inside simulation and autopilot code.

It serves as the analytical prerequisite for Semester 2's **Guidance, Navigation and Control (`GNC`)** course—teaching you how to turn physical motion models into stable, tuned feedback controllers (`PID`, state feedback, and `Model Predictive Control`) tested against noisy sensor streams.

### Modeling, Control & Sensor Fusion Scope
- **Vehicle Motion Modeling:** Deriving kinematic and dynamic state-space descriptions of autonomous platforms, operating-point linearization, and actuator limits.
- **Feedback Control Architectures:** Connecting classical `LTI-SISO` / `PID` loop shaping with modern state-space pole placement/observers and optimization-based **Model Predictive Control (MPC)**.
- **Sensor Fusion & State Estimation:** Combining noisy multi-rate measurements (IMU, wheel/visual/GNSS cues) to reconstruct clean state feedback signals.

### What You Actually Do in the Lab
You implement controllers in **software-in-the-loop virtual environments** (MATLAB/Simulink coupled with simulated vehicles and ROS topics), design experiments showing tracking performance vs. disturbance rejection, and compile results into mandatory project reports—then defend why your gains, discretization, or MPC horizon behave the way they do in an **individual oral exam**.
