## Course Preface & Mental Model

**Introduction to Drone Technology** (`T550063101`, **5 ECTS**, Semester 1) is SDU's practical onboarding into multirotor **Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS)** specifically framed for robotics engineers rather than pure aerospace students. While traditional robotics courses teach manipulator kinematics or wheeled odometry, a drone adds hard constraints that fail catastrophically if wired or configured wrong: thrust-to-weight margins, high-frequency IMU vibration, magnetic interference on compasses, GNSS dropouts, RC/telemetry link budgets, and operational air-risk assessment.

Think of this course as learning how every physical and software subsystem inside a real quadrotor connects—from battery/ESC power and Pixhawk autopilot loops up to companion-computer messaging (`Python`, `C`, `ROS`)—so that in Semester 2 you are ready to build full autonomous GNC and perception stacks on top of reliable hardware.

### Subsystem Architecture Taught
- **UAV Platforms & Actuation:** Frame geometries, brushless motors, ESCs, propellers, power distribution, and basic flight-time/payload trade-offs.
- **Flight Controllers & Attitude Estimation:** Role of the real-time flight management unit (e.g., Pixhawk/PX4), IMU gyro/accelerometer/magnetometer integration, and inner attitude stabilization.
- **Navigation & Wireless Links:** GNSS positioning principles/limitations, telemetry & command links (`MAVLink` / `QGroundControl` workflows), and RF/link considerations.
- **UAS Safety & Risk Assessment:** Pre-flight checklists, failsafes, operational risk frameworks (essential for flying at SDU/HCA Airport test ranges).

### What You Actually Do in the Lab
Rather than abstract derivations, sessions follow brief theory briefings immediately into **team bench and testbed exercises**: wiring/configuring multirotor subsystems, verifying sensor/communication streams cleanly in software, and documenting experiments rigorously in **mandatory team lab reports**. Those reports are not busywork—your final **individual oral exam** uses your own submitted lab data and system behavior as the starting point for theoretical questioning.

### Bachelor Bridges & Preparation
- Review hardware interfaces, microcontrollers, and timing concepts in **[Computer Architecture](/dynamic-courses/computer-architecture/)** and power/analog fundamentals in **[Elettronica](/dynamic-courses/elettronica/)**.
- Before arriving in Odense, getting comfortable with basic Linux terminal workflows, Git, and simple `ROS 2` + `Python`/`C++` node messaging pays massive dividends during Semester 1 labs.
