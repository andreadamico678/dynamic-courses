## Course Preface & Mental Model

**Mechanical Aerial Systems** (`T550076101` / `T550064101`, **5 ECTS**, Semester 2) is where software-heavy engineers get their hands dirty with aerodynamics, airframe mechanics, and physical fabrication. Autonomy algorithms only perform as well as the physical airframe carrying them: an under-stiff arm excites structural resonance inside your IMU, poorly matched propeller/motor geometry slashes hover endurance, and shifting center of gravity destabilizes pitch authority.

Instead of treating drones as off-the-shelf black boxes, this course takes you end-to-end from aerodynamic calculation and CAD modeling straight to **3D printing, assembling, and flight-testing a physical prototype**.

### Aerodynamics & Airframe Engineering Scope
- **Aerodynamics & Rotor Physics:** Airfoil lift/drag polars, blade-element / momentum intuition for propellers and rotors, thrust, torque, and hover/forward-flight power draw.
- **Configurations & Stability:** Multirotor vs. fixed-wing vs. VTOL hybrid trade-offs, center-of-gravity placement, static/dynamic stability margins, and actuator sizing.
- **Materials & Rapid Prototyping:** Selecting lightweight structural composites/polymers, designing printable load-bearing parts for fatigue/vibration resilience, and workshop integration.

### What You Actually Do in the Lab
Teams go through the full physical product cycle: build a virtual **CAD model**, run mechanical/structural simulations to verify stiffness and weight targets, fabricate parts via **3D printing** and lab workshops, integrate avionics/propulsion, **flight-test your prototype**, and defend all engineering trade-offs in your reports and oral exam.
