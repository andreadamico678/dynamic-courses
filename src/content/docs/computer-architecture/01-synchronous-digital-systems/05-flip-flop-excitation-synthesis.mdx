## Introduction

After merging equivalent states via an [Implication Table](/dynamic-courses/computer-architecture/01-synchronous-digital-systems/04-implication-table-minimization/), the minimal state set $S_{\text{min}}$ must be encoded as binary bit vectors stored in $k = \lceil \log_2 \lvert S_{\text{min}} \rvert \rceil$ clocked flip-flops. **Flip-flop excitation synthesis** works backward from each required state-bit transition $Q_i \to Q_i^*$ to the bistable control inputs ($J_i, K_i$, $S_i, R_i$, $T_i$, or $D_i$), treats every unassigned binary code as a full row of don't-cares (`X`), and extracts minimal SOP logic via [Karnaugh Maps](/dynamic-courses/computer-architecture/01-synchronous-digital-systems/01-karnaugh-map-minimization/).

### Three-State JK Flip-Flop Synthesis

Let us synthesize the reduced 3-state Mealy machine ($S_A, S_B, S_D$) derived in [Implication Table Minimization](/dynamic-courses/computer-architecture/01-synchronous-digital-systems/04-implication-table-minimization/) using **JK flip-flops**.

1. **Register Dimensioning & Binary Encoding:**
   With $\lvert S_{\text{min}} \rvert = 3$, we need $k = \lceil \log_2 3 \rceil = 2$ flip-flops ($Q_1, Q_0$). Assigning lexicographical codes $S_A = 00$, $S_B = 01$, and $S_D = 10$ leaves $Q_1 Q_0 = 11$ as an **unused state**.

2. **Transition and JK Excitation Table:**
   Recall the JK excitation rules: $0 \to 0$ gives $(J, K) = (0, X)$, $0 \to 1$ gives $(1, X)$, $1 \to 0$ gives $(X, 1)$, and $1 \to 1$ gives $(X, 0)$. For both rows of unused state `11`, **all** flip-flop inputs and output $u$ are set to `X`:

| Present State $Q_1 Q_0$ | Input $X$ | Next State $Q_1^* Q_0^*$ | $Q_1 \to Q_1^*$ | $J_1$ | $K_1$ | $Q_0 \to Q_0^*$ | $J_0$ | $K_0$ | Output $u$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **$00$ ($S_A$)** | $0$ | $01$ ($S_B$) | $0 \to 0$ | $0$ | $X$ | $0 \to 1$ | $1$ | $X$ | $0$ |
| **$00$ ($S_A$)** | $1$ | $00$ ($S_A$) | $0 \to 0$ | $0$ | $X$ | $0 \to 0$ | $0$ | $X$ | $0$ |
| **$01$ ($S_B$)** | $0$ | $10$ ($S_D$) | $0 \to 1$ | $1$ | $X$ | $1 \to 0$ | $X$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| **$01$ ($S_B$)** | $1$ | $00$ ($S_A$) | $0 \to 0$ | $0$ | $X$ | $1 \to 0$ | $X$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| **$10$ ($S_D$)** | $0$ | $00$ ($S_A$) | $1 \to 0$ | $X$ | $1$ | $0 \to 0$ | $0$ | $X$ | $1$ |
| **$10$ ($S_D$)** | $1$ | $01$ ($S_B$) | $1 \to 0$ | $X$ | $1$ | $0 \to 1$ | $1$ | $X$ | $0$ |
| **$11$ (Unused)** | $0$ | $XX$ | $X \to X$ | $X$ | $X$ | $X \to X$ | $X$ | $X$ | $X$ |
| **$11$ (Unused)** | $1$ | $XX$ | $X \to X$ | $X$ | $X$ | $X \to X$ | $X$ | $X$ | $X$ |

3. **Three-Variable Karnaugh Maps ($Q_1 Q_0 \backslash X$ in Gray Order `00, 01, 11, 10`):**

   - **Map for $J_1$ and Output $u$:**

     | $Q_1 Q_0 \backslash X$ | $0$ ($J_1$) | $1$ ($J_1$) | $0$ ($u$) | $1$ ($u$) |
     | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
     | **$00$** | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |
     | **$01$** | **$1$** | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |
     | **$11$** | **$X$** | $X$ | **$X$** | $X$ |
     | **$10$** | $X$ | $X$ | **$1$** | $0$ |

     In $J_1$, $(01, 0) = 1$ merges with $(11, 0) = X \implies J_1 = Q_0 \overline{X}$. In $u$, $(10, 0) = 1$ merges with $(11, 0) = X \implies u = Q_1 \overline{X}$.

   - **Maps for $K_1$, $J_0$, and $K_0$:**

     | $Q_1 Q_0 \backslash X$ | $0$ ($K_1$) | $1$ ($K_1$) | $0$ ($J_0$) | $1$ ($J_0$) | $0$ ($K_0$) | $1$ ($K_0$) |
     | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
     | **$00$** | **$X$** | **$X$** | **$1$** | $0$ | **$X$** | **$X$** |
     | **$01$** | **$X$** | **$X$** | **$X$** | $X$ | **$1$** | **$1$** |
     | **$11$** | **$X$** | **$X$** | $X$ | **$X$** | **$X$** | **$X$** |
     | **$10$** | **$1$** | **$1$** | $0$ | **$1$** | **$X$** | **$X$** |

     Every single one of the **8 cells** in both $K_1$ and $K_0$ contains either `1` or `X` (zero `0`s anywhere!), so all 8 cells merge to eliminate $Q_1, Q_0$, and $X$ completely, tying both pins straight to logic high: $K_1 = 1$ and $K_0 = 1$! Meanwhile, in $J_0$, $(00, 0)$ merges with $(01, 0)$ into $\overline{Q}_1 \overline{X}$ and $(10, 1)$ merges with $(11, 1)$ into $Q_1 X$:

$$J_1 = Q_0 \overline{X}, \qquad K_1 = 1, \qquad J_0 = \overline{Q}_1 \overline{X} + Q_1 X, \qquad K_0 = 1, \qquad u = Q_1 \overline{X}$$

### State Register Dimensioning

Encoding $\lvert S_{\text{min}} \rvert$ distinct states in binary requires $k$ flip-flops, where:

$$k = \lceil \log_2 \lvert S_{\text{min}} \rvert \rceil$$

Because $k$ flip-flops provide $2^k$ binary patterns, whenever $\lvert S_{\text{min}} \rvert$ is not an exact power of two, the remaining $2^k - \lvert S_{\text{min}} \rvert$ unassigned bit patterns become **unused states** (for instance, $\lvert S_{\text{min}} \rvert = 5 \implies k = 3$ flip-flops with $8 - 5 = 3$ unused codes `101, 110, 111`).

> **Notation Bridge:** Italian examination papers frequently label encoded state bits as $y_1, y_0$ (with next states $y_1^*, y_0^*$ or $y_1^+, y_0^+$) interchangeably with flip-flop register outputs $Q_1, Q_0$ ($Q_1^*, Q_0^*$). Both notations represent the exact same physical flip-flop bits.

### Flip-Flop Excitation Equations

During analysis, a flip-flop's characteristic equation computes $Q^*$ from given inputs. During **synthesis**, you invert that equation to find which inputs force a known bit transition $Q \to Q^*$:

1. **JK Flip-Flop ($Q^* = J\overline{Q} + \overline{K}Q$):**
   - When $Q = 0$, $Q^* = J$, so $J = Q^*$ and $K = X$ (`0 -> 0` gives `0 X`; `0 -> 1` gives `1 X`).
   - When $Q = 1$, $Q^* = \overline{K}$, so $J = X$ and $K = \overline{Q^*}$ (`1 -> 0` gives `X 1`; `1 -> 1` gives `X 0`).
   - Because **every** transition places a don't-care `X` in either $J$ or $K$ ($50\%$ wildcard density), JK synthesis yields the most compact SOP logic on manual exams.

2. **SR Flip-Flop ($Q^* = S + \overline{R}Q$ subject to $S \cdot R = 0$):**
   - Holding `0 -> 0` allows $(S, R) = (0, X)$, and holding `1 -> 1` allows $(X, 0)$.
   - Changing state has **no don't-cares** because $S = R = 1$ is forbidden: `0 -> 1` requires strictly $(1, 0)$, and `1 -> 0` requires strictly $(0, 1)$.

3. **T (Toggle) Flip-Flop ($Q^* = T \oplus Q \iff T = Q \oplus Q^*$):**
   - Set $T = 1$ whenever the bit flips (`0 -> 1` or `1 -> 0`) and $T = 0$ whenever the bit stays unchanged (`0 -> 0` or `1 -> 1`).

4. **D (Data) Flip-Flop ($Q^* = D$):**
   - Copy the desired next-state bit directly into the excitation column: $D_i = Q_i^*$.

### Three-Step Synthesis Procedure

1. **Compute $k$ and Assign Binary Codes:** Calculate $k = \lceil \log_2 \lvert S_{\text{min}} \rvert \rceil$, assign sequential binary codes (`00, 01, 10, ...`) to minimal states, and list all unused $k$-bit codes.
2. **Build the Full $2^k \times \lvert X \rvert$ Excitation Table:** Tabulate every combination of $(Q_{k-1} \dots Q_0, X)$. Fill $Q_i^*$ and $u$ for reachable states, evaluate each bit transition $Q_i \to Q_i^*$ independently using the requested flip-flop type, and fill **every column of every unused-state row with `X`**.
3. **Minimize Each Control Signal via K-Maps:** Plot a Gray-coded Karnaugh map over $(Q_{k-1} \dots Q_0, X)$ for each flip-flop input and output $u$. Tie maps containing only `{1, X}` to constant `1`, tie maps containing zero `1`s to constant `0`, and read minimal SOP terms for the rest.

<Callout type="tip" title="Practical Hardware Perspective">

Modern RTL compilers (`always_ff @(posedge clk)` in SystemVerilog) synthesize synchronous FSMs almost exclusively onto standard-cell **D flip-flops**, choosing **One-Hot** encoding ($N$ flip-flops for $N$ states) on flip-flop-rich FPGAs to minimize next-state LUT depth, or binary/Gray encoding on ASICs to save area. In radiation-exposed aerospace or drone hardware, engineers explicitly route unused states back to reset `00` rather than `X` to prevent state lock-up after a single-event upset (SEU) bit flip.

</Callout>

### Exam Execution Checklist

1. Double-check which bistable the prompt requests (**FF JK**, **FF SR**, **FF T**, or **FF D**) before filling excitation columns.
2. Include explicit rows for **all** unused binary state codes and fill every excitation input and output $u$ on those rows with `X`.
3. Evaluate MSB ($Q_1 \to Q_1^*$) and LSB ($Q_0 \to Q_0^*$) transitions one column at a time to avoid bit-swap errors.
4. Order 3-variable and 4-variable K-map state rows in Gray sequence (`00, 01, 11, 10`), placing unused state `11` in the **third** row (between `01` and `10`), not at the bottom!
5. Check whether any excitation map has zero `0`s—if so, simplify it all the way to constant `1`.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistakes">

1. **Using JK Don't-Cares for SR Flip-Flops:** Writing $(S, R) = (1, X)$ on $0 \to 1$ or $(X, 1)$ on $1 \to 0$ allows the illegal $S = R = 1$ condition; SR strictly requires $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ on bit flips.
2. **Putting Row `11` at the Bottom of the K-Map:** In the excitation table, unused state `11` is listed last, but in the Gray-coded K-map (`00, 01, 11, 10`), `11` sits in **row 3**, while reachable state `10` sits in **row 4**! Swapping rows `10` and `11` when copying into K-maps corrupts every equation.
3. **Leaving Unused States as `0`:** Treating state `11` as `0` instead of `X` prevents adjacent 2-cell and 8-cell merges and yields unminimized SOP equations.

</Callout>

   Evaluating $Q_i \oplus Q_i^*$ across all 6 reachable rows (plus `X` on row `11`):

   | $Q_1 Q_0 \backslash X$ | $0$ ($T_1$) | $1$ ($T_1$) | $0$ ($T_0$) | $1$ ($T_0$) |

   - **$T_0$:** Rows `01, 11` form 4-cell block $Q_0$ (essential for $(01, 1)$), $(00, 0) + (01, 0)$ forms EPI $\overline{Q}_1 \overline{X}$, and $(10, 1) + (11, 1)$ forms EPI $Q_1 X \implies T_0 = Q_0 + \overline{Q}_1 \overline{X} + Q_1 X$.
