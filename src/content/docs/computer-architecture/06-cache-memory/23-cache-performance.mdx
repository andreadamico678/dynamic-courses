## Memory Latency and Execution Time

Every architectural adjustment in Chapters 19–22 pulls cache parameters in opposite directions: raising capacity $C$, associativity $N$, or block size $B$ lowers miss rate $\text{MR}$ at the cost of slower hit time $t_{\text{hit}}$ or larger miss penalty $\text{MP}$. To arbitrate those trade-offs quantitatively—and plug real memory behavior back into [CPU Performance Equation](/dynamic-courses/computer-architecture/02-computer-performance/06-cpu-performance-equation/) and [Pipeline Performance](/dynamic-courses/computer-architecture/05-pipelined-processor/18-pipeline-performance/)—we evaluate **Average Memory Access Time** ($\text{AMAT}$) and **memory-stall $\text{CPI}$**.

<Divider label="1 · Example" />

### Ranking Caches and Adding L2

Suppose we benchmark three $16\text{ KiB}$ `L1` candidates for our RV32IM processor:
- **Cache A** (Direct-Mapped): $t_{\text{hit}} = 1.00\text{ ns}$, $\text{MR} = 5.0\%$ ($0.050$), $\text{MP} = 40\text{ ns}$.
- **Cache B** (2-Way, larger block): $t_{\text{hit}} = 1.05\text{ ns}$, $\text{MR} = 4.7\%$ ($0.047$), $\text{MP} = 42\text{ ns}$.
- **Cache C** (4-Way, larger block): $t_{\text{hit}} = 1.10\text{ ns}$, $\text{MR} = 4.5\%$ ($0.045$), $\text{MP} = 42\text{ ns}$.

Evaluating $\text{AMAT} = t_{\text{hit}} + \text{MR} \cdot \text{MP}$ for each design:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{AMAT}_A &= 1.00 + (0.050 \times 40) = 1.00 + 2.000 = 3.000\text{ ns} \\
\text{AMAT}_B &= 1.05 + (0.047 \times 42) = 1.05 + 1.974 = 3.024\text{ ns} \\
\text{AMAT}_C &= 1.10 + (0.045 \times 42) = 1.10 + 1.890 = 2.990\text{ ns}
\end{aligned}
$$

Because $\text{AMAT}$ is a **latency** metric, smaller $\text{AMAT}$ means **higher performance**:
1. **1st: Cache C** ($2.990\text{ ns}$) — its $0.5\%$ miss-rate drop saves enough DRAM stalls to outweigh its $+0.10\text{ ns}$ hit-time tax.
2. **2nd: Cache A** ($3.000\text{ ns}$) — beats Cache B because B's higher $t_{\text{hit}}$ and $\text{MP}$ cost more than a modest $0.3\%$ miss reduction saves.
3. **3rd: Cache B** ($3.024\text{ ns}$).

Now insert a unified `L2` cache ($t_{\text{hit},L2} = 5.0\text{ ns}$, local miss rate $\text{MR}_{L2} = 10\%$, DRAM penalty $\text{MP}_{L2} = 40\text{ ns}$) beneath Cache A, as shown in [the two-level hierarchy ladder](#fig-memory-hierarchy-ladder). Cache A's miss penalty drops from $40\text{ ns}$ to $\text{AMAT}_{L2} = 5.0 + (0.10 \times 40) = 9.0\text{ ns}$, slashing overall latency to $\text{AMAT} = 1.00 + (0.050 \times 9.0) = 1.450\text{ ns}$—more than twice as fast!

We start with single-level $\text{AMAT}$ and its break-even design inequalities, extend the latency model to nested two-level (`L1` + `L2`) hierarchies, and finally integrate instruction and data cache stalls into effective processor $\text{CPI}$ and execution time $T_{\text{exec}}$.

### Single-Level AMAT and Threshold Inequalities

For a single-level cache, every memory reference pays the hit lookup time $t_{\text{hit}}$, and the fraction $\text{MR}$ that misses pays an additional miss penalty $\text{MP}$ (written $M$ and $P$ in some exam sheets; both conventions denote the exact same quantities):

$$\text{AMAT} = t_{\text{hit}} + \text{MR} \cdot \text{MP}$$

Notice the asymmetric sensitivity: $t_{\text{hit}}$ is multiplied by $1.0$ (paid on $100\%$ of accesses), whereas $\text{MP}$ is scaled down by $\text{MR} \ll 1$ (typically $0.01\text{--}0.05$). Consequently, a seemingly tiny $+0.1\text{ ns}$ increase in $t_{\text{hit}}$ impacts $\text{AMAT}$ as much as a $+2\text{ ns}$ increase in $\text{MP}$ at $\text{MR} = 5\%$.

When designing a candidate **Cache B** to perform no worse than reference **Cache A**, enforce the latency non-inferiority inequality:

$$\text{AMAT}_B \le \text{AMAT}_A \iff t_{\text{hit},B} + \text{MR}_B \cdot \text{MP}_B \le t_{\text{hit},A} + \text{MR}_A \cdot \text{MP}_A$$

Solving for whichever target parameter is left free yields the two exact design bounds:

1. **Max Hit Time** ($t_{\text{hit},B,\max}$):
   $$t_{\text{hit},B} \le \text{AMAT}_A - \text{MR}_B \cdot \text{MP}_B$$
   For instance, against $\text{AMAT}_A = 1.0 + 0.04 \times 40 = 2.60\text{ ns}$, a candidate with $\text{MR}_C = 3.5\%$ and $\text{MP}_C = 44\text{ ns}$ satisfies $t_{\text{hit},C} \le 2.60 - (0.035 \times 44) = 2.60 - 1.540 = 1.060\text{ ns}$.
2. **Max Miss Rate** ($\text{MR}_{B,\max}$):
   $$\text{MR}_B \le \frac{\text{AMAT}_A - t_{\text{hit},B}}{\text{MP}_B}$$
   For instance, against $\text{AMAT}_A = 1.1 + 0.03 \times 40 = 2.30\text{ ns}$, a candidate with $t_{\text{hit},B} = 1.12\text{ ns}$ and $\text{MP}_B = 42\text{ ns}$ must achieve $\text{MR}_B \le \frac{2.30 - 1.12}{42} = \frac{1.18}{42} \approx 0.0281 = 2.81\%$. (If $t_{\text{hit},B} > \text{AMAT}_A$, the numerator is negative—meaning even $\text{MR}_B = 0\%$ could never match Cache A!)

### Nested Two-Level AMAT

In a two-level hierarchy (`L1` backed by `L2`, backed by DRAM), a miss in `L1` does not go straight to DRAM—its effective penalty $\text{MP}_{L1}$ equals the average access time of `L2`:

$$\text{MP}_{L1} = \text{AMAT}_{L2} = t_{\text{hit},L2} + \text{MR}_{L2} \cdot \text{MP}_{L2}$$

Substituting $\text{MP}_{L1}$ into the single-level equation produces the **nested two-level AMAT formula**:

$$\text{AMAT} = t_{\text{hit},L1} + \text{MR}_{L1}\big(t_{\text{hit},L2} + \text{MR}_{L2} \cdot \text{MP}_{L2}\big)$$

Be careful to distinguish the two ways `L2` miss rates are reported:

- **Local Miss Rate ($\text{MR}_{L2}$):** $\frac{\text{Misses}_{L2}}{\text{Accesses}_{L2}} = \frac{\text{Misses}_{L2}}{\text{Misses}_{L1}}$—the fraction of `L1` misses that *also* miss in `L2`. Because `L1` already filters out easy hits, local $\text{MR}_{L2}$ often looks numerically high ($10\%\text{--}50\%$). Always use **local $\text{MR}_{L2}$** inside the nested $\text{AMAT}$ formula above.
- **Global Miss Rate ($\text{MR}_{L2,\text{global}}$):** $\frac{\text{Misses}_{L2}}{\text{Total CPU References}} = \text{MR}_{L1} \cdot \text{MR}_{L2}$—the fraction of all CPU accesses that miss in *both* caches and reach DRAM. Expanding the nested formula confirms $\text{AMAT} = t_{\text{hit},L1} + \text{MR}_{L1} t_{\text{hit},L2} + \text{MR}_{L2,\text{global}} \cdot \text{MP}_{L2}$. When a problem reports absolute miss counts over $N$ instructions (e.g., $40$ `L1` misses and $20$ `L2` misses per $1{,}000$ instructions, as in the course slides), compute $\text{MR}_{L1} = 40/1000 = 4\%$ and local $\text{MR}_{L2} = 20/40 = 50\%$ (so $\text{MR}_{L2,\text{global}} = 20/1000 = 2\%$).

### Processor CPI with Memory Stalls

While $\text{AMAT}$ evaluates the memory hierarchy in isolation, whole-program runtime $T_{\text{exec}} = \text{IC} \cdot \text{CPI} \cdot T_{\text{clk}}$ requires adding instruction and data cache miss stalls (measured in clock cycles $P_I \equiv \text{MP}_I$ and $P_D \equiv \text{MP}_D$) onto the ideal-memory execution $\text{CPI}_{\text{exe}}$ of a Harvard split-cache pipeline ([Structural Hazards](/dynamic-courses/computer-architecture/05-pipelined-processor/15-structural-hazards/)):

$$\text{CPI} = \text{CPI}_{\text{exe}} + \text{Stalls}_{I} + \text{Stalls}_{D}$$

1. **Instruction Cache Stalls** ($\text{Stalls}_I$): Every dynamic instruction ($100\%$ frequency) requires $1$ instruction fetch from `L1I`:
   $$\text{Stalls}_I = 1.0 \cdot \text{MR}_I P_I$$
2. **Data Stalls** (Percentage Form): Only Load ($f_{\text{load}}$) and Store ($f_{\text{store}}$) instructions access `L1D`. When unified data miss rate $\text{MR}_D$ applies to both loads and stores:
   $$\text{CPI} = \text{CPI}_{\text{exe}} + \text{MR}_I P_I + (f_{\text{load}} + f_{\text{store}})\,\text{MR}_D P_D$$
   (If separate $\text{MR}_{\text{load}}$ and $\text{MR}_{\text{store}}$ rates are given, expand the data term as $f_{\text{load}}\,\text{MR}_{\text{load}} P_D + f_{\text{store}}\,\text{MR}_{\text{store}} P_D$.)
3. **Data Stalls** (Hybrid Absolute-Miss Form): When a problem specifies total dynamic instructions $\text{IC}$ alongside an **absolute integer miss count** for one data operation (loads *or* stores) and an **instruction percentage** $f_{\text{op}}$ with miss rate $\text{MR}_D$ for the other:
   - Convert the percentage branch to an absolute miss count: $N_{\text{op}} = \text{IC} \cdot f_{\text{op}}$, then $\text{Misses}_{\text{op}} = N_{\text{op}} \cdot \text{MR}_D$.
   - Sum all data cache misses and divide by $\text{IC}$ to obtain data misses per instruction:
     $$\text{Stalls}_D = \frac{\text{Misses}_{\text{load}} + \text{Misses}_{\text{store}}}{\text{IC}} \cdot P_D$$

Once effective $\text{CPI}$ is known, whole-program execution time follows directly from [CPU Performance Equation](/dynamic-courses/computer-architecture/02-computer-performance/06-cpu-performance-equation/):

$$T_{\text{exec}} = \text{IC} \cdot \text{CPI} \cdot T_{\text{clk}} = \frac{\text{IC} \cdot \text{CPI}}{f_{\text{clk}}}$$

Let us verify this hybrid workflow numerically for $\text{IC} = 2{,}000$, $\text{CPI}_{\text{exe}} = 1.5$, $f_{\text{load}} = 20\%$, absolute $\text{Store Misses} = 15$, $\text{MR}_I = 2.5\%$, $P_I = 45\text{ cycles}$, $\text{MR}_D = 4\%$, and $P_D = 45\text{ cycles}$:
- $\text{Stalls}_I = 1.0 \times 0.025 \times 45 = 1.125\text{ cycles/instr}$.
- $N_{\text{load}} = 2{,}000 \times 0.20 = 400\text{ loads} \implies \text{Misses}_{\text{load}} = 400 \times 0.04 = 16\text{ misses}$.
- Total `L1D` misses $= 16 + 15 = 31 \implies \text{Stalls}_D = \frac{31}{2{,}000} \times 45 = 0.6975\text{ cycles/instr}$.
- Effective $\text{CPI} = 1.5 + 1.125 + 0.6975 = 3.3225\text{ cycles/instr}$ (more than double $\text{CPI}_{\text{exe}}$!).

<Divider label="3 · Summary" />

| Metric / Design Problem | Exact Formula | Critical Unit / Convention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single-Level AMAT** | $\text{AMAT} = t_{\text{hit}} + \text{MR} \cdot \text{MP}$ | Minimum $\text{AMAT}$ = Rank 1 (Best) |
| **Max Hit Time** ($\text{AMAT}_B \le \text{AMAT}_A$) | $t_{\text{hit},B,\max} = \text{AMAT}_A - \text{MR}_B \cdot \text{MP}_B$ | Keep $t_{\text{hit}}$ and $\text{MP}$ in same units ($\text{ns}$ or cycles) |
| **Max Miss Rate** ($\text{AMAT}_B \le \text{AMAT}_A$) | $\text{MR}_{B,\max} = \dfrac{\text{AMAT}_A - t_{\text{hit},B}}{\text{MP}_B}$ | Multiply decimal ratio by $100$ for $\%$ |
| **Nested Two-Level AMAT** | $\text{AMAT} = t_{\text{hit},L1} + \text{MR}_{L1}\big(t_{\text{hit},L2} + \text{MR}_{L2}\cdot\text{MP}_{L2}\big)$ | $\text{MR}_{L2}$ is **local** L2 miss rate |
| **Global L2 Miss Rate** | $\text{MR}_{L2,\text{global}} = \text{MR}_{L1} \cdot \text{MR}_{L2}$ | Fraction of all CPU accesses reaching DRAM |
| **Memory-Stall CPI ($\%$ Form)** | $\text{CPI} = \text{CPI}_{\text{exe}} + \text{MR}_I P_I + (f_{\text{load}} + f_{\text{store}})\,\text{MR}_D P_D$ | $P_I, P_D$ in **CPU clock cycles** |
| **D-Cache Stalls (Absolute Misses)** | $\text{Stalls}_D = \dfrac{\text{Misses}_{\text{load}} + \text{Misses}_{\text{store}}}{\text{IC}} \cdot P_D$ | Divide total D-misses by dynamic $\text{IC}$ |

### Performance Calculation Procedure

1. **Convert Percentages Immediately:** Rewrite every miss rate and instruction frequency as a decimal ($5\% \to 0.05$, $3.6\% \to 0.036$, $0.4\% \to 0.004$) before multiplying.
2. **Match Time Units:** If $t_{\text{hit}}$ is in $\text{ns}$ and $\text{MP}$ is given in clock cycles at frequency $f_{\text{clk}}$, convert via $T_{\text{clk}} = 1 / f_{\text{clk}}$ ($\text{MP}_{\text{ns}} = \text{MP}_{\text{cycles}} \cdot T_{\text{clk}}$).
3. **Rank Ascending for AMAT:** Sort candidate caches from smallest $\text{AMAT}$ (fastest, 1st place) to largest $\text{AMAT}$ (slowest, last place), keeping at least $3$ decimal places ($\text{ns}$) to resolve tight ties.
4. **Weight `L1I` vs. `L1D`:** Multiply `L1I` misses by $1.0$ (every instruction is fetched) and `L1D` misses strictly by $f_{\text{load}} + f_{\text{store}}$ (or $\text{Misses}_{D,\text{tot}} / \text{IC}$), then plug effective $\text{CPI}$ into $T_{\text{exec}} = \text{IC} \cdot \text{CPI} \cdot T_{\text{clk}}$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistakes">

1. **Decimal Shift Error** ($5\% \neq 0.5$): Entering $4.5\%$ as $0.45$ inflates the miss penalty term by $10\times$ and flips every inequality.
2. **Inverted Ranking Order:** Never crown the highest $\text{AMAT}$ as "highest performance"—lower latency ($\min \text{AMAT}$) is strictly superior.
3. **Over-Weighting Data Stalls:** Arithmetic and branch instructions never touch `L1D`; omitting $(f_{\text{load}} + f_{\text{store}})$ overcounts $\text{MR}_D P_D$ by $2.5\times\text{--}4\times$.
4. **Local vs. Global Mix-Up:** Never plug $\text{MR}_{L2,\text{global}}$ inside $\text{MR}_{L1}(\dots)$, which would multiply by $\text{MR}_{L1}$ twice!

</Callout>

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. **Stalls and Effective CPI:**
   - **Instruction Cache Stalls** ($100\%$ fetch):
     $$\text{Stalls}_I = 1.0 \times 0.02 \times 45 = \mathbf{0.90\text{ cycles/instr}}$$
   - **Data Cache Stalls** (Load + Store):

       $$\text{Stalls}_D = \frac{38.5}{3{,}000} \times 45 = \mathbf{0.5775\text{ cycles/instr}}$$
   - **Total Processor CPI:**
     $$\text{CPI} = \text{CPI}_{\text{exe}} + \text{Stalls}_I + \text{Stalls}_D = 1.5 + 0.90 + 0.5775 = \mathbf{2.9775\text{ cycles/instr}}$$

2. **Total Execution Time** ($T_{\text{clk}} = 0.5\text{ ns}$):
   $$T_{\text{exec}} = \text{IC} \cdot \text{CPI} \cdot T_{\text{clk}} = 3{,}000 \times 2.9775 \times 0.5\text{ ns} = 4{,}466.25\text{ ns} \approx \mathbf{4.466\text{ }\mu\text{s}}$$
