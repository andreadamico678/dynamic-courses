## Introduction

What happens when a state-space model fails full [controllability](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/01-kalman-controllability-matrix-rank-criterion/) ($\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) < n$) or full [observability](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/02-kalman-observability-matrix-rank-criterion/) ($\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O}) < n$)? Rather than treating the entire model as broken, Rudolf Kalman proved that you can apply an invertible similarity transformation $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{T}\mathbf{z}$ ([Chapter 2](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/01-state-models/02-coordinate-basis-similarity-transformations/)) to partition the state space $\mathbb{R}^n$ cleanly into four structural compartments: states you can both steer and see ($\mathcal{X}_{co}$), states you can steer but cannot see ($\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$), states you can see but cannot steer ($\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}o}$), and states completely isolated from both actuators and sensors ($\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}$).

In real hardware—like a flexible robotic arm viewed only by an external overhead camera—high-frequency joint oscillations may sit inside the controllable-unobservable block $\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$, completely absent from the transfer function $W(s)$ yet still shaking the gearbox apart. Kalman's canonical decomposition explains mathematically why transfer functions ([Chapter 7](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/02-laplace-resolvent/04-transfer-function-matrix-invariants/)) see *only* the controllable-observable core $\mathcal{X}_{co}$ and why pole-zero cancellations correspond to hidden internal subspaces.

### Isolating the Transfer-Function Core

Consider a decoupled third-order continuous-time system ($n = 3$, $m = 1$, $p = 1$) whose three coordinate axes already align with distinct Kalman compartments:

$$
\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -5 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t) + \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} u(t), \qquad y(t) = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t)
$$

Writing out the three scalar state differential equations alongside the sensor output equation exposes each state's exact structural connections to the actuator $u(t)$ and sensor $y(t)$:

$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{x}_1(t) = -x_1(t) + u(t) \\
\dot{x}_2(t) = -2x_2(t) + u(t) \\
\dot{x}_3(t) = -5x_3(t)
\end{cases}, \qquad y(t) = 3x_1(t) + 0x_2(t) + 2x_3(t)
$$

Inspecting the actuator entries $b_i$ and sensor weights $c_i$ classifies each state mode immediately:
- **State $x_1$ ($\lambda_1 = -1$):** Driven directly by the actuator ($b_1 = 1 \neq 0$) and measured directly by the sensor ($c_1 = 3 \neq 0$). Thus $x_1$ belongs to $\mathcal{X}_{co}$ (**controllable and observable**).
- **State $x_2$ ($\lambda_2 = -2$):** Driven directly by the actuator ($b_2 = 1 \neq 0$), so $u(t)$ actively excites $x_2(t)$, but its sensor coefficient is zero ($c_2 = 0$). Thus $x_2$ belongs to $\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$ (**controllable but unobservable**).
- **State $x_3$ ($\lambda_3 = -5$):** Measured by the sensor ($c_3 = 2 \neq 0$), so any initial condition $x_3(0) \neq 0$ appears in $y(t)$, but its actuator coefficient is zero ($b_3 = 0$). Thus $x_3$ belongs to $\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}o}$ (**uncontrollable but observable**).

Let us verify that the formal Kalman matrices $\mathcal{R}$ and $\mathcal{O}$ reproduce this exact classification. First, compute the controllability matrix $\mathcal{R} = [\mathbf{B} \;\; \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B} \;\; \mathbf{A}^2\mathbf{B}]$:

$$
\mathcal{R} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies \mathcal{X}_c = \operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{R}) = \operatorname{span}\left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \right\} = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2\}
$$

Since row 3 of $\mathcal{R}$ is zero while the top $2 \times 2$ block has determinant $1(-2) - (-1)(1) = -1 \neq 0$, the controllable subspace has dimension $r_c = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) = 2$ and spans $x_1$ and $x_2$. Next, compute the observability matrix $\mathcal{O} = [\mathbf{C}; \;\; \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}; \;\; \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^2]$:

$$
\mathcal{O} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ -3 & 0 & -10 \\ 3 & 0 & 50 \end{bmatrix} \implies \mathcal{X}_{no} = \ker(\mathcal{O}) = \operatorname{span}\left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \right\} = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_2\}
$$

Because column 2 of $\mathcal{O}$ is zero while columns 1 and 3 are independent, $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O}) = 2$ and the unobservable subspace $\mathcal{X}_{no} = \ker(\mathcal{O})$ is the 1D axis $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_2\}$. Intersecting $\mathcal{X}_c = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2\}$ with $\mathcal{X}_{no} = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_2\}$ confirms $\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}} = \mathcal{X}_c \cap \mathcal{X}_{no} = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_2\}$, leaving $\mathcal{X}_{co} = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_1\}$ and $\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}o} = \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{e}_3\}$.

Now compute the input-output transfer function $W(s) = \mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B}$ from [Chapter 7](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/02-laplace-resolvent/04-transfer-function-matrix-invariants/) by inverting the diagonal resolvent matrix $s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} = \operatorname{diag}(s+1, s+2, s+5)$:

$$
W(s) = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{s+1} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{s+2} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1}{s+5} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = 3\left(\frac{1}{s+1}\right)(1) + 0\left(\frac{1}{s+2}\right)(1) + 2\left(\frac{1}{s+5}\right)(0) = \frac{3}{s + 1}
$$

When written over the common characteristic denominator $\det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}) = (s+1)(s+2)(s+5)$, this transfer function has experienced two exact **pole-zero cancellations**:

$$
W(s) = \frac{3(s+2)(s+5)}{(s+1)(s+2)(s+5)} = \frac{3}{s + 1}
$$

Both the controllable-unobservable eigenvalue $\lambda_2 = -2$ and the uncontrollable-observable eigenvalue $\lambda_3 = -5$ vanish completely from $W(s)$, leaving a 1st-order external transfer function governed solely by the controllable and observable state $x_1 \in \mathcal{X}_{co}$.

### Four-Subspace State Partition

Consider a general continuous-time LTI state-space realization $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}, \mathbf{D})$ of state dimension $n$:

$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}(t) \\
\mathbf{y}(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}(t)
\end{cases} \qquad \mathbf{x}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n, \quad \mathbf{u}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^m, \quad \mathbf{y}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^p
$$

From [Chapter 20](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/01-kalman-controllability-matrix-rank-criterion/) and [Chapter 21](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/02-kalman-observability-matrix-rank-criterion/), every realization possesses two canonical $\mathbf{A}$-invariant subspaces inside $\mathbb{R}^n$:
- The **controllable subspace** $\mathcal{X}_c = \operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{R})$ of dimension $r_c = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R})$, satisfying $\mathbf{A}\mathcal{X}_c \subseteq \mathcal{X}_c$ and $\operatorname{Im}(\mathbf{B}) \subseteq \mathcal{X}_c$.
- The **unobservable subspace** $\mathcal{X}_{no} = \ker(\mathcal{O})$ of dimension $n - r_o = n - \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O})$, satisfying $\mathbf{A}\mathcal{X}_{no} \subseteq \mathcal{X}_{no}$ and $\mathcal{X}_{no} \subseteq \ker(\mathbf{C})$.

Because the intersection and sum of two $\mathbf{A}$-invariant subspaces are also $\mathbf{A}$-invariant, we can partition the state space $\mathbb{R}^n$ into the direct sum of four mutually independent subspaces:

$$
\mathbb{R}^n = \mathcal{X}_{co} \oplus \mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}} \oplus \mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}o} \oplus \mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}
$$

We construct the bases for these four subspaces in four sequential steps:
1. **Controllable and Unobservable Subspace ($\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$):** Define the intersection of the controllable and unobservable subspaces:
   $$
   \mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}} = \mathcal{X}_c \cap \mathcal{X}_{no} = \operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{R}) \cap \ker(\mathcal{O}), \qquad n_{c\bar{o}} = \dim(\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}})
   $$
   Because both $\mathcal{X}_c$ and $\mathcal{X}_{no}$ are $\mathbf{A}$-invariant, their intersection $\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$ is strictly $\mathbf{A}$-invariant ($\mathbf{A}\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}} \subseteq \mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$). States in $\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$ can be excited by $\mathbf{u}(t)$ from the origin, yet produce zero output at the sensors.
2. **Controllable and Observable Subspace ($\mathcal{X}_{co}$):** Choose any subspace $\mathcal{X}_{co} \subseteq \mathcal{X}_c$ complementary to $\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$ inside $\mathcal{X}_c$ so that:
   $$
   \mathcal{X}_c = \mathcal{X}_{co} \oplus \mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}, \qquad n_{co} = \dim(\mathcal{X}_{co}) = r_c - n_{c\bar{o}}
   $$
   Every vector in $\mathcal{X}_{co}$ is reachable from the origin ($\mathcal{X}_{co} \subseteq \mathcal{X}_c$) and no non-zero vector in $\mathcal{X}_{co}$ lies in $\ker(\mathcal{O})$ ($\mathcal{X}_{co} \cap \mathcal{X}_{no} = \{\mathbf{0}\}$).
3. **Uncontrollable and Unobservable Subspace ($\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}$):** Choose any subspace $\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}} \subseteq \mathcal{X}_{no}$ complementary to $\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$ inside $\mathcal{X}_{no}$ so that:
   $$
   \mathcal{X}_{no} = \mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}} \oplus \mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}, \qquad n_{\bar{c}\bar{o}} = \dim(\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}) = (n - r_o) - n_{c\bar{o}}
   $$
4. **Uncontrollable and Observable Subspace ($\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}o}$):** Choose any complementary subspace $\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}o}$ that completes the sum $\mathcal{X}_c + \mathcal{X}_{no} = \mathcal{X}_{co} \oplus \mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}} \oplus \mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}$ to the entire state space $\mathbb{R}^n$:
   $$
   \mathbb{R}^n = (\mathcal{X}_c + \mathcal{X}_{no}) \oplus \mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}o}, \qquad n_{\bar{c}o} = n - n_{co} - n_{c\bar{o}} - n_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}
   $$

The four subspace dimensions always sum to the total state order: $n_{co} + n_{c\bar{o}} + n_{\bar{c}o} + n_{\bar{c}\bar{o}} = n$.

### Canonical Block Matrix Form

Now assemble an invertible $n \times n$ coordinate transformation matrix $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ by placing the basis columns of the four subspaces side by side in the order $(co, \; c\bar{o}, \; \bar{c}o, \; \bar{c}\bar{o})$:

$$
\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{T}_{co} & \mathbf{T}_{c\bar{o}} & \mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}o} & \mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{x}(t) = \mathbf{T}\mathbf{z}(t) = \mathbf{T} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{z}_{co}(t) \\ \mathbf{z}_{c\bar{o}}(t) \\ \mathbf{z}_{\bar{c}o}(t) \\ \mathbf{z}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}(t) \end{bmatrix}
$$

Applying the similarity transformation $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}$, $\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B}$, $\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}$ from [Chapter 2](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/01-state-models/02-coordinate-basis-similarity-transformations/) forces specific block entries of $(\hat{\mathbf{A}}, \hat{\mathbf{B}}, \hat{\mathbf{C}})$ to vanish identically because of subspace invariance:
- **Invariance of $\mathcal{X}_c = \mathcal{X}_{co} \oplus \mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$:** Because $\mathbf{A}\mathcal{X}_c \subseteq \mathcal{X}_c$, multiplying $\mathbf{A}$ by any column in $\mathbf{T}_{co}$ or $\mathbf{T}_{c\bar{o}}$ produces a vector purely inside $\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{T}_{co}, \mathbf{T}_{c\bar{o}})$, with **zero** component along $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}o}$ or $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}$. This forces the bottom-left $2 \times 2$ blocks (rows 3–4, columns 1–2) of $\hat{\mathbf{A}}$ to be $\mathbf{0}$.
- **Invariance of $\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}} = \mathcal{X}_c \cap \mathcal{X}_{no}$:** Because $\mathbf{A}\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}} \subseteq \mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$, multiplying $\mathbf{A}$ by $\mathbf{T}_{c\bar{o}}$ produces a vector purely inside $\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{T}_{c\bar{o}})$, forcing block $(1, 2)$ of $\hat{\mathbf{A}}$ to be $\mathbf{0}$.
- **Invariance of $\mathcal{X}_{no} = \mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}} \oplus \mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}$:** Because $\mathbf{A}\mathcal{X}_{no} \subseteq \mathcal{X}_{no}$, multiplying $\mathbf{A}$ by $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}$ produces a vector purely inside $\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{T}_{c\bar{o}}, \mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}})$, with zero component along $\mathbf{T}_{co}$ or $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}o}$. This forces blocks $(1, 4)$ and $(3, 4)$ of $\hat{\mathbf{A}}$ to be $\mathbf{0}$.
- **Input and Output Containment:** Because $\operatorname{Im}(\mathbf{B}) \subseteq \mathcal{X}_c = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{T}_{co}, \mathbf{T}_{c\bar{o}})$, the bottom two blocks of $\hat{\mathbf{B}}$ vanish ($\mathbf{B}_{\bar{c}o} = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{B}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}} = \mathbf{0}$). Because $\mathcal{X}_{no} = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{T}_{c\bar{o}}, \mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}) \subseteq \ker(\mathbf{C})$, columns 2 and 4 of $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ vanish ($\mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}_{c\bar{o}} = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}} = \mathbf{0}$).

Putting these structural zeros together yields the **Kalman Canonical Decomposition Theorem**:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{A}_{co} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A}_{13} & \mathbf{0} \\
\mathbf{A}_{21} & \mathbf{A}_{c\bar{o}} & \mathbf{A}_{23} & \mathbf{A}_{24} \\
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}o} & \mathbf{0} \\
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A}_{43} & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}
\end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_{co} \\ \mathbf{B}_{c\bar{o}} \\ \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_{co} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{C}_{\bar{c}o} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}
$$

By construction, the pair $(\mathbf{A}_{co}, \mathbf{B}_{co})$ is completely controllable, the pair $(\mathbf{A}_{co}, \mathbf{C}_{co})$ is completely observable, and the total characteristic polynomial factors across the four diagonal blocks:

$$
\det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}) = \det(s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}}) = \det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co}) \det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{c\bar{o}}) \det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}o}) \det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}})
$$

Let us now prove rigorously why **only** the controllable-observable block $(\mathbf{A}_{co}, \mathbf{B}_{co}, \mathbf{C}_{co})$ survives in the input-output transfer function matrix $W(s) = \mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{D}$. Recall from [Chapter 7](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/02-laplace-resolvent/04-transfer-function-matrix-invariants/) that similarity transformations leave the transfer function strictly invariant: $W(s) = \hat{\mathbf{C}}(s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{B}} + \mathbf{D}$. Partition $s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}}$ into $2 \times 2$ super-blocks separating the controllable states $(\mathbf{z}_{co}, \mathbf{z}_{c\bar{o}})$ from the uncontrollable states $(\mathbf{z}_{\bar{c}o}, \mathbf{z}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}})$:

$$
s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}} = \begin{bmatrix}
s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co} & \mathbf{0} & -\mathbf{A}_{13} & \mathbf{0} \\
-\mathbf{A}_{21} & s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{c\bar{o}} & -\mathbf{A}_{23} & -\mathbf{A}_{24} \\
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}o} & \mathbf{0} \\
\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & -\mathbf{A}_{43} & s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Using the block upper-triangular inverse identity $\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{M}_{11} & \mathbf{M}_{12} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{M}_{22} \end{bmatrix}^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_1 \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{M}_{11}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_1 \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}$, multiplying $(s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}$ on the right by $\hat{\mathbf{B}} = [\mathbf{B}_{co}^T, \mathbf{B}_{c\bar{o}}^T, \mathbf{0}^T, \mathbf{0}^T]^T$ eliminates rows 3 and 4 immediately:

$$
(s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \begin{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co} & \mathbf{0} \\ -\mathbf{A}_{21} & s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{c\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix}^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_{co} \\ \mathbf{B}_{c\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix} \\ \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{co} \\
\mathbf{\star} \\
\mathbf{0} \\
\mathbf{0}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Here $\mathbf{\star} = (s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{c\bar{o}})^{-1}[\mathbf{A}_{21}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{co} + \mathbf{B}_{c\bar{o}}]$ is the Laplace response of the controllable-unobservable state $\mathbf{Z}_{c\bar{o}}(s)$. Now multiply on the left by $\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_{co} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{C}_{\bar{c}o} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}$:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{C}}(s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_{co} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{C}_{\bar{c}o} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} (s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{co} \\ \mathbf{\star} \\ \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{C}_{co}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{co}
$$

The zero in the second block of $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ annihilates $\mathbf{\star}$, while the zeros in the bottom two blocks of $(s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}\hat{\mathbf{B}}$ annihilate $\mathbf{C}_{\bar{c}o}$! We obtain Kalman's fundamental transfer function reduction:

$$
\boxed{W(s) = \mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{D} = \mathbf{C}_{co}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{co} + \mathbf{D}}
$$

The transfer function $W(s)$ depends **exclusively** on the controllable and observable subsystem $(\mathbf{A}_{co}, \mathbf{B}_{co}, \mathbf{C}_{co}, \mathbf{D})$. All eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}_{c\bar{o}}$, $\mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}o}$, and $\mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}$ cancel out algebraically between $\operatorname{adj}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})$ and $\det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})$. Consequently, a state-space realization $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}, \mathbf{D})$ is **minimal** (achieving the smallest possible state dimension $n = n_{co}$, called the **McMillan degree** of $W(s)$) if and only if it is both **completely controllable** and **completely observable**.

### Controllability Decomposition Algorithm

On written exams, you are frequently asked to perform the two-block **controllability decomposition** when $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) = r_c < n$, separating the $r_c$ controllable coordinates $\mathbf{z}_c$ from the $n - r_c$ uncontrollable coordinates $\mathbf{z}_{\bar{c}}$. Follow this exact four-step recipe:

1. **Compute $\mathcal{R}$ and its rank:** Form $\mathcal{R} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B} & \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B} & \dots & \mathbf{A}^{n-1}\mathbf{B} \end{bmatrix}$ and determine $r_c = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) < n$.
2. **Place $\mathbf{T}_c$ on the LEFT:** Select $r_c$ linearly independent columns from $\mathcal{R}$ (starting with the independent columns of $\mathbf{B}$) to form the left block $\mathbf{T}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r_c}$ spanning $\mathcal{X}_c = \operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{R})$.
3. **Complete the basis on the RIGHT:** Append $n - r_c$ linearly independent columns $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times (n - r_c)}$ (usually simple standard unit vectors $\mathbf{e}_k$) so that the full $n \times n$ matrix $\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{T}_c & \mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix}$ has $\det(\mathbf{T}) \neq 0$.
4. **Transform $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C})$:** Compute $\mathbf{T}^{-1}$ and evaluate $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}$, $\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B}$, and $\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}$. Because $\mathbf{A}\operatorname{Im}(\mathbf{T}_c) \subseteq \operatorname{Im}(\mathbf{T}_c)$ and $\operatorname{Im}(\mathbf{B}) \subseteq \operatorname{Im}(\mathbf{T}_c)$, the transformed matrices are guaranteed to have the block upper-triangular form:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_c & \mathbf{A}_{12} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_c \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_c & \mathbf{C}_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix}
$$

Here $(\mathbf{A}_c, \mathbf{B}_c)$ of order $r_c$ is completely controllable, while the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}}$ of order $n - r_c$ are the exact **uncontrollable modes** of the plant.

### Observability Decomposition Algorithm

Dually, when observability fails ($\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O}) = r_o < n$), we separate the $r_o$ observable coordinates $\mathbf{z}_o$ from the $n - r_o$ unobservable coordinates $\mathbf{z}_{\bar{o}}$ using the **observability decomposition**:

1. **Compute $\mathcal{O}$ and its kernel:** Form $\mathcal{O} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}; & \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}; & \dots; & \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{n-1} \end{bmatrix}$, find $r_o = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O}) < n$, and solve the homogeneous equation $\mathcal{O}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ to find $n - r_o$ linearly independent basis vectors spanning $\mathcal{X}_{no} = \ker(\mathcal{O})$.
2. **Place $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}}$ on the RIGHT:** Group those $n - r_o$ kernel basis vectors into the **right-hand** matrix block $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times (n - r_o)}$.
3. **Complete the basis on the LEFT:** Prepend $r_o$ linearly independent columns $\mathbf{T}_o \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r_o}$ on the **left** so that $\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{T}_o & \mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix}$ is invertible ($\det(\mathbf{T}) \neq 0$).
4. **Transform $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C})$:** Because $\mathcal{X}_{no} = \operatorname{Im}(\mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}})$ is $\mathbf{A}$-invariant ($\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}} = \mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}}\mathbf{A}_{\bar{o}}$) and $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}} = \mathbf{0}$, evaluating $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}$, $\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B}$, and $\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}$ yields the dual block lower-triangular form:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_o & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{A}_{21} & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_o \\ \mathbf{B}_{\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_o & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}
$$

Here $(\mathbf{A}_o, \mathbf{C}_o)$ of order $r_o$ is completely observable, while the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}_{\bar{o}}$ of order $n - r_o$ are the exact **unobservable modes** of the plant.

<Callout type="bridge" title="Robotics Bridge: Flexible Joint Dynamics vs. End-Effector Vision">

In collaborative robotic manipulators (such as Universal Robots arms), joint gearboxes exhibit torsional elasticity between motor angles $\boldsymbol{\theta}_m$ and link angles $\boldsymbol{q}$. If an external camera (e.g., OptiTrack motion capture) measures only end-effector pose $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{f}(\boldsymbol{q})$, high-frequency torsional oscillations between motor and link lie in $\mathcal{X}_{c\bar{o}}$ or $\mathcal{X}_{\bar{c}\bar{o}}$. The camera-to-tool transfer function $W(s) = \mathbf{C}_{co}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{co}$ appears smooth, yet fast motions excite hidden gear vibrations unless internal motor encoders restore full observability.

### Two-Block Decomposition Procedure

1. **For controllability ($r_c < n$):** Take $r_c$ independent columns of $\mathcal{R}$ as $\mathbf{T}_c$ on the **left**, complete $\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{T}_c \; \mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}}]$ with $\det(\mathbf{T}) \neq 0$, and compute $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}$, $\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B}$. Verify $\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{21} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{B}}_2 = \mathbf{0}$.
2. **For observability ($r_o < n$):** Solve $\mathcal{O}\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ for $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}}$, place $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}}$ on the **right** in $\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{T}_o \; \mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}}]$ with $\det(\mathbf{T}) \neq 0$, and compute $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}$, $\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}$. Verify $\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{12} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{C}}_2 = \mathbf{0}$.

### Key Proofs

- **Transfer Function Invariance:** Block triangularity of $\hat{\mathbf{A}}$ alongside zero rows in $\hat{\mathbf{B}}$ and zero columns in $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ guarantees $(s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}})^{-1}$ transmits input to output strictly through $(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{co})^{-1}$.
- **Minimality Criterion:** Eliminating any state outside $\mathcal{X}_{co}$ leaves $W(s)$ unchanged, so $n$ cannot be reduced further if and only if $\mathcal{X}_{co} = \mathbb{R}^n$ ($\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O}) = n$).

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistakes">
1. **Reversing Basis Column Order:** Put $\mathbf{T}_c = \operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{R})$ on the **left** ($\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{T}_c \; \mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}}]$) for controllability, but put $\mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}} = \ker(\mathcal{O})$ on the **right** ($\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{T}_o \; \mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}}]$) for observability.
2. **Linearly Dependent Completion Vectors:** Always verify $\det(\mathbf{T}) \neq 0$ before inverting $\mathbf{T}$.
3. **Confusing External and Internal Stability:** Unobservable or uncontrollable unstable modes cancel out of $W(s)$ (giving a benign BIBO transfer function) while still blowing up internal states ([Chapter 16](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/04-stability-routh/06-bounded-input-bounded-output-stability/)).
</Callout>

<Exercise client:load id="ex-22-1" title="Exercise 22.1 — Controllability Kalman Decomposition" level="Warm-Up" hint="Pick $\mathbf{t}_1$ from $\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{M}_c)$ and complete $\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{t}_1 \; \mathbf{t}_2]$ with any vector giving $\det(\mathbf{T}) \neq 0$.">

Given the continuous-time state-space system:

$$
\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

1. Compute the Kalman controllability matrix $\mathcal{M}_c$ and determine its rank $r_c$.
2. Choose a basis vector $\mathbf{t}_1 \in \operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{M}_c)$ and complete the basis with an independent vector $\mathbf{t}_2$ to construct $\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{t}_1 \; \mathbf{t}_2]$. Compute $\mathbf{T}^{-1}$.
3. Calculate the transformed matrices $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B}$.
4. Verify the block-triangular structure $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \begin{bmatrix} A_c & A_{12} \\ 0 & A_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix}$ and identify the uncontrollable eigenvalue mode.

<StepCheck client:load
  prompt="What is the uncontrollable eigenvalue $\lambda_{\bar{c}}$ of the system above?"
  expected={3}
  tolerance={0.01}
  unit=""
  explanation="Since $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$, the uncontrollable block is $A_{\bar{c}} = [3]$, giving $\lambda_{\bar{c}} = 3$."
/>

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. **Controllability matrix and rank:**
   $$
   \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   $$
   \mathcal{M}_c = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B} & \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   The columns are identical, so $\det(\mathcal{M}_c) = 0$.
   Since column 1 is non-zero, $r_c = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{M}_c) = 1 < 2 = n$.

2. **Basis transformation construction:**
   The controllable subspace is $\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{M}_c) = \operatorname{span}\left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \right\}$.
   Select $\mathbf{t}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
   Complete the basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$ with a linearly independent vector, for instance $\mathbf{t}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
   The transformation matrix is:
   $$
   \mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{t}_1 & \mathbf{t}_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \det(\mathbf{T}) = 1 \neq 0
   $$
   The inverse matrix is:
   $$
   \mathbf{T}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

3. **Transformed state-space matrices:**
   $$
   \hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   $$
   \hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

4. **Canonical structure and uncontrollable eigenvalue:**
   The transformed matrix has block upper triangular structure:
   $$
   \hat{\mathbf{A}} = \begin{bmatrix} A_c & A_{12} \\ 0 & A_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   with $\hat{A}_{21} = 0$. The controllable block is $A_c = [1]$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_c = 1$.
   The uncontrollable block is $A_{\bar{c}} = [3]$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_{\bar{c}} = 3$. Because $\hat{B}_2 = 0$, input $u(t)$ cannot influence the evolution of state $z_2(t)$.

<Exercise client:load id="ex-22-2" title="Exercise 22.2 — Observability Decomposition and Minimality" level="Exam Standard" hint="Find $\ker(\mathcal{M}_o)$ first to get $\mathbf{t}_2$, then pick $\mathbf{t}_1$ independent of $\mathbf{t}_2$ and compute $W(s)$ from $(A_o, B_o, C_o)$.">

Given the state-space model:

$$
\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$

1. Compute the Kalman observability matrix $\mathcal{M}_o$ and verify that $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{M}_o) = 1$.
2. Determine the unobservable subspace $\mathcal{X}_{\bar{o}} = \ker(\mathcal{M}_o)$ and select basis vector $\mathbf{t}_2 \in \ker(\mathcal{M}_o)$. Choose $\mathbf{t}_1$ to complete the basis, forming $\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{t}_1 \; \mathbf{t}_2]$.
3. Compute $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}$, $\hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{B}$, and $\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{T}$.
4. Compute the transfer function $W(s) = \mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B}$ directly and from the observable subsystem $(\hat{A}_o, \hat{B}_o, \hat{C}_o)$. Explain why the eigenvalue mode $\lambda = -1$ is absent from $W(s)$.

<StepCheck client:load
  mode="choice"
  prompt="After extracting the observable subsystem $(A_o, B_o, C_o)$, what is the transfer function $W(s)$?"
  options={[
    "$W(s) = \\frac{1}{s + 1}$",
    "$W(s) = \\frac{1}{s - 2}$",
    "$W(s) = \\frac{s + 1}{(s - 2)(s + 1)^2}$"
  ]}
  correctIndex={1}
  explanation="The unobservable mode $\\lambda = -1$ cancels out via $\\frac{s+1}{(s-2)(s+1)}$, leaving $W(s) = \\frac{1}{s-2}$."

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. **Observability matrix and rank:**
   $$
   \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   $$
   \mathcal{M}_o = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C} \\ \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   The second row is $2 \times (\text{Row } 1)$, so $\det(\mathcal{M}_o) = 0$.
   Since Row 1 is non-zero, $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{M}_o) = 1 < 2 = n$. The system is not completely observable.

2. **Unobservable subspace and basis transformation:**
   The unobservable subspace is the null space $\mathcal{X}_{\bar{o}} = \ker(\mathcal{M}_o)$:
   $$
   \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies v_1 = 0, \quad v_2 \in \mathbb{R}
   $$
   Thus $\mathcal{X}_{\bar{o}} = \operatorname{span}\left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}$.
   Select $\mathbf{t}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathcal{X}_{\bar{o}}$.
   To complete the basis, choose an independent vector $\mathbf{t}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
   Then:
   $$
   \mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{t}_1 & \mathbf{t}_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{I}, \quad \mathbf{T}^{-1} = \mathbf{I}
   $$

3. **Transformed matrices:**
   Since $\mathbf{T} = \mathbf{I}$, the original realization is already in observability Kalman canonical form:
   $$
   \hat{\mathbf{A}} = \mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A_o & 0 \\ A_{21} & A_{\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   $$
   \hat{\mathbf{B}} = \mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} B_o \\ B_{\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{C}} = \mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} C_o & 0 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Notice that the upper-right block is identically zero ($\hat{A}_{12} = 0$), and the second column of $\hat{\mathbf{C}}$ is zero ($\hat{C}_{\bar{o}} = 0$).

4. **Transfer function and pole-zero cancellation:**
   Computing $W(s)$ directly using full state-space formulas:
   $$
   (s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} s - 2 & 0 \\ -1 & s + 1 \end{bmatrix}^{-1} = \frac{1}{(s - 2)(s + 1)} \begin{bmatrix} s + 1 & 0 \\ 1 & s - 2 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   $$
   W(s) = \mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \frac{1}{(s - 2)(s + 1)} \begin{bmatrix} s + 1 & 0 \\ 1 & s - 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   $$
   W(s) = \frac{1}{(s - 2)(s + 1)} \begin{bmatrix} s + 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{s + 1}{(s - 2)(s + 1)} = \frac{1}{s - 2}
   $$
   Using the minimal observable subsystem $(A_o, B_o, C_o) = (2, 1, 1)$:
   $$
   W(s) = C_o (s - A_o)^{-1} B_o = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{s - 2} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{s - 2}
   $$
   The mode $\lambda = -1$ corresponds to the unobservable subspace ($A_{\bar{o}} = [-1]$). Because $C_{\bar{o}} = 0$, this dynamic mode produces an algebraic pole-zero cancellation at $s = -1$ and does not appear in the input-output transfer function $W(s)$.

</details>
