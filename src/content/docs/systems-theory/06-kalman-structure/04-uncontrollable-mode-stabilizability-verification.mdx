## Introduction

Full [controllability](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/01-kalman-controllability-matrix-rank-criterion/) ($\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) = n$) and full [observability](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/02-kalman-observability-matrix-rank-criterion/) ($\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O}) = n$) are idealized benchmarks—in complex aircraft, satellites, or underactuated robots, some internal dynamics (such as passive aerodynamic damping or sealed battery thermal modes) cannot be driven by actuators or measured directly by sensors. Does losing full rank mean closed-loop feedback control or state estimation is impossible?

Not if those unsteerable or invisible modes decay naturally on their own! **Stabilizability** and its dual **detectability** relax Kalman's strict rank tests to the exact practical engineering threshold required in [Part VII](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/07-state-feedback/00-introduction/): you only need actuator or sensor authority over **unstable or marginally stable** modes ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0$), because already-damped modes ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$) settle safely to zero by themselves.

### Stabilizing an Uncontrollable Plant

Consider a second-order continuous-time plant ($n = 2$, $m = 1$) that possesses one unstable exponential mode ($\lambda_1 = +1$) and one passive damping mode ($\lambda_2 = -4$):

$$
\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -4 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t) + \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} u(t)
$$

Writing out the two scalar differential equations shows how the actuator $u(t)$ affects each state variable:

$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{x}_1(t) = x_1(t) + u(t) \\
\dot{x}_2(t) = -4x_2(t)
\end{cases}
$$

First, check Kalman's controllability matrix $\mathcal{R} = [\mathbf{B} \;\; \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}]$:

$$
\mathcal{R} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies \det(\mathcal{R}) = 0, \quad \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) = 1 < 2
$$

Because $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) = 1 < 2$, the system is **not** completely controllable: the second state $x_2(t)$ receives zero actuator input ($b_2 = 0$) and zero coupling from $x_1(t)$ ($a_{21} = 0$).

Now suppose we close a full-state linear feedback loop $u(t) = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x}(t) = -k_1 x_1(t) - k_2 x_2(t)$ with feedback gain row vector $\mathbf{K} = \begin{bmatrix} k_1 & k_2 \end{bmatrix}$. Substituting $u(t) = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x}(t)$ into $\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}u(t)$ produces the closed-loop dynamics $\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\mathbf{x}(t)$ with closed-loop state matrix:

$$
\mathbf{A}_{\text{cl}} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -4 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} k_1 & k_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -4 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} k_1 & k_2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - k_1 & -k_2 \\ 0 & -4 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Because the bottom row of $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$ is identically zero, $\mathbf{A}_{\text{cl}}$ remains upper triangular for every possible choice of gains $(k_1, k_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2$. Its closed-loop characteristic polynomial factors directly along the main diagonal:

$$
p_{\text{cl}}(s) = \det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{cl}}) = \det\begin{bmatrix} s - (1 - k_1) & k_2 \\ 0 & s + 4 \end{bmatrix} = \big(s - (1 - k_1)\big)(s + 4) = 0
$$

The two closed-loop eigenvalues are therefore $\lambda_{1,\text{cl}} = 1 - k_1$ and $\lambda_{2,\text{cl}} = -4$. Notice two things:
1. **Unmovable uncontrollable pole:** No choice of $(k_1, k_2)$ can ever shift $\lambda_2 = -4$. Even if we pick a non-zero cross-feedback gain $k_2 \neq 0$, the term $-k_2$ sits in the upper-right corner of $\mathbf{A}_{\text{cl}}$ and has zero effect on the eigenvalues!
2. **Complete stabilization:** Even though we cannot move $\lambda_2 = -4$, we *do not need to move it*, because $x_2(t) = x_2(0)e^{-4t} \to 0$ is already naturally asymptotically stable ($\operatorname{Re}(-4) = -4 < 0$)! Meanwhile, the unstable pole $\lambda_1 = +1$ is controllable and can be shifted anywhere in the open left half-plane—for example, to $\lambda_{1,\text{cl}} = -3$ by setting $1 - k_1 = -3 \implies k_1 = 4$ (with $k_2 = 0$).

Because the closed-loop eigenvalues $\{-3, -4\}$ both lie strictly in the open left half-plane, the pair $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ is **stabilizable**. By contrast, if the second diagonal entry of $\mathbf{A}$ had been $+2$ (unstable) or $0$ (marginally stable integrator), that unmovable eigenvalue would remain trapped in the closed right half-plane $\operatorname{Re}(s) \ge 0$ under every feedback gain $\mathbf{K}$, rendering the plant **unstabilizable**.

### Stabilizability Invariant

A continuous-time LTI pair $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ is defined to be **stabilizable** if there exists a constant state-feedback gain matrix $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ under the control law $\mathbf{u}(t) = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x}(t)$ such that the closed-loop state matrix $\mathbf{A}_{\text{cl}} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$ is asymptotically stable (Hurwitz, meaning every closed-loop eigenvalue satisfies $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$, as established in [Chapter 12](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/04-stability-routh/02-lyapunov-spectral-internal-stability/)).

To derive the exact necessary and sufficient structural condition for stabilizability, apply the two-block controllability Kalman decomposition $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{T}\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{T}[\mathbf{z}_c^T, \mathbf{z}_{\bar{c}}^T]^T$ from [Chapter 22](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/03-kalman-canonical-subspace-decomposition/), which separates the $r_c$ controllable coordinates $\mathbf{z}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{r_c}$ from the $n - r_c$ uncontrollable coordinates $\mathbf{z}_{\bar{c}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n - r_c}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{\mathbf{z}}_c(t) \\ \dot{\mathbf{z}}_{\bar{c}}(t) \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_c & \mathbf{A}_{12} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix}}_{\hat{\mathbf{A}}} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{z}_c(t) \\ \mathbf{z}_{\bar{c}}(t) \end{bmatrix} + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_c \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}}_{\hat{\mathbf{B}}} \mathbf{u}(t)
$$

Now partition the feedback gain matrix in the transformed coordinates as $\hat{\mathbf{K}} = \mathbf{K}\mathbf{T} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{K}_c & \mathbf{K}_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix}$, where $\mathbf{K}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r_c}$ feeds back the controllable states $\mathbf{z}_c$ and $\mathbf{K}_{\bar{c}} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times (n - r_c)}$ feeds back the uncontrollable states $\mathbf{z}_{\bar{c}}$. Substituting $\mathbf{u}(t) = -\hat{\mathbf{K}}\mathbf{z}(t)$ into the decomposed state equation yields the transformed closed-loop matrix $\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{cl}} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\mathbf{T} = \hat{\mathbf{A}} - \hat{\mathbf{B}}\hat{\mathbf{K}}$:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{cl}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_c & \mathbf{A}_{12} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_c \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{K}_c & \mathbf{K}_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_c & \mathbf{A}_{12} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B}_c\mathbf{K}_c & \mathbf{B}_c\mathbf{K}_{\bar{c}} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_c - \mathbf{B}_c \mathbf{K}_c & \mathbf{A}_{12} - \mathbf{B}_c \mathbf{K}_{\bar{c}} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}} \end{bmatrix}
$$

Because the lower block of $\hat{\mathbf{B}}$ is zero ($\mathbf{B}_{\bar{c}} = \mathbf{0}$), the bottom row of $\hat{\mathbf{B}}\hat{\mathbf{K}}$ is identically zero for **every** feedback matrix $\mathbf{K}$. As a result, $\hat{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{cl}}$ remains block upper-triangular, and its characteristic polynomial factors into the product of the two diagonal block determinants:

$$
\det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{cl}}) = \det(s\mathbf{I} - \hat{\mathbf{A}}_{\text{cl}}) = \det\big(s\mathbf{I}_{r_c} - (\mathbf{A}_c - \mathbf{B}_c \mathbf{K}_c)\big) \cdot \det(s\mathbf{I}_{n - r_c} - \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}})
$$

Look closely at the two factors on the right-hand side:
1. **Controllable factor:** Because the subsystem pair $(\mathbf{A}_c, \mathbf{B}_c)$ is completely controllable by construction, we can choose $\mathbf{K}_c$ (via Ackermann's formula in [Chapter 25](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/07-state-feedback/02-ackermann-state-feedback-gain-formula/)) to place all $r_c$ eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}_c - \mathbf{B}_c\mathbf{K}_c$ anywhere we please in the open left half-plane $\operatorname{Re}(s) < 0$.
2. **Uncontrollable factor:** The second polynomial $\det(s\mathbf{I}_{n - r_c} - \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}})$ does not contain $\mathbf{K}_c$ or $\mathbf{K}_{\bar{c}}$ at all! Its roots—the $n - r_c$ **uncontrollable eigenvalues** $\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}})$—are fixed structural invariants under state feedback.

Therefore, a feedback gain $\mathbf{K}$ can make all $n$ eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$ strictly stable if and only if the unmovable eigenvalues of the uncontrollable block $\mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}}$ already lie strictly in the open left half-plane:

$$
\boxed{(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}) \text{ is stabilizable} \iff \forall \lambda \in \sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}}), \;\; \operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0}
$$

Equivalently, every unstable or marginally stable mode of $\mathbf{A}$ ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0$) must belong to the controllable subsystem $\sigma(\mathbf{A}_c)$.

### PBH Stabilizability Criterion

Computing a full basis transformation $\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{T}_c \;\; \mathbf{T}_{\bar{c}}]$ and inverting $\mathbf{T}$ just to check whether $\mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}}$ is Hurwitz would be slow and error-prone on an exam. Instead, the **Popov-Belevitch-Hautus (PBH) test** from [Chapter 20](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/01-kalman-controllability-matrix-rank-criterion/) lets us test stabilizability directly on the original matrices $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ in seconds.

Recall that an eigenvalue $\lambda \in \sigma(\mathbf{A})$ belongs to the uncontrollable block $\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}})$ if and only if $\operatorname{rank}[\lambda\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;\; \mathbf{B}] < n$. Requiring all uncontrollable eigenvalues to satisfy $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$ is the exact logical contrapositive of requiring every eigenvalue with $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0$ to be controllable ($\operatorname{rank}[\lambda\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;\; \mathbf{B}] = n$):

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\begin{bmatrix} \lambda \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} & \mathbf{B} \end{bmatrix} = n \qquad \forall \lambda \in \sigma(\mathbf{A}) \text{ such that } \operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0}
$$

This **PBH Stabilizability Criterion** gives a fast operational shortcut:
- If an eigenvalue $\lambda_i \in \sigma(\mathbf{A})$ is already strictly stable ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i) < 0$), **do not even test it** for stabilizability! Whether $\operatorname{rank}[\lambda_i\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;\; \mathbf{B}]$ equals $n$ (controllable) or drops below $n$ (uncontrollable), that mode will decay to zero exponentially as $e^{\lambda_i t} \to 0$.
- You only need to evaluate $\operatorname{rank}[\lambda_u\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;\; \mathbf{B}]$ at the eigenvalues $\lambda_u \in \sigma(\mathbf{A})$ lying in the **closed right half-plane** $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_u) \ge 0$ (which includes strictly unstable poles $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_u) > 0$, integrators at $\lambda_u = 0$, and undamped imaginary-axis poles $\lambda_u = \pm j\omega$). If every such mode has full row rank $n$, the system is stabilizable; if even one mode with $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_u) \ge 0$ has rank $< n$, the system is **unstabilizable**.

### Dual Concept: Detectability

Now consider the dual problem in state estimation ([Chapter 27](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/08-observers-compensation/01-luenberger-state-observer-architecture/)). To estimate an unmeasured state vector $\mathbf{x}(t)$ from input-output measurements $(\mathbf{u}(t), \mathbf{y}(t))$, a **Luenberger state observer** runs an internal simulation of the plant driven by the output prediction error $\mathbf{y}(t) - \mathbf{C}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ through an observer gain matrix $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$:

$$
\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}(t) + \mathbf{L}\big(\mathbf{y}(t) - \mathbf{C}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)\big)
$$

Define the state estimation error vector $\mathbf{e}(t) = \mathbf{x}(t) - \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$. Subtracting the observer equation from the true plant equation $\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}(t)$ cancels the control input term $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}(t)$ and yields the autonomous error differential equation:

$$
\dot{\mathbf{e}}(t) = \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) - \dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\big(\mathbf{x}(t) - \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)\big) - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}\big(\mathbf{x}(t) - \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)\big) = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\mathbf{e}(t)
$$

For the state estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ to converge exponentially to the true state $\mathbf{x}(t)$ ($\mathbf{e}(t) \to \mathbf{0}$ as $t \to \infty$) from any initial estimation mismatch $\mathbf{e}(0)$, we need to choose $\mathbf{L}$ so that the observer error matrix $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ is Hurwitz ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$).

A pair $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{C})$ is defined to be **detectable** if there exists an observer gain matrix $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ such that $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ is Hurwitz. Why is detectability the exact Kalman dual of stabilizability? Because transposing a square matrix leaves its characteristic polynomial and eigenvalues unchanged ($\det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}) = \det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}^T)$):

$$
\sigma(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}) = \sigma\left((\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})^T\right) = \sigma\left(\mathbf{A}^T - \mathbf{C}^T\mathbf{L}^T\right)
$$

Setting $\mathbf{A}_{\text{dual}} = \mathbf{A}^T$, $\mathbf{B}_{\text{dual}} = \mathbf{C}^T$, and $\mathbf{K}_{\text{dual}} = \mathbf{L}^T$ turns $\mathbf{A}^T - \mathbf{C}^T\mathbf{L}^T$ into the exact state-feedback matrix $\mathbf{A}_{\text{dual}} - \mathbf{B}_{\text{dual}}\mathbf{K}_{\text{dual}}$! Therefore:

$$
\boxed{(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{C}) \text{ is detectable} \iff (\mathbf{A}^T, \mathbf{C}^T) \text{ is stabilizable}}
$$

In observability Kalman canonical coordinates $\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{T}_o \;\; \mathbf{T}_{\bar{o}}]$ from [Chapter 22](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/03-kalman-canonical-subspace-decomposition/), where $\hat{\mathbf{A}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_o & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{A}_{21} & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_o & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}$, subtracting $\hat{\mathbf{L}}\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{L}_o \\ \mathbf{L}_{\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_o & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}$ gives:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{A}} - \hat{\mathbf{L}}\hat{\mathbf{C}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_o - \mathbf{L}_o\mathbf{C}_o & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{A}_{21} - \mathbf{L}_{\bar{o}}\mathbf{C}_o & \mathbf{A}_{\bar{o}} \end{bmatrix} \implies \det(s\mathbf{I} - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})) = \det\big(s\mathbf{I} - (\mathbf{A}_o - \mathbf{L}_o\mathbf{C}_o)\big)\det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\bar{o}})
$$

The eigenvalues of the unobservable subsystem $\mathbf{A}_{\bar{o}}$ cannot be shifted by any observer gain $\mathbf{L}$. Thus $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{C})$ is detectable if and only if every unobservable mode is already strictly stable ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$ for all $\lambda \in \sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\bar{o}})$), which is tested directly via the **PBH Detectability Criterion**:

$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\begin{bmatrix} \lambda \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \\ \mathbf{C} \end{bmatrix} = n \qquad \forall \lambda \in \sigma(\mathbf{A}) \text{ such that } \operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0}
$$

If an unobservable mode has $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0$ (such as an unmeasured pure integrator drift at $\lambda = 0$), then the estimation error component along that right eigenvector obeys $\dot{e}_i(t) = \lambda e_i(t)$ and never decays to zero, no matter how the observer gain $\mathbf{L}$ is tuned.

<Callout type="bridge" title="Robotics Bridge: Actuator Saturation and Underactuation">

In multirotor drones and robotic manipulators, an actuator failure (such as a seized quadrotor ESC or jammed tilt rotor) drops input matrix rank: $\mathbf{B}_{\text{nominal}} \to \mathbf{B}_{\text{fault}}$. Before executing reconfigurable control allocation, the flight computer checks the **PBH stabilizability test** on $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}_{\text{fault}})$. If the fault renders an unstable rigid-body mode uncontrollable, the vehicle is provably unstabilizable and must trigger emergency parachute deployment or controlled autorotation.

| **Detectability** | $\operatorname{rank}[\lambda\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}; \; \mathbf{C}] = n$ | $\lambda \in \sigma(\mathbf{A})$ with $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0$ | All have $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$ | Can make $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ Hurwitz |

### Operational PBH Procedure

1. Compute all open-loop eigenvalues $\lambda_i \in \sigma(\mathbf{A})$ by solving $\det(\lambda\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}) = 0$.
2. Filter the spectrum to keep **only** the unstable or marginally stable modes satisfying $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i) \ge 0$ (including $\lambda = 0$ and $\lambda = \pm j\omega$). If every eigenvalue of $\mathbf{A}$ already has $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i) < 0$, the system is automatically both stabilizable and detectable (even for $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{0}$).
3. Test $\operatorname{rank}[\lambda_i\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;\; \mathbf{B}] = n$ (for stabilizability) or $\operatorname{rank}[\lambda_i\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}; \; \mathbf{C}] = n$ (for detectability) at each filtered eigenvalue $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i) \ge 0$.
4. The property holds if and only if full rank $n$ is achieved at **every** filtered eigenvalue.

### Key Proofs

- **Invariance of $\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}})$:** In controllability canonical coordinates, $\mathbf{B}_{\bar{c}} = \mathbf{0}$ preserves the lower-left zero block of $\hat{\mathbf{A}} - \hat{\mathbf{B}}\hat{\mathbf{K}}$, factoring $\det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{cl}}) = \det(s\mathbf{I} - (\mathbf{A}_c - \mathbf{B}_c\mathbf{K}_c))\det(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\bar{c}})$.
- **Detectability Duality:** Because $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ and $(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})^T = \mathbf{A}^T - \mathbf{C}^T\mathbf{L}^T$ share the exact same eigenvalues, $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{C})$ is detectable if and only if $(\mathbf{A}^T, \mathbf{C}^T)$ is stabilizable.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistakes">
1. **Ignoring $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) = 0$:** Marginal eigenvalues (such as integrators at $\lambda = 0$ or undamped poles $\pm j\omega$) satisfy $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0$ and **must** pass the PBH test for stabilizability/detectability.
2. **Checking $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R})$ Alone:** Finding $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) < n$ only proves the system is not completely controllable—it may still be **stabilizable** if the uncontrollable modes lie strictly in the open left half-plane.
3. **Mixing $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{C}$ Dimensions:** Stabilizability appends $\mathbf{B}$ on the **right** ($n \times (n+m)$); detectability stacks $\mathbf{C}$ at the **bottom** ($(n+p) \times n$).
</Callout>

<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. **Open-loop eigenvalues:**
   Matrix $\mathbf{A}$ is upper triangular with diagonal elements $2, -3, -1$. Its eigenvalues are:
   $$
   \lambda_1 = 2, \quad \lambda_2 = -3, \quad \lambda_3 = -1
   $$
   The unstable eigenvalue is $\lambda_1 = 2$ because $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_1) = 2 \ge 0$. Modes $\lambda_2 = -3$ and $\lambda_3 = -1$ are strictly stable ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$).

2. **PBH test matrix at $\lambda = 2$:**
   $$
   2\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 - 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 - (-3) & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 - (-1) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   $$
   \mathbf{M}_{\text{PBH}}(2) = \begin{bmatrix} 2\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} & \mathbf{B} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

3. **Rank evaluation:**
   Row 1 of $\mathbf{M}_{\text{PBH}}(2)$ is identically zero, while rows 2 and 3 have pivots in columns 2 and 3 ($5$ and $3$). Therefore:
   $$
   \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{M}_{\text{PBH}}(2)) = 2 < 3 = n
   $$

4. **Stabilizability conclusion:**
   By the PBH stabilizability criterion, a system is stabilizable if and only if $\operatorname{rank}[\lambda \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;|\; \mathbf{B}] = n$ for all $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$ with $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0$.
   Because the rank test fails at the unstable eigenvalue $\lambda = 2$, this mode is uncontrollable.
   Hence, the system is **unstabilizable**. No linear state feedback law $\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x}$ can alter the unstable eigenvalue $\lambda = 2$; the closed-loop system will always possess an unstable pole at $s = 2$.

</details>

<Exercise client:load id="ex-23-2" title="Exercise 23.2 — Parametric Stabilizability Analysis" level="Exam Standard" hint="Test the PBH rank condition at both $\lambda_1 = 1$ and $\lambda_3 = 3$; $\lambda_2 = -2$ is already stable.">

A third-order system depends on a real parameter $\gamma \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$
\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ \gamma - 2 \end{bmatrix}
$$

1. Identify all open-loop eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}$ and determine which modes require active feedback stabilization.
2. Assemble the PBH test matrix for each unstable eigenvalue ($\lambda_1 = 1$ and $\lambda_3 = 3$).
3. Find the condition on $\gamma$ under which the system is stabilizable.
4. Show that mode $\lambda_2 = -2$ is uncontrollable for all $\gamma \in \mathbb{R}$. Explain why its uncontrollability does not prevent the system from being stabilizable.

<StepCheck client:load
  prompt="For which excluded value of $\gamma$ does the system become unstabilizable?"
  expected={2}
  tolerance={0.01}
  unit=""
  explanation="At $\lambda_3 = 3$, row 3 of $[3\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A} \mid \mathbf{B}]$ is $[0, 0, 0, \gamma - 2]$, which vanishes when $\gamma = 2$."
/>

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. **Open-loop eigenvalues and stabilization requirements:**
   Since $\mathbf{A} = \operatorname{diag}(1, -2, 3)$, its eigenvalues are:
   $$
   \lambda_1 = 1, \quad \lambda_2 = -2, \quad \lambda_3 = 3
   $$
   The modes with $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) \ge 0$ that require active stabilization are $\lambda_1 = 1$ and $\lambda_3 = 3$. Mode $\lambda_2 = -2$ is already naturally asymptotically stable ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_2) < 0$).

2. **PBH test matrices for unstable eigenvalues:**
   * **For $\lambda_1 = 1$:**
     $$
     [1\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;|\; \mathbf{B}] = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - 1 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 - (-2) & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 - 3 & \gamma - 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 & \gamma - 2 \end{bmatrix}
     $$
     Row 1 has pivot in column 4 (value $1 \neq 0$). Row 2 has pivot in column 2 (value $3 \neq 0$). Row 3 has pivot in column 3 (value $-2 \neq 0$).
     The rows are linearly independent for all values of $\gamma$. Thus:
     $$
     \operatorname{rank}[1\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;|\; \mathbf{B}] = 3 = n \quad \text{for all } \gamma \in \mathbb{R}
     $$
     The unstable mode $\lambda_1 = 1$ is always controllable.

   * **For $\lambda_3 = 3$:**
     $$
     [3\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;|\; \mathbf{B}] = \begin{bmatrix} 3 - 1 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 3 - (-2) & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 - 3 & \gamma - 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 5 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \gamma - 2 \end{bmatrix}
     $$
     The first two rows have independent pivots ($2$ in column 1, $5$ in column 2). The third row is $[0, \; 0, \; 0, \; \gamma - 2]$.
     * If $\gamma \neq 2$, the third row is non-zero (pivot $\gamma - 2$), yielding $\operatorname{rank} = 3 = n$.
     * If $\gamma = 2$, the third row is entirely zero, yielding $\operatorname{rank} = 2 < 3$.

3. **Stabilizability condition on $\gamma$:**
   The system is stabilizable if and only if all unstable eigenvalues are controllable.
   Therefore, the system is **stabilizable** if and only if $\gamma \neq 2$.
   When $\gamma = 2$, the unstable eigenvalue $\lambda_3 = 3$ is uncontrollable, rendering the system unstabilizable.

4. **Uncontrollability of mode $\lambda_2 = -2$:**
   Testing PBH at $\lambda_2 = -2$:
   $$
   [-2\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A} \;|\; \mathbf{B}] = \begin{bmatrix} -3 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -5 & \gamma - 2 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Row 2 is identically zero regardless of $\gamma$, so $\operatorname{rank} = 2 < 3$. Mode $\lambda_2 = -2$ is uncontrollable for all $\gamma \in \mathbb{R}$.
   However, stabilizability only requires that the uncontrollable subsystem be asymptotically stable ($\operatorname{Re}(\lambda) < 0$). Because the autonomous trajectory of state $x_2$ naturally decays exponentially to zero ($e^{-2t} \to 0$ as $t \to \infty$), it does not require external control authority to remain bounded.
