## Introduction

Once controllability $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{R}) = n$ is confirmed in Part VI, state-space control transitions from analysis to synthesis. Closing a static state-feedback law $\mathbf{u}(t) = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{v}(t)$ shifts the open-loop dynamics from $\mathbf{A}$ to the closed-loop matrix $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$, allowing arbitrary assignment of all $n$ closed-loop eigenvalues.

Part VII builds pole placement from the ground up: matching desired polynomial coefficients directly in Controllable Canonical Form, bypassing basis conversion with **Ackermann's Formula**, and choosing physically realizable target poles from transient overshoot and settling-time specifications.

### Running Example

Across Chapters 24–26, we stabilize and shape a controllable plant via full-state feedback $\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{v}$:

$$\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}(t), \qquad \det\big(s\mathbf{I} - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\big) = p^*(s)$$

Chapter 24 converts $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ to companion coordinates to match coefficients, Chapter 25 computes $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{e}_n^T \mathcal{R}^{-1} p^*(\mathbf{A})$ directly, and Chapter 26 selects $p^*(s)$ from overshoot $\hat{s}$ and settling time $T_s$ via the Dominant Pole Rule.

### Real-World Applications

- **Unstable Plant Stabilization:** Full-state feedback moves unstable open-loop RHP poles (such as upright inverted pendulums and launchers) strictly into the left-half plane.
- **Actuator Bandwidth Balance:** Placing dominant second-order poles and placing auxiliary poles $5\times\text{--}10\times$ faster prevents excessive gains from saturating actuators or amplifying sensor noise.
