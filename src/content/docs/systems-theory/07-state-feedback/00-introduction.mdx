## Introduction

Up through Part VI, you analyzed systems as they are. Part VII flips the perspective from **analysis** to **synthesis**: if the pair $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ is controllable, you gain complete authority to reshape the system's dynamics by closing a static state-feedback law $\mathbf{u}(t) = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{v}(t)$.

Instead of settling for the open-loop eigenvalues nature gave the plant, you choose the desired closed-loop characteristic polynomial $p^*(s)$ and solve algebraically for the gain vector $\mathbf{K}$ that places all $n$ closed-loop poles wherever performance requires—first conceptually in Controllable Canonical Form, then directly via **Ackermann's Formula**, and finally practically by mapping real time-domain specs (settling time and overshoot) into dominant pole locations.

### Practical Real-World Perspective

- **Balancing Unstable Hardware:** An inverted pendulum, Segway, or vertical-takeoff rocket has an open-loop pole in the right-half plane ($\lambda > 0$). Full-state feedback moves that unstable pole safely into the left-half plane so the vehicle stands upright actively.
- **Preventing Actuator Saturation:** Knowing how to pick closed-loop poles using the **Dominant Pole Principle** (Rule of Five) keeps auxiliary poles fast enough to stay invisible without pushing gains so high that motor drivers clip or amplify gyroscope noise.
