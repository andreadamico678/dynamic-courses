## Course Scope

This textbook covers **Linear Systems Theory** (*Teoria dei Sistemi*, UniCT L-8 *Ingegneria Informatica*), pairing **step-by-step analytical procedures** for the comprehensive written exam with **structural state-space theory** for oral mastery and downstream robotics control.

| Exam Track | Core Coverage | Mandatory Assessment Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Written Exam** | Areas 1–8 | State equations, resolvent $(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}$, $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$, Routh-Hurwitz tables, Bode plots, Kalman rank, Ackermann control & observers |
| **Oral Defense** | Foundational Proofs | Lyapunov spectral conditions, BIBO vs. internal stability, Kalman criteria & Cayley-Hamilton, Separation Principle factorization |

Every computational procedure tested on the written exam is developed in full step-by-step detail, while peripheral algebraic corner cases and non-examinable specialized proofs are omitted to keep focus on core analytical fluency and physical insight.

## Prerequisites

1. **Linear Algebra:** Matrix multiplication, determinants, rank, matrix inversion, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors.
2. **Differential Equations:** Linear constant-coefficient ordinary differential equations and initial conditions.
3. **Complex Variables:** Euler's formula, polar magnitude and phase angle, and complex conjugation in $\mathbb{C}$.

## Engineering Applications

Every mathematical tool in this course maps directly onto autonomous systems, robotics, and flight-control engineering:

- **State-Space Modeling:** Multi-body mechanical linkages, quadrotor pitch dynamics, and coordinate frame rotations obey coupled first-order state equations $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}$.
- **Stability and Routh Bounds:** Parametric Routh-Hurwitz arrays predict closed-loop proportional/PI gain limits $K_{\max}$ and structural oscillation frequencies $\omega_{\text{osc}}$ analytically before hardware testing.
- **Frequency Response:** Bode magnitude and phase diagrams measure resonance peaks $M_r$, phase margin $\varphi_m$, and bandwidth ceilings from RHP zeros and telemetry delays.
- **Control and Estimation:** Kalman rank tests validate actuator and sensor placement before synthesizing state-feedback pole placement ($\mathbf{K}$), Luenberger observers ($\mathbf{L}$), and dynamic output compensators.

## Curriculum Roadmap

- **[Part I — Dynamic State Models](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/01-state-models/00-introduction/)** — State variables, multi-body modeling, similarity transformations, and block diagram reduction.
- **[Part II — Laplace and Resolvent](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/02-laplace-resolvent/00-introduction/)** — Unilateral Laplace theorems, partial fraction residues, $(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}$, and transfer matrix invariants.
- **[Part III — Time-Domain Response](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/03-time-response/00-introduction/)** — Matrix exponential $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$, free modal trajectories, impulse response $w(t)$, and Lagrange convolution.
- **[Part IV — System Stability](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/04-stability-routh/00-introduction/)** — Equilibrium points, Lyapunov spectral conditions, Routh-Hurwitz arrays, parametric gain bounds, and BIBO stability.
- **[Part V — Frequency Response](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/05-frequency-bode/00-introduction/)** — Sinusoidal steady state, asymptotic Bode magnitude/phase drafting, stability margins, and non-minimum phase dynamics.
- **[Part VI — Kalman Structure](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/00-introduction/)** — Kalman controllability and observability rank criteria, PBH modal tests, canonical decomposition, and stabilizability.
- **[Part VII — State Feedback Control](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/07-state-feedback/00-introduction/)** — Controllable canonical form, Ackermann's formula $\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x}$, and dominant closed-loop pole placement.
- **[Part VIII — State Observers](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/08-observers-compensation/00-introduction/)** — Luenberger observers, dual Ackermann gain $\mathbf{L}$, error dynamics, and the Separation Principle compensator.
