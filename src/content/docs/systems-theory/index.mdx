1. **Academic Mastery (UniCT L-8 Automatica):** Achieving unconditional success ($\ge 28/30$) across the single comprehensive end-of-semester in-itinere exam (or general written exam) and oral defense of the *Teoria dei Sistemi* module at the Università degli Studi di Catania.

## AI & Large Language Model Influence

In modern control engineering, numerical matrix operations, symbolic Laplace inversions, and boilerplate state-feedback gain computations are easily automated by numerical solvers (MATLAB, Python `scipy.signal`, SymPy) and generative AI coding agents. However, AI cannot diagnose physical instability caused by unmodeled structural dynamics, actuator saturation, or unobservable sensor modes.

| Automated by AI & Symbolic Solvers | Irreplaceable Human Engineering Judgment |
| :--- | :--- |
| Symbolic inversion of $(sI - A)^{-1}$ and partial fraction decomposition | Selecting state coordinates that reflect measurable physical quantities |
| Computing Routh arrays and polynomial determinants | Distinguishing mathematical pole cancellation from hidden internal instability |
| Evaluating Ackermann's formula $\mathbf{k} = \mathbf{e}_n^T \mathcal{P}^{-1} \alpha(\mathbf{A})$ | Choosing closed-loop pole locations that respect physical motor bandwidth and slew rates |
| Drawing Bode magnitude and phase plots from rational transfer functions | Validating sensor noise immunity versus transient tracking trade-offs in drone autopilots |

When designing flight controllers for autonomous multirotors, blindly trusting an LLM-generated state-feedback gain vector without verifying the rank of the controllability matrix or understanding the separation principle leads directly to hardware destruction. This text enforces the mathematical rigor necessary to supervise, verify, and architect autonomous systems with mathematical certainty.

### Academic Exam Filters vs. Production Engineering Reality

* **Academic Exam Filter:** Manually computing by hand the adjugate matrix of a $3\times 3$ symbolic matrix $(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})$ with matricola parameters $a$ and $b$, or evaluating the epsilon method on a singular Routh table.
* **Production Reality:** Understanding that pole-zero cancellations in $\mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B}$ hide uncontrollable or unobservable modes that can grow exponentially to infinity inside the system without appearing in the input-output transfer function.
* **Bridging the Divide:** We drill the hand-calculation algorithms required to ace the written exams while grounding every theorem in physical hardware invariants (actuator current limits, IMU sampling jitter, and real-time state estimation).

## Structure of the Textbook

The curriculum is partitioned into two distinct operational parts and eight strictly sequential, monotonically dependent areas, mirroring the UniCT semester curriculum:

### Part I: State-Space Modeling, Resolvent & Stability Dynamics
1. **Area 1: Continuous-Time Dynamic State Models & Physical Systems** — Establishing state variables, differential state equations, multi-body mechanical cart systems, coordinate similarity transformations, and block diagram algebra reduction.
2. **Area 2: Operational Laplace Transforms & State Resolvent Inversion** — Unilateral Laplace transformation primitives, operational theorems, partial fraction expansions (Heaviside residues), the state resolvent matrix $(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}$, and transfer function invariance $\mathbf{W}(s) = \mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{D}$.
3. **Area 3: Dynamic Trajectories & Matrix Exponential Transitions** — The matrix exponential $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$, Taylor series, Cayley-Hamilton synthesis, free state response evaluation $\mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{x}(0)$, and forced response via the Lagrange convolution integral.
4. **Area 4: System Stability Criteria & Routh** — LTI equilibrium points, Lyapunov internal stability via eigenvalue spectra ($m_a$ vs $m_g$), algebraic Routh-Hurwitz tabular arrays (including singular zero pivots and all-zero rows), parametric stability bounds, and BIBO external stability.

### Part II: Harmonic Response, Structural Kalman Properties & State Feedback Control (Comprehensive January Exam)
5. **Area 5: Harmonic Response & Frequency Analysis** — Frequency response function $\mathbf{W}(j\omega)$, asymptotic and exact Bode magnitude and phase diagrams, real resonances, and non-minimum phase systems (RHP zeros and time delays).
6. **Area 6: Kalman Structural Properties** — Kalman controllability ($\mathcal{P}$) and observability ($\mathcal{Q}$) rank criteria, PBH tests, 4-subspace Kalman canonical decomposition, and stabilizability verification of uncontrollable modes.
7. **Area 7: State Feedback Control & Dominant Pole Placement** — Controllable canonical form pole placement, Ackermann state feedback formula $\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{K}\mathbf{x}$, and dominant second-order closed-loop pole specifications ($-a \pm jb$, damping $\xi$, natural frequency $\omega_n$).
8. **Area 8: State Observer Synthesis & Dynamic Compensation** — Luenberger asymptotic state observers, error dynamics autonomy $\dot{\mathbf{e}} = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\mathbf{e}$, dual Ackermann observer gain synthesis ($\mathbf{L}$), and the Separation Principle dynamic output compensator.

Every chapter concludes with active-recall exercises spanning mechanical calculation, conceptual differentiation, real-world troubleshooting, and failure mode analysis. Complete step-by-step solutions are compiled in [Appendix: Exercise Solutions](#structure-of-the-textbook). Furthermore, dedicated Obsidian spaced-repetition flashcards are provided in [flashcards/](https://github.com/andreadamico678/dynamic-courses/tree/main/courses/systems-theory/flashcards) for every single chapter.

## 25/30 Fast-Track Exam Syllabus & Disclaimers

To maximize study efficiency for students targeting a solid **25/30** without getting bogged down in 30 e Lode edge cases, this edition incorporates a **Pure-Exam Progression Filter**:

1. **Two-Level Problem Sets:**
   - **[LEVEL 1: WARM-UP DRILL]**: Builds procedural fluency in matrix manipulation, canonical forms, and formula application.
   - **[LEVEL 2: EXAM BENCHMARK]**: Represents authentic written exam problems across the four standard UniCT exam archetypes.
   - **Level 3 & Non-Examinable Derivations (Trimmed for 25/30 Target):** Highly specialized edge cases and advanced derivations (time-domain Lagrange convolution integrals, Poisson sensitivity integral bounds, singular Routh $\varepsilon$-tables, full $4\times 4$ Kalman decomposition coordinate synthesis, Ackermann formula Cayley-Hamilton algebraic proofs, Doyle's robustness margin counterexamples, and high-gain observer peaking) have been trimmed from the exercise sets to keep focus on core written and oral exam competence.
2. **Oral Defense Core:**
   - The oral exam focuses on fundamental theoretical pillars: Lyapunov spectral stability conditions, the relation between internal and BIBO stability, Kalman rank criteria and Cayley-Hamilton, state feedback arbitrary pole placement, observer error dynamics $\dot{\mathbf{e}} = (\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\mathbf{e}$, the Separation Principle block-triangular proof, and the harmonic frequency response theorem.
   - Complex manual proofs that are never evaluated on blackboard defenses are explicitly flagged so students can bypass them with complete confidence.
