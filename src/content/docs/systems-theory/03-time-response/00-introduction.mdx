## Introduction

Once a dynamical system is expressed in state-space form $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}$, predicting its exact trajectory $\mathbf{x}(t)$ for all $t \ge 0$ requires generalizing the scalar exponential $e^{at}$ into the **state transition matrix** $\mathbf{\Phi}(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}$.

Part III solves continuous-time state and output evolution cleanly via **Lagrange's Formula**, separating the total response by superposition into the natural free evolution $\mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{x}(0)$ driven by initial stored energy and the forced convolution response driven by actuator inputs $\mathbf{u}(t)$.

### Running Example

Across Chapters 8–10, we evaluate a damped second-order state model $\mathbf{x}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ under non-zero initial displacement $\mathbf{x}(0)$ and step excitation $u(t) = 1(t)$:

$$\mathbf{x}(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{x}(0) + \int_0^t e^{\mathbf{A}(t-\tau)}\mathbf{B}u(\tau)\,d\tau$$

Chapter 8 computes $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$ via resolvent inversion, spectral decomposition, and Cayley-Hamilton; Chapter 9 maps free trajectories $\mathbf{x}_l(t)$ onto phase portraits; and Chapter 10 evaluates forced trajectories and impulse weights $w(t) = \mathbf{C}e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{B}$.

### Real-World Applications

- **Attitude Gust Recovery:** Free response $e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{x}(0)$ determines whether a disturbed quadrotor settles smoothly, overshoots, or diverges along an eigenvector.
- **Digital Discretization:** Embedded flight loops compute $\mathbf{A}_d = e^{\mathbf{A}\Delta t}$ to advance discrete-time estimators and controllers every sampling interval.
- **Impulse Characterization:** Measuring impulse response $w(t) = \mathbf{C}e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{B}$ predicts output trajectories under arbitrary excitation via convolution.
