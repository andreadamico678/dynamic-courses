## Introduction

Once a physical system is written as $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}$, the central question is: **where will the state vector be at any future time $t > 0$?** Part III answers this completely in the time domain by generalizing the scalar exponential $e^{at}$ into the **state transition matrix** $\mathbf{\Phi}(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}$.

Conceptually, $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$ acts as the system's propagator: multiply any initial state $\mathbf{x}(0)$ by $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$ and you immediately trace its natural, unforced trajectory. Add external actuator commands $\mathbf{u}(t)$, and **Lagrange's Formula** blends every past impulse of control effort up to time $t$ via superposition into one clean free + forced decomposition.

### Practical Real-World Perspective

- **Drone Gust Recovery (Free Response):** When a crosswind tilts a hovering quadrotor and then vanishes, $\mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{x}_0$ determines whether the attitude spirals smoothly back to level, overshoots, or tumbles monotonically along an eigenvector manifold.
- **Digital Simulation & Discrete Control Loops:** Embedded autopilots running on microcontrollers at $400\text{ Hz}$ discretize continuous plant dynamics by evaluating $\mathbf{A}_d = e^{\mathbf{A}\Delta t}$ every $2.5\text{ ms}$.
- **Impulse Weighting:** Measuring a structure's impulse response $w(t) = \mathbf{C}e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{B}$ from a single hammer tap lets vibration engineers predict its output under any arbitrary road or aerodynamic load via convolution.
