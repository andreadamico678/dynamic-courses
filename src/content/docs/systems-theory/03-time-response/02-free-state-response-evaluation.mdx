## Autonomous Dynamics and Natural Modes

When a continuous-time linear system is subjected to an initial energy displacement $\mathbf{x}(0) = \mathbf{x}_0$ in the absence of external excitation ($\mathbf{u}(t) \equiv \mathbf{0}$ for all $t \ge 0$), its internal motion is termed the **Free State Response** (or autonomous motion $\mathbf{x}_l(t)$):

$$\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x}(t), \quad \mathbf{x}(0) = \mathbf{x}_0 \implies \mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t} \mathbf{x}_0$$

$$\mathbf{y}_l(t) = \mathbf{C} \mathbf{x}_l(t) = \mathbf{C} e^{\mathbf{A}t} \mathbf{x}_0$$

The trajectory $\mathbf{x}_l(t)$ is entirely synthesized from a finite linear combination of basic elementary mathematical waveforms termed the **Natural Modes** of the system.

<Callout type="note" title="Taxonomy of Natural Dynamic Modes">

The natural modes are determined uniquely by the roots of the characteristic polynomial $p(\lambda) = \det(\lambda \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}) = 0$:
1. **Real Simple Eigenvalue ($\lambda_i \in \mathbb{R}$):** Generates an exponential mode:
   $$m_i(t) = e^{\lambda_i t}$$
   Converges monotonically to 0 if $\lambda_i < 0$; diverges to $\pm \infty$ if $\lambda_i > 0$.
2. **Real Repeated Eigenvalue of Multiplicity $m$ with Defect ($m_g < m_a$):** Generates secular polynomial-exponential modes:
   $$e^{\lambda_i t}, \quad t e^{\lambda_i t}, \quad t^2 e^{\lambda_i t}, \quad \dots, \quad t^{m-1} e^{\lambda_i t}$$
3. **Complex Conjugate Pair ($\lambda_{1,2} = \sigma \pm j\omega$):** Generates pseudoperiodic harmonic modes:
   $$m(t) = e^{\sigma t} \cos(\omega t), \quad e^{\sigma t} \sin(\omega t)$$
   Damped oscillation if $\sigma < 0$; undamped harmonic oscillation if $\sigma = 0$; growing oscillatory divergence if $\sigma > 0$.

</Callout>

## Modal Projection and Eigenvector Invariance

Let $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ possess $n$ distinct eigenvalues $\{\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n\}$ with corresponding right eigenvectors $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_n\}$. Any initial condition vector $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ can be uniquely expanded in the eigenvector basis:

$$\mathbf{x}_0 = c_1 \mathbf{v}_1 + c_2 \mathbf{v}_2 + \dots + c_n \mathbf{v}_n = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \mathbf{v}_i$$

where the modal coordinates $\mathbf{c} = \begin{bmatrix} c_1 & \dots & c_n \end{bmatrix}^T$ are computed via $\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{T}^{-1} \mathbf{x}_0$ with $\mathbf{T} = [\mathbf{v}_1 \; \dots \; \mathbf{v}_n]$.
Applying the state transition operator to the equation above:

$$\mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t} \mathbf{x}_0 = e^{\mathbf{A}t} \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \mathbf{v}_i = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i e^{\mathbf{A}t} \mathbf{v}_i$$

Because $\mathbf{A} \mathbf{v}_i = \lambda_i \mathbf{v}_i$, the series definition the equation above establishes that $e^{\mathbf{A}t} \mathbf{v}_i = e^{\lambda_i t} \mathbf{v}_i$. Thus:

$$\mathbf{x}_l(t) = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i e^{\lambda_i t} \mathbf{v}_i = c_1 e^{\lambda_1 t} \mathbf{v}_1 + c_2 e^{\lambda_2 t} \mathbf{v}_2 + \dots + c_n e^{\lambda_n t} \mathbf{v}_n$$

<Callout type="tip" title="Foundational Theorem: Invariance of Eigenspaces">

If the initial state $\mathbf{x}_0$ is chosen **parallel to an eigenvector** $\mathbf{v}_k$ (i.e., $\mathbf{x}_0 = c_k \mathbf{v}_k$), then $c_i = 0$ for all $i \neq k$.
The resulting state trajectory is strictly confined to the 1-dimensional ray $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{v}_k\}$:
$$\mathbf{x}_l(t) = c_k e^{\lambda_k t} \mathbf{v}_k$$
In this condition, **only the single mode $e^{\lambda_k t}$ is excited**. No other modes appear in the internal motion or output!

</Callout>

## Detailed Exam Archetype: Hand Derivation of $\mathbf{x}_l(t)$

We solve the analytical derivation appearing in the 2024 and 2025 examination problems:

**Problem Statement:** Given the second-order system:
$$\dot{x}_1(t) = x_2(t)$$
$$\dot{x}_2(t) = -6 x_1(t) - 5 x_2(t)$$
Calculate the free state response $\mathbf{x}_l(t)$ starting from initial conditions $\mathbf{x}(0) = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ a \end{bmatrix}$.

### Step 1: Formulate Dynamic Matrix and Characteristic Roots
$$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -6 & -5 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{x}(0) = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ a \end{bmatrix}$$
$$p(\lambda) = \det\begin{bmatrix} \lambda & -1 \\ 6 & \lambda + 5 \end{bmatrix} = \lambda^2 + 5\lambda + 6 = (\lambda + 2)(\lambda + 3) = 0$$
The eigenvalues are $\lambda_1 = -2$ and $\lambda_2 = -3$. Both are strictly negative real numbers, ensuring asymptotic stability.

### Step 2: Compute Resolvent and State Transition Matrix
From [Matrix Exponential State Transition](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/03-time-response/01-matrix-exponential-state-transition/), the state transition matrix is:
$$e^{\mathbf{A}t} = \begin{bmatrix} 3e^{-2t} - 2e^{-3t} & e^{-2t} - e^{-3t} \\ -6e^{-2t} + 6e^{-3t} & -2e^{-2t} + 3e^{-3t} \end{bmatrix}$$

### Step 3: Matrix-Vector Multiplication with Initial State
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x}_l(t) &= e^{\mathbf{A}t} \mathbf{x}(0) = \begin{bmatrix} 3e^{-2t} - 2e^{-3t} & e^{-2t} - e^{-3t} \\ -6e^{-2t} + 6e^{-3t} & -2e^{-2t} + 3e^{-3t} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} a \\ a \end{bmatrix} \\
&= a \begin{bmatrix} (3e^{-2t} - 2e^{-3t}) + (e^{-2t} - e^{-3t}) \\ (-6e^{-2t} + 6e^{-3t}) + (-2e^{-2t} + 3e^{-3t}) \end{bmatrix} \\
&= a \begin{bmatrix} 4e^{-2t} - 3e^{-3t} \\ -8e^{-2t} + 9e^{-3t} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4a e^{-2t} - 3a e^{-3t} \\ -8a e^{-2t} + 9a e^{-3t} \end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}$$

### Step 4: Verification of Boundary Condition at $t = 0$
$$\mathbf{x}_l(0) = \begin{bmatrix} 4a(1) - 3a(1) \\ -8a(1) + 9a(1) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ a \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{x}(0) \quad \checkmark$$

<Callout type="bridge" title="SDU Robotics Bridge: Attitude Recovery in Multirotors After Wind Gusts">

When an autonomous drone experiences a sudden wind gust, aerodynamic torque displaces its pitch and roll angles away from hover trim ($\theta_0 \neq 0, \dot{\theta}_0 \neq 0$). If motor thrust commands temporarily saturate or if the autopilot executes an emergency unforced damping mode, the drone's attitude trajectory follows the free response:
$$\mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t} \mathbf{x}_0$$
Because aerodynamic damping guarantees $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_i) < 0$, the airframe naturally dissipates rotational kinetic energy and aligns back toward equilibrium. SDU flight testing protocols analyze free-response decay rates to experimentally identify unmodeled physical airframe damping parameters $d_r/J_{yy}$.

</Callout>

## Chapter Exercises

<Exercise client:load id="ex-9-1" title="Exercise 9.1: Decoupled Free State Trajectory" level="LEVEL 1: WARM-UP DRILL">

Consider the decoupled stable continuous-time system:
$$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 0 & -5 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{x}(0) = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$

1. Write the state transition matrix $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$ by inspection.
2. Calculate the explicit free state trajectory $\mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{x}(0)$.
3. Determine which of the two state variables decays faster. Calculate the time $t_1$ required for $x_1(t)$ to decay to $1\%$ of its initial value, and compare it with the time $t_2$ required for $x_2(t)$.

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. For diagonal matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 0 & -5 \end{bmatrix}$, the state transition matrix is:
   $$e^{\mathbf{A}t} = \begin{bmatrix} e^{-2t} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{-5t} \end{bmatrix}$$
2. The free state trajectory from initial state $\mathbf{x}(0) = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$ is:
   $$\mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{x}(0) = \begin{bmatrix} e^{-2t} & 0 \\ 0 & e^{-5t} \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3e^{-2t} \\ -e^{-5t} \end{bmatrix}$$
3. State variable $x_2(t)$ decays much faster because its eigenvalue magnitude $|\lambda_2| = 5$ is larger than $|\lambda_1| = 2$.
   Time to reach $1\%$ ($0.01$) of initial amplitude:
   * For $x_1(t)$: $e^{-2t_1} = 0.01 \implies -2t_1 = \ln(0.01) = -4.605 \implies t_1 \approx 2.303\text{ s}$.
   * For $x_2(t)$: $e^{-5t_2} = 0.01 \implies -5t_2 = -4.605 \implies t_2 \approx 0.921\text{ s}$.
   The slow mode $\lambda_1 = -2$ persists $2.5$ times longer and completely dictates the long-term convergence time.

</details>

</Exercise>

<Exercise client:load id="ex-9-2" title="Exercise 9.2: Authentic Exam Problem 1 Free Response" level="LEVEL 2: EXAM BENCHMARK">

Consider the continuous-time system from the UniCT examination paper ($a=1$):
$$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ -5/2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
with initial state condition:
$$\mathbf{x}(0) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

1. Determine system eigenvalues $\det(\lambda\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}) = 0$ and classify internal stability.
2. Compute the state resolvent matrix $(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}$ and its inverse Laplace transform $e^{\mathbf{A}t}$.
3. Calculate the analytical free state trajectory $\mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}\mathbf{x}(0)$.
4. Compute the corresponding free output trajectory $y_l(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}_l(t)$.

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. Characteristic polynomial:
   $$p(\lambda) = \det(\lambda \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}) = \det\begin{bmatrix} \lambda & -2 \\ 5/2 & \lambda + 2 \end{bmatrix} = \lambda(\lambda + 2) + 5 = \lambda^2 + 2\lambda + 5 = 0$$
   Eigenvalues: $\lambda_{1,2} = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 20}}{2} = -1 \pm j2$.
   Since $\operatorname{Re}(\lambda_{1,2}) = -1 < 0$, the system is **asymptotically stable** with underdamped oscillatory modes.
2. Resolvent matrix:
   $$(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1} = \frac{1}{s^2 + 2s + 5}\begin{bmatrix} s + 2 & 2 \\ -5/2 & s \end{bmatrix}$$
   Completing the square on the denominator: $(s+1)^2 + 2^2$.
   Taking inverse Laplace transforms entry-by-entry:
   * Entry $(1,1)$: $\frac{s+2}{(s+1)^2 + 4} = \frac{s+1}{(s+1)^2 + 4} + \frac{1}{2}\frac{2}{(s+1)^2 + 4} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{-1}} e^{-t}\cos(2t) + \frac{1}{2}e^{-t}\sin(2t)$
   * Entry $(1,2)$: $\frac{2}{(s+1)^2 + 4} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{-1}} e^{-t}\sin(2t)$
   * Entry $(2,1)$: $\frac{-5/2}{(s+1)^2 + 4} = -\frac{5}{4}\frac{2}{(s+1)^2 + 4} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{-1}} -\frac{5}{4}e^{-t}\sin(2t)$
   * Entry $(2,2)$: $\frac{s}{(s+1)^2 + 4} = \frac{s+1}{(s+1)^2 + 4} - \frac{1}{2}\frac{2}{(s+1)^2 + 4} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{-1}} e^{-t}\cos(2t) - \frac{1}{2}e^{-t}\sin(2t)$
   $$e^{\mathbf{A}t} = e^{-t}\begin{bmatrix} \cos(2t) + \frac{1}{2}\sin(2t) & \sin(2t) \\ -\frac{5}{4}\sin(2t) & \cos(2t) - \frac{1}{2}\sin(2t) \end{bmatrix}$$
3. Free state trajectory with $\mathbf{x}(0) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$:
   $$\mathbf{x}_l(t) = e^{\mathbf{A}t}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = e^{-t}\begin{bmatrix} \cos(2t) + \frac{3}{2}\sin(2t) \\ \cos(2t) - \frac{7}{4}\sin(2t) \end{bmatrix}$$
4. Free output response for $\mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$:
   $$y_l(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}_l(t) = 2 x_{l1}(t) = e^{-t}\left( 2\cos(2t) + 3\sin(2t) \right)$$

</details>

</Exercise>
