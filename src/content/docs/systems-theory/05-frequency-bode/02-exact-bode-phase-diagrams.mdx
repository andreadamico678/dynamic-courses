## Introduction

Knowing how much a system amplifies a sinusoid tells you only half of its frequency behavior: every physical energy storage element—whether a motor inductance, a mechanical mass, or an RC filter—also delays the output waveform in time relative to the input. In an open-loop audio filter, a phase shift merely delays a note by a few milliseconds; inside a closed negative-feedback loop, however, phase lag decides whether the system stabilizes or destroys itself.

If the total open-loop phase shift reaches $-180^\circ$ (half a cycle of delay) at a frequency where the loop gain $|L(j\omega)|$ is still $1$ ($0\text{ dB}$) or greater, the feedback signal arrives at the summing junction completely inverted. Subtracting an inverted signal adds it in phase, turning negative feedback into self-sustaining positive feedback. The **Bode phase diagram** tracks this phase evolution across decades of frequency and lets us read **phase margin** ($\varphi_m$) and **gain margin** ($K_{m,\text{dB}}$) directly off the open-loop curves.

### Phase Trajectory and Phase Margin of the Part V Loop

Consider our Part V running system placed inside the standard unity negative-feedback architecture from [Chapter 3](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/01-state-models/03-block-diagram-algebra-reduction/):

  <figcaption>Unity negative-feedback loop: Bode diagrams plot open-loop $L(s) = C(s)G(s)$ to test how close $1 + L(j\omega) = 0$ is to destabilizing $W_{\text{CL}}(s) = L(s)/(1 + L(s))$.</figcaption>
</figure>

Why do we plot the **open-loop transfer function** $L(s) = C(s)G(s)$ on a Bode diagram when our real goal is the stability of the **closed-loop transfer function** $W_{\text{CL}}(s) = \frac{L(s)}{1 + L(s)}$? Because the closed-loop poles are the solutions of $1 + L(s) = 0 \iff L(s) = -1$. In complex polar coordinates, the critical point $-1$ has magnitude $|-1| = 1$ ($0\text{ dB}$) and phase $\arg(-1) = -180^\circ$. Thus, checking how far $L(j\omega)$ stays away from $0\text{ dB}$ and $-180^\circ$ simultaneously on the open-loop Bode plot tells us directly how far the closed-loop poles sit from the imaginary axis.

Let us trace the open-loop phase $\varphi(\omega) = \arg L(j\omega)$ of our minimum-phase Part V system with $K = 6$:

$$L(s) = \frac{6(1 + s/4)}{s(1 + s/2)}$$

Substituting $s = j\omega$ gives the complex quotient $L(j\omega) = \frac{6(1 + j\omega/4)}{(j\omega)(1 + j\omega/2)}$. Because the angle of a product or quotient of complex numbers equals the sum of the numerator angles minus the sum of the denominator angles, we can write $\varphi(\omega)$ as the sum of three elementary phase angles:

$$\varphi(\omega) = \underbrace{\arg(6)}_{0^\circ} - \underbrace{\arg(j\omega)}_{90^\circ} - \underbrace{\arg(1 + j\omega/2)}_{\arctan(\omega/2)} + \underbrace{\arg(1 + j\omega/4)}_{\arctan(\omega/4)}$$

$$\varphi(\omega) = -90^\circ - \arctan\!\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right) + \arctan\!\left(\frac{\omega}{4}\right)$$

Follow how this phase curve evolves from low to high frequency:
1. **Low-Frequency Asymptote ($\omega \to 0^+$):** As $\omega \to 0^+$, $\arctan(0) = 0^\circ$ for both the pole at $\omega_p = 2$ and the zero at $\omega_z = 4$. Only the integrator $1/(j\omega) = -j/\omega$ contributes, holding the phase at a constant horizontal line $\varphi(0^+) = -90^\circ$.
2. **Pole Lag vs. Zero Lead ($0.2 \le \omega \le 40\text{ rad/s}$):**
   - At $\omega = 2\text{ rad/s}$ (the pole corner $\omega_p$), the pole lags by $-\arctan(2/2) = -45^\circ$, while the zero at $\omega_z = 4$ already leads by $+\arctan(2/4) = +\arctan(0.5) \approx +26.57^\circ$. Their sum is:
     $$\varphi(2) = -90^\circ - 45^\circ + 26.57^\circ = -108.43^\circ$$
   - At $\omega_{gc} \approx 3.84\text{ rad/s}$ (where $|L(j\,3.84)| = 1$, i.e., $0\text{ dB}$), the pole lags by $-\arctan(3.84/2) \approx -62.5^\circ$ and the zero leads by $+\arctan(3.84/4) \approx +43.8^\circ$, giving:
     $$\varphi(3.84) = -90^\circ - 62.5^\circ + 43.8^\circ = -108.7^\circ$$
     Since the critical instability boundary sits at $-180^\circ$, our safety buffer—the **phase margin** $\varphi_m$—is:
     $$\varphi_m = 180^\circ + \varphi(3.84) = 180^\circ - 108.7^\circ = +71.3^\circ > 0^\circ$$
3. **High-Frequency Asymptote ($\omega \to \infty$):** As $\omega \to \infty$, $\arctan(\omega/2) \to 90^\circ$ and $\arctan(\omega/4) \to 90^\circ$. The pole's $-90^\circ$ lag and the zero's $+90^\circ$ lead cancel completely, bringing the curve back to the horizontal asymptote $\varphi(\infty) = -90^\circ - 90^\circ + 90^\circ = -90^\circ$.

### Phase Addition Principle

Why do phases add when transfer functions multiply? Every complex factor $F_k(j\omega)$ can be written in Euler polar form as $F_k(j\omega) = |F_k(j\omega)|\,e^{j\arg F_k(j\omega)}$. When a transfer function $W(j\omega)$ consists of numerator factors divided by denominator factors, combining the complex exponentials adds the exponents in the numerator and subtracts the exponents in the denominator.

For any minimum-phase transfer function in Bode canonical form:

$$W(s) = \frac{K_B}{s^g} \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m_0} (1 + s\tau_{z,i}) \prod_{q} \left(1 + \frac{2\zeta_{z,q}}{\omega_{n,z,q}}s + \frac{s^2}{\omega_{n,z,q}^2}\right)}{\prod_{k=1}^{n_0} (1 + s\tau_{p,k}) \prod_{l} \left(1 + \frac{2\zeta_{p,l}}{\omega_{n,p,l}}s + \frac{s^2}{\omega_{n,p,l}^2}\right)}$$

evaluating at $s = j\omega$ gives the exact total phase at frequency $\omega$:

$$\boxed{\arg(W(j\omega)) = \arg(K_B) - g \cdot 90^\circ + \sum \arg(\text{numerator factors}) - \sum \arg(\text{denominator factors})}$$

Notice the two constant baseline terms at the front:
- **Sign of Bode Gain ($\arg K_B$):** If $K_B > 0$, $\arg(K_B) = 0^\circ$. If $K_B < 0$ (an inverting amplifier or reversed actuator polarity), $\arg(K_B) = -180^\circ$ across all frequencies.
- **Origin Poles/Zeros ($s^{-g}$):** Because $j\omega = \omega\,e^{j90^\circ}$, each integrator pole $1/(j\omega)$ contributes a constant $-90^\circ$ at every frequency, while each differentiator zero $j\omega$ contributes $+90^\circ$. Together, $1/(j\omega)^g$ shifts the entire phase diagram vertically by $-g \cdot 90^\circ$.

### 3-Segment Phase Ramps

Consider a single stable first-order real pole $(1 + s\tau_p)^{-1} = (1 + s/\omega_c)^{-1}$ with $\tau_p = 1/\omega_c > 0$. At $s = j\omega$, the denominator $1 + j(\omega/\omega_c)$ has real part $1 > 0$ and imaginary part $+\omega/\omega_c > 0$, so its exact phase contribution is:

$$\varphi_p(\omega) = -\arg\!\left(1 + j\frac{\omega}{\omega_c}\right) = -\arctan\!\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_c}\right)$$

Unlike magnitude—which stays close to $0\text{ dB}$ all the way up to $\omega_c$ and then breaks sharply at a single point—the arctangent function transitions gradually over **two full decades** centered on $\omega_c$:
- **One decade below the corner ($\omega = 0.1\omega_c$):** $\varphi_p(0.1\omega_c) = -\arctan(0.1) \approx -5.71^\circ$ (nearly $0^\circ$).
- **At the corner frequency ($\omega = \omega_c$):** $\varphi_p(\omega_c) = -\arctan(1) = -45.00^\circ$ (exact midpoint of the transition).
- **One decade above the corner ($\omega = 10\omega_c$):** $\varphi_p(10\omega_c) = -\arctan(10) \approx -84.29^\circ$ (within $5.71^\circ$ of its $-90^\circ$ limit).

To draw this smooth S-curve with a ruler on semi-log paper, Bode replaced $-\arctan(\omega/\omega_c)$ with a **three-segment piecewise-linear approximation**:
1. A flat horizontal line at **$0^\circ$** for all $\omega \le 0.1\omega_c$.
2. A straight **inclined ramp of slope $-45^\circ/\text{decade}$** spanning the two decades $[0.1\omega_c,\, 10\omega_c]$, passing through $(\omega_c, -45^\circ)$ with **zero error** and dropping by $-45^\circ \times 2\text{ decades} = -90^\circ$ across the interval.
3. A flat horizontal line at **$-90^\circ$** for all $\omega \ge 10\omega_c$.

The maximum discrepancy between the 3-segment ramp and the exact $-\arctan(\omega/\omega_c)$ curve occurs right at the two ramp endpoints $0.1\omega_c$ and $10\omega_c$, where it equals $\arctan(0.1) \approx 5.71^\circ$. For a stable real LHP zero $(1 + s/\omega_c)$, the exact curve $+\arctan(\omega/\omega_c)$ and the 3-segment ramp simply flip sign to **$+45^\circ/\text{decade}$** across $[0.1\omega_c, 10\omega_c]$, rising from $0^\circ$ to $+90^\circ$.

### Second-Order Phase Steepness

For a stable complex conjugate pole pair $F_{p2}(s) = \left(1 + \frac{2\zeta}{\omega_n}s + \frac{s^2}{\omega_n^2}\right)^{-1}$ with $\zeta > 0$, substituting $s = j\omega$ gives denominator $\left(1 - \frac{\omega^2}{\omega_n^2}\right) + j\left(2\zeta\frac{\omega}{\omega_n}\right)$. Notice how the real part changes sign as $\omega$ crosses $\omega_n$:
- For $\omega < \omega_n$, the real part $1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2 > 0$ is positive (first quadrant), so the denominator angle lies between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$.
- At $\omega = \omega_n$, the real part vanishes ($1 - 1 = 0$) and the denominator is purely imaginary $+j2\zeta$, so the exact phase is **always $-90^\circ$ regardless of $\zeta$**.
- For $\omega > \omega_n$, the real part $1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2 < 0$ becomes negative (second quadrant), so the phase continues dropping from $-90^\circ$ toward $-180^\circ$.

Using the four-quadrant arctangent $\operatorname{atan2}(y, x)$, the exact phase across all frequencies is:

$$\varphi_{p2}(\omega) = -\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(2\zeta\frac{\omega}{\omega_n},\; 1 - \frac{\omega^2}{\omega_n^2}\right)$$

On an asymptotic sketch, because two poles break at $\omega_n$, the 3-segment ramp spans $[0.1\omega_n, 10\omega_n]$ with twice the first-order slope: **$-90^\circ/\text{decade}$**, dropping from $0^\circ$ to $-180^\circ$ and passing through $(\omega_n, -90^\circ)$. However, the *true* steepness of the S-curve at $\omega = \omega_n$ depends on $\zeta$. Differentiating $\varphi_{p2}$ with respect to normalized frequency $v = \omega/\omega_n$ via $\frac{d}{dv}\arctan\!\left(\frac{2\zeta v}{1 - v^2}\right)$ gives:

$$\frac{d\varphi_{p2}}{dv} = -\frac{2\zeta(1 - v^2) - (2\zeta v)(-2v)}{(1 - v^2)^2 + (2\zeta v)^2} = -\frac{2\zeta(1 + v^2)}{(1 - v^2)^2 + 4\zeta^2 v^2}$$

Evaluating at the corner $v = 1$ ($\omega = \omega_n$) yields:

$$\left.\frac{d\varphi_{p2}}{d(\omega/\omega_n)}\right|_{\omega = \omega_n} = -\frac{2\zeta(2)}{0 + 4\zeta^2} = -\frac{1}{\zeta}\;\text{rad}$$

As $\zeta \to 0^+$, the slope $-1/\zeta \to -\infty$: a lightly damped resonance flips almost discontinuously from $0^\circ$ to $-180^\circ$ right across $\omega_n$.

### Low- and High-Frequency Phase Limits

Before and after sketching any phase diagram, two exact horizontal boundary checks catch almost every arithmetic mistake:
1. **Low-Frequency Limit ($\omega \to 0^+$):** Every factor $(1 + j\omega\tau)$ approaches $1 = 1\,e^{j0^\circ}$, leaving only the gain sign and origin integrators:
   $$\boxed{\varphi(0^+) = \lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \arg W(j\omega) = \arg(K_B) - g \cdot 90^\circ}$$
2. **High-Frequency Limit ($\omega \to \infty$):** As $\omega \to \infty$, every minimum-phase pole contributes $-90^\circ$ ($-180^\circ$ for a complex pole pair) and every minimum-phase zero contributes $+90^\circ$ ($+180^\circ$ for a complex zero pair). If $W(s)$ has $n$ total poles (including $g$ at the origin) and $m$ total finite zeros, the high-frequency phase settles onto the horizontal **pole-zero excess** asymptote:
   $$\boxed{\varphi(\infty) = \lim_{\omega \to \infty} \arg W(j\omega) = \arg(K_B) - (n - m)\cdot 90^\circ}$$

### Stability Margins on the Bode Plot

In a unity negative-feedback loop $W_{\text{CL}}(s) = \frac{L(s)}{1 + L(s)}$ where the open-loop transfer function $L(s)$ has no RHP poles (and decreases monotonically in gain/phase near crossover), the closed loop is asymptotically stable if and only if at the frequency where $|L(j\omega)| = 1$ ($0\text{ dB}$), the open-loop phase $\arg L(j\omega)$ has not yet dropped to $-180^\circ$. We quantify the safety buffer against the critical point $L(j\omega) = -1$ ($0\text{ dB}, -180^\circ$) with two margins:

1. **Gain Crossover Frequency ($\omega_{gc}$) & Phase Margin ($\varphi_m$):**
   The **gain crossover frequency** $\omega_{gc}$ (also denoted $\omega_c$) is the frequency where open-loop magnitude crosses unity gain:
   $$|L(j\omega_{gc})| = 1 \iff |L(j\omega_{gc})|_{\text{dB}} = 0\text{ dB}$$
   The **phase margin** $\varphi_m$ is the angular distance in degrees from the open-loop phase $\arg L(j\omega_{gc})$ down to the $-180^\circ$ instability line:
   $$\boxed{\varphi_m = 180^\circ + \arg L(j\omega_{gc})}$$
   If $\varphi_m > 0^\circ$ (for instance $\arg L(j\omega_{gc}) = -125^\circ \implies \varphi_m = +55^\circ$), the closed loop is stable and can tolerate up to $\varphi_m$ degrees of additional phase lag at $\omega_{gc}$ before oscillating. In practical control design, engineers require $\varphi_m \ge 45^\circ\text{–}60^\circ$ to prevent lightly damped ringing in the closed-loop step response.
2. **Phase Crossover Frequency ($\omega_\pi$) & Gain Margin ($K_{m,\text{dB}}$):**
   The **phase crossover frequency** $\omega_\pi$ is the frequency where open-loop phase crosses $-180^\circ$:
   $$\arg L(j\omega_\pi) = -180^\circ$$
   The **gain margin** $K_m = \frac{1}{|L(j\omega_\pi)|}$ (or in decibels, $K_{m,\text{dB}}$) measures how many decibels below $0\text{ dB}$ the magnitude curve sits when phase hits $-180^\circ$:
   $$\boxed{K_{m,\text{dB}} = 20\log_{10} K_m = -|L(j\omega_\pi)|_{\text{dB}}}$$
   If $|L(j\omega_\pi)|_{\text{dB}} = -12\text{ dB}$, then $K_{m,\text{dB}} = +12\text{ dB} > 0\text{ dB}$ ($K_m \approx 4$), meaning loop gain $K$ can be multiplied by a factor of $4$ before the closed loop goes unstable.

### 4-Step Phase Procedure

1. **Sort corners & choose window:** Order $\omega_{c,1} < \dots < \omega_{c,r}$ and set $\omega_{\text{start}} \le 0.1\omega_{c,1}$, $\omega_{\text{end}} \ge 10\omega_{c,r}$.
2. **Low-frequency limit:** Start horizontally at $\varphi(0^+) = \arg(K_B) - g \cdot 90^\circ$.
3. **Trace ramps or exact points:** Sum exact $\arctan$ contributions at key frequencies or walk ramp breakpoints $0.1\omega_{c,k}$ and $10\omega_{c,k}$ via:
   $$\boxed{\varphi(\omega_{m+1}) = \varphi(\omega_m) + S_{\varphi,\text{current}}\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\omega_{m+1}}{\omega_m}\right)}$$
4. **Check high-frequency limit & margins:** Verify the plot ends at $\varphi(\infty) = \arg(K_B) - (n - m)\cdot 90^\circ$, then read $\varphi_m = 180^\circ + \arg L(j\omega_{gc})$ and $K_{m,\text{dB}} = -|L(j\omega_\pi)|_{\text{dB}}$.

### Key Proofs

- **3-segment ramp accuracy:** Show that $-\arctan(\omega/\omega_c)$ equals $-45^\circ$ at $\omega = \omega_c$ (zero error) and differs from the $[0.1\omega_c, 10\omega_c]$ linear ramp by at most $\arctan(0.1) \approx 5.71^\circ$ at the endpoints.
- **Second-order corner slope:** Differentiate $-\arctan\!\left(\frac{2\zeta v}{1-v^2}\right)$ with respect to $v = \omega/\omega_n$ at $v = 1$ to show $\left.\frac{d\varphi}{dv}\right|_{v=1} = -\frac{1}{\zeta}$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistakes">
1. **Forgetting the $-90^\circ$ Integrator Offset:** When $g = 1$, the entire phase curve starts at $-90^\circ$, not $0^\circ$.
2. **Reading Ramp Endpoints Wrong:** A real factor shifts phase across two full decades $[0.1\omega_c, 10\omega_c]$ (starting a decade *before* $\omega_c$), not from $\omega_c$ onward.
</Callout>

<Callout type="bridge" title="Robotics Note: Phase Margin Erosion in Drone Loops">
In $1000\text{ Hz}$ autopilot loops with $T_d = 2\text{ ms}$ computational delay, latency subtracts $\omega T_d (180^\circ/\pi)$; at $\omega_{gc} = 250\text{ rad/s}$, that costs $28.6^\circ$ of phase margin.
</Callout>

A unity-feedback loop has the third-order open-loop transfer function:
$$L(s) = \frac{5}{s(1 + s/2)(1 + s/10)}$$
1. Using $\arctan(a) + \arctan(b) = 90^\circ \iff a\,b = 1$, find the exact phase crossover frequency $\omega_\pi$ where $\angle L(j\omega_\pi) = -180^\circ$.
2. Evaluate $|L(j\omega_\pi)|$ and compute the gain margin $K_{m,\text{dB}}$ in decibels.

1. **Phase crossover $\omega_\pi$:** Setting $\angle L(j\omega_\pi) = -90^\circ - \arctan(\omega_\pi/2) - \arctan(\omega_\pi/10) = -180^\circ$ requires:
   $$\arctan\!\left(\frac{\omega_\pi}{2}\right) + \arctan\!\left(\frac{\omega_\pi}{10}\right) = 90^\circ \implies \frac{\omega_\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{\omega_\pi}{10} = 1 \implies \omega_\pi = \sqrt{20} \approx 4.472\text{ rad/s}$$
2. **Gain margin:** At $\omega_\pi^2 = 20$:
   $$|L(j\sqrt{20})| = \frac{5}{\sqrt{20}\sqrt{1 + 20/4}\sqrt{1 + 20/100}} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{20 \cdot 6 \cdot 1.2}} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{144}} = \frac{5}{12}$$
   Therefore $K_m = \frac{1}{|L(j\omega_\pi)|} = \frac{12}{5} = 2.4$ ($K_{m,\text{dB}} = 20\log_{10}(2.4) \approx +7.60\text{ dB} > 0\text{ dB}$).
