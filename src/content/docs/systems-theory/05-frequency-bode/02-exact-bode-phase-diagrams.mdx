## Intuition and Physical Meaning

While the Bode magnitude plot measures how strongly a system amplifies or attenuates a sinusoidal input at frequency $\omega$, the **Bode phase diagram** $\varphi(\omega) = \arg(W(j\omega))$ measures the **time shift (phase lag or lead)** between the input and steady-state output sinusoids. In negative-feedback control loops, excessive phase lag is dangerous: when the open-loop phase reaches $-180^\circ$, negative feedback turns into *positive* feedback, amplifying oscillations and potentially destabilizing the system.

Just like magnitude diagrams, phase diagrams use a logarithmic horizontal axis ($\log_{10}\omega$) and separate the ruler-drawn **Asymptotic Plot** (piecewise-linear ramps) from the smooth **Exact Response** ($\arctan$ curves).

## Formal Definition and Derivation (Oral Core)

For any minimum-phase rational transfer function in Bode canonical form:
$$W(s) = \frac{K_B}{s^g} \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m_0} (1 + s\tau_{z,i}) \prod_{q} \left(1 + \frac{2\zeta_{z,q}}{\omega_{n,z,q}}s + \frac{s^2}{\omega_{n,z,q}^2}\right)}{\prod_{k=1}^{n_0} (1 + s\tau_{p,k}) \prod_{l} \left(1 + \frac{2\zeta_{p,l}}{\omega_{n,p,l}}s + \frac{s^2}{\omega_{n,p,l}^2}\right)}$$
evaluating at $s = j\omega$ and taking the complex argument converts multiplication into addition and division into subtraction:
$$\boxed{\arg(W(j\omega)) = \arg(K_B) - g \cdot 90^\circ + \sum \arg(\text{numerator factors}) - \sum \arg(\text{denominator factors})}$$

### Derivation / Oral Proof: The 3-Segment Ramp and Second-Order Steepness

For a stable first-order pole $(1 + j\omega/\omega_c)^{-1}$ ($\tau_p = 1/\omega_c > 0$), the exact phase contribution is $\varphi_p(\omega) = -\arctan(\omega/\omega_c)$:
* At $\omega = 0.1\omega_c$: $-\arctan(0.1) \approx -5.71^\circ$.
* At $\omega = \omega_c$: $-\arctan(1) = -45^\circ$.
* At $\omega = 10\omega_c$: $-\arctan(10) \approx -84.29^\circ$.

Approximating this smooth S-curve by $0^\circ$ for $\omega \le 0.1\omega_c$, a straight ramp of $-45^\circ/\text{dec}$ across $[0.1\omega_c, 10\omega_c]$, and $-90^\circ$ for $\omega \ge 10\omega_c$ gives **zero error at $\omega_c$** and a maximum discrepancy of **$5.71^\circ$** at the ramp endpoints $0.1\omega_c$ and $10\omega_c$.

For a stable complex conjugate pole pair with $\zeta > 0$, $\varphi_{p2}(\omega) = -\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(2\zeta\frac{\omega}{\omega_n},\; 1 - \frac{\omega^2}{\omega_n^2}\right)$ passes through $-90^\circ$ at $\omega = \omega_n$ regardless of $\zeta$, with local slope $\left.\frac{d\varphi}{d(\omega/\omega_n)}\right|_{\omega_n} = -\frac{1}{\zeta}$ (lightly damped pairs drop steeply across $\omega_n$, whereas heavily damped pairs spread out smoothly).

## 4-Step Phase Construction Algorithm (Written Exam Core)

All operational factor phase rules—both exact trigonometric formulas and 3-segment asymptotic ramps—are consolidated into a single reference in the reference table.

| Factor in Bode Form | Exact Phase $\arg(\cdot)$ | Low-Freq ($\omega \ll \omega_c$) | Ramp Interval & Slope | High-Freq ($\omega \gg \omega_c$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Constant gain $K_B$ | $0^\circ$ ($K_B > 0$), $-180^\circ$ ($K_B < 0$) | Constant | None ($0^\circ/\text{dec}$) | Constant |
| Origin poles/zeros $s^{-g}$ | $-g \cdot 90^\circ$ | Constant | None ($0^\circ/\text{dec}$) | Constant |
| Real pole $(1 + s\tau_p)^{-1}$ | $-\arctan(\omega\tau_p)$ | $0^\circ$ | $[0.1\omega_c, 10\omega_c]$: $-45^\circ/\text{dec}$ | $-90^\circ$ |
| Real zero $(1 + s\tau_z)$ | $+\arctan(\omega\tau_z)$ | $0^\circ$ | $[0.1\omega_c, 10\omega_c]$: $+45^\circ/\text{dec}$ | $+90^\circ$ |
| Complex poles ($\omega_n, \zeta > 0$) | $-\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(2\zeta\frac{\omega}{\omega_n}, 1-\frac{\omega^2}{\omega_n^2}\right)$ | $0^\circ$ | $[0.1\omega_n, 10\omega_n]$: $-90^\circ/\text{dec}$ | $-180^\circ$ |
| Complex zeros ($\omega_n, \zeta > 0$) | $+\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(2\zeta\frac{\omega}{\omega_n}, 1-\frac{\omega^2}{\omega_n^2}\right)$ | $0^\circ$ | $[0.1\omega_n, 10\omega_n]$: $+90^\circ/\text{dec}$ | $+180^\circ$ |

*Table: Single-source operational exact phase formulas and 3-segment asymptotic ramps for minimum-phase factors ($\tau > 0$).*

### Step 1: Order Corner Frequencies and Pick $[\omega_{\text{start}}, \omega_{\text{end}}]$

Sort all corner frequencies $\omega_c = 1/\tau$ and natural frequencies $\omega_n$ in ascending order:
$$\omega_{c,1} < \omega_{c,2} < \dots < \omega_{c,r}$$
Because every phase ramp spans $[0.1\omega_{c,k}, 10\omega_{c,k}]$, choose a finite plotting window $[\omega_{\text{start}}, \omega_{\text{end}}]$ extending at least one decade past the outermost corners:
$$\omega_{\text{start}} \le 0.1\,\omega_{c,1}, \qquad \omega_{\text{end}} \ge 10\,\omega_{c,r}$$

### Step 2: Compute the Low-Frequency Horizontal Limit $\varphi(0^+)$

For $\omega \le \omega_{\text{start}} \le 0.1\omega_{c,1}$, every non-origin factor contributes $0^\circ$. Place your starting dot at $(\omega_{\text{start}},\, \varphi(0^+))$ on the initial horizontal line:
$$\boxed{\varphi(0^+) = \arg(K_B) - g \cdot 90^\circ}$$
where $\arg(K_B) = 0^\circ$ if $K_B > 0$ and $-180^\circ$ if $K_B < 0$.

### Step 3: Evaluate Exact Points or Sum the 3-Segment Ramps

Depending on whether the exam asks for an **exact curve** or a **piecewise-linear asymptotic sketch**:
* **Exact Phase Curve (Point-by-Point):** Evaluate the exact sum at key frequencies ($\omega_{\text{start}}$, each corner $\omega_{c,k}$, gain crossover $\omega_c$, and $\omega_{\text{end}}$):
  $$\varphi(\omega) = \varphi(0^+) + \sum_{i} \arctan(\omega\tau_{z,i}) - \sum_{k} \arctan(\omega\tau_{p,k}) - \sum_{l} \operatorname{atan2}\!\left(2\zeta_{p,l}\frac{\omega}{\omega_{n,p,l}},\, 1 - \frac{\omega^2}{\omega_{n,p,l}^2}\right) + \dots$$
  and connect those dots with a smooth S-shaped curve.
* **Asymptotic Phase Plot (Connect-the-Dots Ramps):** List all ramp transitions $0.1\omega_{c,k}$ (add factor slope from the reference table) and $10\omega_{c,k}$ (subtract factor slope) in ascending order $\omega_1 < \omega_2 < \dots < \omega_q$. Starting from $(\omega_{\text{start}}, \varphi(0^+))$, walk vertex-to-vertex using:
  $$\boxed{\varphi(\omega_{m+1}) = \varphi(\omega_m) + S_{\varphi,\text{current}} \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{\omega_{m+1}}{\omega_m}\right)}$$
  and connect consecutive vertices with straight line segments ([the figure](#fig-bode-phase)).

### Step 4: Verify the High-Frequency Horizontal Limit $\varphi(\infty)$

For $\omega \ge \omega_{\text{end}} \ge 10\omega_{c,r}$, every factor has saturated at its final horizontal value. Verify that your plot lands on the exact pole-zero excess asymptote:
$$\boxed{\varphi(\infty) = \arg(K_B) - (n - m)\cdot 90^\circ}$$
where $n$ is the total number of poles (including $g$ at the origin) and $m$ is the total number of zeros of $W(s)$.

## Stability Margins on the Bode Diagram

For a unity negative-feedback loop $W_{CL}(s) = \frac{L(s)}{1 + L(s)}$:
* **Gain Crossover Frequency $\omega_c$:** $|L(j\omega_c)|_{\text{dB}} = 0\text{ dB}$. The **phase margin** is:
  $$\boxed{\varphi_m = 180^\circ + \arg(L(j\omega_c))}$$
* **Phase Crossover Frequency $\omega_\pi$:** $\arg(L(j\omega_\pi)) = -180^\circ$. The **gain margin** in decibels is:
  $$\boxed{K_{m,\text{dB}} = -|L(j\omega_\pi)|_{\text{dB}}}$$

## Worked Exam Archetype and Pitfalls

### Worked Example: 4-Step Phase Construction and Margin Evaluation

Construct the phase diagram and compute the phase margin for $W(s) = \frac{10(1 + 0.2s)}{s(1 + 0.5s)(1 + 0.02s)}$ from [Bode Magnitude Diagrams](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/05-frequency-bode/01-asymptotic-bode-magnitude-diagrams/) ($g = 1$, $K_B = 10 > 0$):

1. **Order Corners & Pick Window:**
   $\omega_{p1} = 2\text{ rad/s}$ ($\tau_{p1} = 0.5$), $\omega_{z1} = 5\text{ rad/s}$ ($\tau_{z1} = 0.2$), $\omega_{p2} = 50\text{ rad/s}$ ($\tau_{p2} = 0.02$).
   Ramp endpoints are $0.2, 0.5, 5, 20, 50, 500\text{ rad/s}$, so pick $[\omega_{\text{start}}, \omega_{\text{end}}] = [0.1, 1000]\text{ rad/s}$.
2. **Low-Frequency Horizontal Limit:**
   $$\varphi(0^+) = 0^\circ - 1 \cdot 90^\circ = -90^\circ$$
3. **Evaluate Exact Phase & Ramp Vertices:**
   * Exact formula: $\varphi(\omega) = -90^\circ + \arctan(0.2\omega) - \arctan(0.5\omega) - \arctan(0.02\omega)$.
   * At the asymptotic gain crossover $\omega_c \approx 4.47\text{ rad/s}$:
     $$\varphi(4.47) = -90^\circ + \arctan(0.894) - \arctan(2.235) - \arctan(0.0894) \approx -90^\circ + 41.8^\circ - 65.9^\circ - 5.1^\circ = -119.2^\circ$$
     $$\varphi_m = 180^\circ + (-119.2^\circ) = +60.8^\circ > 0^\circ$$
   * Ramp slopes: $0$ on $[0.1, 0.2]$; $-45^\circ/\text{dec}$ on $[0.2, 0.5]$; $0^\circ/\text{dec}$ on $[0.5, 5]$; $-45^\circ/\text{dec}$ on $[5, 20]$; $0^\circ/\text{dec}$ on $[20, 50]$; $-45^\circ/\text{dec}$ on $[50, 500]$; $0^\circ/\text{dec}$ on $[500, 1000]$.
4. **Verify High-Frequency Horizontal Limit:**
   $W(s)$ has $n = 3$ poles and $m = 1$ zero, so:
   $$\varphi(\infty) = 0^\circ - (3 - 1)\cdot 90^\circ = -180^\circ$$
   which matches the cumulative ramp change $-90^\circ - 45^\circ\log_{10}(0.5/0.2) - 45^\circ\log_{10}(20/5) - 45^\circ\log_{10}(500/50) = -90^\circ - 90^\circ = -180^\circ$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Exam Pitfall: Forgetting the $-90^\circ$ Integrator Offset">

Students often start the phase plot at $0^\circ$ when $g = 1$. Remember that every integrator $1/s^g$ shifts the **entire** phase curve down by $g \cdot 90^\circ$ starting at $\omega_{\text{start}}$.

</Callout>

<Callout type="bridge" title="SDU Robotics Bridge: Phase Margin Erosion in Drone Attitude Loops">

In PX4 autopilot firmware ($f = 1000\text{ Hz}$), computational transport delay $T_d = 2\text{ ms}$ adds linear phase lag $\Delta\varphi(\omega) = -\omega T_d \frac{180^\circ}{\pi}$. At $\omega_c = 250\text{ rad/s}$, this erodes $\varphi_m$ by $28.6^\circ$, turning a healthy $45^\circ$ margin into $16.4^\circ$ and causing high-frequency oscillations.

</Callout>

<Exercise client:load id="ex-18-1" title="Exercise 18.1" level="LEVEL 1: WARM-UP DRILL">

Given the first-order lag system with an integrator:
$$W(s) = \frac{10}{s (1 + 0.1s)}$$
1. Determine the low-frequency phase limit $\lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \arg(W(j\omega))$ and the high-frequency limit $\lim_{\omega \to \infty} \arg(W(j\omega))$.
2. Formulate the exact analytical phase expression $\arg(W(j\omega))$ in degrees.
3. Compute the exact phase angle at the corner frequency $\omega_p = 10\text{ rad/s}$, as well as at $\omega = 1\text{ rad/s}$ and $\omega = 100\text{ rad/s}$ (one decade below and above).
4. Contrast the exact phase at $\omega = 10\text{ rad/s}$ with the piecewise linear asymptotic approximation.

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. Frequency limits:
   * Low-frequency limit:
     $$\lim_{\omega \to 0^+} \arg(W(j\omega)) = -90^\circ$$
     (established entirely by the type $g = 1$ pole at the origin).
   * High-frequency limit:
     Pole-zero excess is $n - m = 2 - 0 = 2$.
     $$\lim_{\omega \to \infty} \arg(W(j\omega)) = -(n - m) \cdot 90^\circ = -2 \cdot 90^\circ = -180^\circ$$
2. Analytical phase expression:
   $$\arg(W(j\omega)) = -90^\circ - \arctan(0.1\omega)$$
3. Numerical phase evaluation:
   * $\omega = 1\text{ rad/s}$:
     $$\arg(W(j1)) = -90^\circ - \arctan(0.1) = -90^\circ - 5.71^\circ = -95.71^\circ$$
   * $\omega = 10\text{ rad/s}$ (corner frequency $\omega_p = 10$):
     $$\arg(W(j10)) = -90^\circ - \arctan(1.0) = -90^\circ - 45.00^\circ = -135.00^\circ$$
   * $\omega = 100\text{ rad/s}$:
     $$\arg(W(j100)) = -90^\circ - \arctan(10.0) = -90^\circ - 84.29^\circ = -174.29^\circ$$
4. Asymptotic comparison:
   Under the standard 3-segment piecewise linear Bode phase rule, a real LHP pole contributes $0^\circ$ at $0.1\omega_p$, $-45^\circ$ at $\omega_p$, and $-90^\circ$ at $10\omega_p$.
   At $\omega = 10\text{ rad/s}$, the asymptotic phase is $-90^\circ - 45^\circ = -135^\circ$, which matches the exact analytical phase $-135.00^\circ$ with zero error.

</details>

</Exercise>

Consider the open-loop transfer function analyzed in [Bode Magnitude Diagrams](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/05-frequency-bode/01-asymptotic-bode-magnitude-diagrams/):

2. Evaluate the theoretical asymptotic limits $\varphi(0^+)$ and $\varphi(\infty)$ using the pole-zero excess $n - m$.

4. Using the gain crossover frequency from [Bode Magnitude Diagrams](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/05-frequency-bode/01-asymptotic-bode-magnitude-diagrams/) ($\omega_c \approx 4.47\text{ rad/s}$), compute the exact phase $\varphi(\omega_c)$ and determine the phase margin $\varphi_m = 180^\circ + \varphi(\omega_c)$. Conclude whether unit negative feedback yields a stable closed loop.

<StepCheck client:load prompt="Compute the phase margin φ_m = 180° + φ(4.47):" expected={60.8} tolerance={1.0} unit="deg" explanation="φ(4.47) ≈ −119.2° ⇒ φ_m ≈ +60.8°." />


<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. Exact analytical phase expression:
   $$\varphi(\omega) = \arg(W(j\omega)) = -90^\circ + \arctan(0.2\omega) - \arctan(0.5\omega) - \arctan(0.02\omega)$$
2. Asymptotic limits:
   * For $\omega \to 0^+$: $\varphi(0^+) = -90^\circ$ (due to the single integrator).
   * For $\omega \to \infty$: with $n = 3$ poles and $m = 1$ zero, pole excess is $n - m = 2$.
     $$\varphi(\infty) = -90^\circ + 90^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ = -180^\circ$$
3. Exact phase at corner frequencies:
   * Corner frequency $\omega = 2\text{ rad/s}$ ($\tau = 0.5\text{ s}$ pole):
     $$\begin{aligned}
     \varphi(2) &= -90^\circ + \arctan(0.4) - \arctan(1.0) - \arctan(0.04) \\
     &= -90^\circ + 21.80^\circ - 45.00^\circ - 2.29^\circ = -115.49^\circ
     \end{aligned}$$
   * Corner frequency $\omega = 5\text{ rad/s}$ ($\tau = 0.2\text{ s}$ zero):
     $$\begin{aligned}
     \varphi(5) &= -90^\circ + \arctan(1.0) - \arctan(2.5) - \arctan(0.1) \\
     &= -90^\circ + 45.00^\circ - 68.20^\circ - 5.71^\circ = -118.91^\circ
     \end{aligned}$$
   * Corner frequency $\omega = 50\text{ rad/s}$ ($\tau = 0.02\text{ s}$ pole):
     $$\begin{aligned}
     \varphi(50) &= -90^\circ + \arctan(10.0) - \arctan(25.0) - \arctan(1.0) \\
     &= -90^\circ + 84.29^\circ - 87.71^\circ - 45.00^\circ = -138.42^\circ
     \end{aligned}$$
4. Phase margin at gain crossover frequency $\omega_c \approx 4.47\text{ rad/s}$:
   Evaluating the phase at $\omega_c = 4.47$:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   \varphi(4.47) &= -90^\circ + \arctan(0.2 \times 4.47) - \arctan(0.5 \times 4.47) - \arctan(0.02 \times 4.47) \\
   &= -90^\circ + \arctan(0.894) - \arctan(2.235) - \arctan(0.0894) \\
   &= -90^\circ + 41.80^\circ - 65.89^\circ - 5.11^\circ = -119.20^\circ
   \end{aligned}$$
   Phase margin:
   $$\varphi_m = 180^\circ + \varphi(\omega_c) = 180^\circ - 119.20^\circ = +60.80^\circ$$
   *Conclusion:* Since $\varphi_m = 60.80^\circ > 0^\circ$ (and comfortably exceeds the standard industrial robustness threshold of $45^\circ$), unit negative feedback produces a well-damped, asymptotically stable closed-loop system.

</details>
