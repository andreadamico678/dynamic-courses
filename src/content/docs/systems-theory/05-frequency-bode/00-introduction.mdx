## Introduction

Part V shifts analysis onto the imaginary axis $s = j\omega$ to quantify **sinusoidal steady-state response**. Excite any BIBO-stable LTI system with a pure sinusoid of frequency $\omega$, and the steady-state output oscillates at the exact same frequency—scaled in amplitude by $\lvert W(j\omega) \rvert$ and shifted in phase by $\arg W(j\omega)$.

Plotting gain in decibels and phase in degrees on a logarithmic frequency axis turns high-order polynomial products into modular straight-line building blocks. **Bode diagrams** directly measure closed-loop stability reserves—**gain margin** $K_m$ and **phase margin** $\varphi_m$—and expose fundamental bandwidth limits imposed by right-half-plane zeros and pure time delays.

### Running Example

Throughout Part V, we analyze the same unity-feedback open-loop plant from successive angles:

$$L(s) = K \frac{1 \pm s/4}{s(1 + s/2)}, \qquad W_{\text{CL}}(s) = \frac{L(s)}{1 + L(s)}$$

Chapter 17 sketches its piecewise-linear asymptotic magnitude $\lvert L(j\omega) \rvert_{\text{dB}}$, Chapter 18 adds phase $\arg L(j\omega)$ to evaluate phase and gain margins, and Chapter 19 flips the numerator sign to quantify how an RHP zero erodes phase margin and induces step undershoot.

### Real-World Applications

- **Structural Notch Filtering:** Identifying resonant magnitude spikes $M_r$ at $\omega_r$ on Bode plots lets engineers place digital notch filters without collapsing phase margin at crossover.
- **Bandwidth & Delay Limits:** Right-half-plane zeros and network transport delays $e^{-sT_d}$ add continuous phase lag that bounds maximum achievable closed-loop crossover frequency.
