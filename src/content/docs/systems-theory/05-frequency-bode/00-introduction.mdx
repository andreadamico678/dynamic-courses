## Introduction

Up to this point, you have viewed systems through differential equations in time $t$ and algebraic matrices in $s$. Part V introduces the engineer's favorite experimental and design lens: **frequency response**. When you excite a stable system with a steady sinusoid of frequency $\omega$, it responds with a sinusoid of the exact same frequency—only scaled in amplitude by $|W(j\omega)|$ and delayed in phase by $\arg(W(j\omega))$.

Plotting gain in decibels and phase in degrees on a logarithmic frequency axis turns complicated polynomial products into straight, modular building blocks. Better yet, **Bode diagrams** directly expose how close a feedback loop sits to instability (**gain margin** and **phase margin**) and reveal hard physical speed limits caused by right-half-plane zeros and time delays.

### Practical Real-World Perspective

- **Drone Structural Filtering & Bandwidth:** Lightweight carbon-fiber quadrotor frames resonate strongly at high frequencies ($\omega_r$), amplifying propeller vibrations into IMU sensors. Reading a Bode magnitude plot shows immediately where the resonant peak lives so you can place a digital notch filter without wrecking phase margin at the crossover frequency $\omega_c$.
- **Steering & Aircraft Delays (Non-Minimum Phase):** To turn a bicycle or aircraft right, you first briefly counter-steer or drop a control surface, creating an initial dip in the wrong direction (**undershoot**). Network packet latency in teleoperation adds $e^{-sT_d}$ phase lag that caps maximum safe closed-loop bandwidth.

### Shared Running Example

Throughout Part V, we analyze the same unity-feedback loop from successive angles:

$$L(s) = K \frac{1 \pm s/4}{s(1 + s/2)}, \qquad W_{\text{CL}}(s) = \frac{L(s)}{1 + L(s)}$$

**Chapter 17** sketches its asymptotic magnitude $|L(j\omega)|$, **Chapter 18** adds phase $\angle L(j\omega)$ to measure stability margins, and **Chapter 19** flips the sign of $s/4$ to show how a single right-half-plane zero changes closed-loop stability.
