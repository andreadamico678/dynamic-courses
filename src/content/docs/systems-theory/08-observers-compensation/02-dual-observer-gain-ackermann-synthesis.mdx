## Introduction

Just as Ackermann's formula computes controller gains $\mathbf{K}$ in closed form without tedious coordinate transformations, you want an equally direct recipe for the observer gain column vector $\mathbf{L}$ when designing high-order state estimators (such as a mobile robot localizer fusing sensor measurements at runtime).

By exploiting **Kalman Duality**—the exact algebraic transpose symmetry between the controller triplet $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{K})$ and the observer triplet $(\mathbf{A}^T, \mathbf{C}^T, \mathbf{L}^T)$—this chapter transforms the controller Ackermann equation into the **Dual Ackermann Observer Formula**, letting you place all $n$ observer error eigenvalues directly while highlighting why the matrix-vector product order reverses.

### Synthesizing Observer Gain by Duality

Consider a second-order single-output plant with non-companion dynamics:

$$
\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$

Its open-loop eigenvalues sit on the main diagonal at $\lambda_1 = -1$ and $\lambda_2 = -2$, and only the first state $y(t) = x_1(t)$ is directly measured, while $x_2(t)$ couples into $\dot{x}_1$ through the off-diagonal entry $2$. Suppose we want to accelerate state convergence by placing both observer error eigenvalues at $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = -5$, which defines the desired monic observer characteristic polynomial:

$$
p_o^*(s) = (s - (-5))^2 = (s + 5)^2 = s^2 + 10s + 25
$$

Instead of forming $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ symbolically and expanding $\det(s\mathbf{I}_2 - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}))$, **Dual Ackermann** computes the column vector $\mathbf{L}$ directly in two decoupled steps:

1. **Observability branch:** First compute the row product $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and stack $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}$ vertically to assemble the Kalman observability matrix $\mathcal{O}$:
   $$
   \mathcal{O} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C} \\ \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det(\mathcal{O}) = (1)(2) - (0)(-1) = 2 \neq 0
   $$
   Rather than inverting all of $\mathcal{O}$, solve the linear system $\mathcal{O}\mathbf{q}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ directly for the last column $\mathbf{q}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} q_{21} \\ q_{22} \end{bmatrix}$ of $\mathcal{O}^{-1}$:
   $$
   \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} q_{21} \\ q_{22} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \implies \begin{cases} q_{21} = 0 \\ -q_{21} + 2q_{22} = 1 \end{cases} \implies \mathbf{q}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Geometrically, this column vector $\mathbf{q}_2$ spans the unmeasured state direction ($\mathbf{C}\mathbf{q}_2 = 0$) normalized so that one step through $\mathbf{A}$ projects to unity at the sensor ($\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q}_2 = 1$).
2. **Spectral branch:** Evaluate the desired observer polynomial on the matrix $\mathbf{A}$ as $p_o^*(\mathbf{A}) = \mathbf{A}^2 + 10\mathbf{A} + 25\mathbf{I}_2$. Computing $\mathbf{A}^2 = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -6 \\ 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ gives:
   $$
   p_o^*(\mathbf{A}) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -6 \\ 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix} + 10\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix} + 25\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 16 & 14 \\ 0 & 9 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

Multiplying the column vector $\mathbf{q}_2$ on the **left** by the matrix polynomial $p_o^*(\mathbf{A})$ yields the observer gain column vector directly:

$$
\mathbf{L} = p_o^*(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{q}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 16 & 14 \\ 0 & 9 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 4.5 \end{bmatrix}
$$

To verify, form the observer error matrix $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 0 \\ 4.5 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -8 & 2 \\ -4.5 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$. Expanding its characteristic polynomial gives $\det(s\mathbf{I}_2 - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})) = (s + 8)(s + 2) - (-2)(4.5) = s^2 + 10s + 16 + 9 = s^2 + 10s + 25 = (s + 5)^2$, confirming that both observer error poles sit at $-5$.

### Principle of Kalman Duality

In [Pole Placement in Canonical Form](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/07-state-feedback/01-pole-placement-controllable-canonical-form/) and [Ackermann Feedback Formula](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/07-state-feedback/02-ackermann-state-feedback-gain-formula/), we solved the state-feedback pole-placement problem: given $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ and a target polynomial $p^*(s)$, find a row vector $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times n}$ such that $\det(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})) = p^*(s)$.

In observer synthesis ([Luenberger Observer Architecture](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/08-observers-compensation/01-luenberger-state-observer-architecture/)), we face the mirror problem: given a single-output pair $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{C})$ with $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times n}$ and a target observer polynomial $p_o^*(s) = \prod_{i=1}^n (s - \beta_i)$, find a column vector $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 1}$ such that the observer error matrix $\mathbf{A}_o = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ satisfies $\det(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})) = p_o^*(s)$.

**Kalman Duality** converts observer synthesis into an algebraically identical state-feedback synthesis problem via matrix transposition. Because the determinant of any square matrix equals the determinant of its transpose ($\det(\mathbf{M}) = \det(\mathbf{M}^T)$) and $(s\mathbf{I}_n)^T = s\mathbf{I}_n$, we can transpose the entire matrix inside the observer characteristic polynomial without altering a single root:

$$
\det\left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\right) = \det\left(\left[s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\right]^T\right) = \det\left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A}^T - \mathbf{C}^T \mathbf{L}^T)\right)
$$

Compare the transposed matrix $\mathbf{A}^T - \mathbf{C}^T \mathbf{L}^T$ against the standard state-feedback matrix $\mathbf{A}_{\text{dual}} - \mathbf{B}_{\text{dual}}\mathbf{K}_{\text{dual}}$. Since $\mathbf{C}^T \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 1}$ is a column vector and $\mathbf{L}^T \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times n}$ is a row vector, the two problems match entry-for-entry under the **duality dictionary**:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{A}_{\text{dual}} = \mathbf{A}^T, \qquad \mathbf{B}_{\text{dual}} = \mathbf{C}^T, \qquad \mathbf{K}_{\text{dual}} = \mathbf{L}^T}
$$

Furthermore, constructing the Kalman controllability matrix $\mathcal{R}_{\text{dual}}$ of the fictional dual plant $(\mathbf{A}_{\text{dual}}, \mathbf{B}_{\text{dual}}) = (\mathbf{A}^T, \mathbf{C}^T)$ yields the exact transpose of the primal observability matrix $\mathcal{O}$:

$$
\mathcal{R}_{\text{dual}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}^T & \mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{C}^T & \dots & (\mathbf{A}^T)^{n-1}\mathbf{C}^T \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C} \\ \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} \\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{n-1} \end{bmatrix}^T = \mathcal{O}^T
$$

Because transposing a matrix preserves its rank ($\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O}^T) = \operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O})$), the primal pair $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{C})$ is completely observable ([Kalman Observability](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/02-kalman-observability-matrix-rank-criterion/)) if and only if the dual pair $(\mathbf{A}^T, \mathbf{C}^T)$ is completely controllable. Likewise, transposing the Controllable Canonical Form $(\mathbf{A}_c, \mathbf{B}_c)$ of Chapter 24 produces **Observable Canonical Form** $(\mathbf{A}_o, \mathbf{C}_o) = (\mathbf{A}_c^T, \mathbf{B}_c^T)$, where the negated characteristic polynomial coefficients $[-a_0, -a_1, \dots, -a_{n-1}]^T$ sit down the last column of $\mathbf{A}_o$ and $\mathbf{C}_o = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \dots & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ selects the last state, so subtracting $\mathbf{L}_o\mathbf{C}_o$ shifts each coefficient decoupledly via $l_{oi} = \gamma_i - a_i$.

### Dual Ackermann Observer Formula

To obtain a direct closed-form expression for $\mathbf{L}$ in arbitrary coordinates without transforming into Observable Canonical Form, we apply the primal Ackermann formula from [Chapter 25](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/07-state-feedback/02-ackermann-state-feedback-gain-formula/) to the dual controllable pair $(\mathbf{A}_{\text{dual}}, \mathbf{B}_{\text{dual}}) = (\mathbf{A}^T, \mathbf{C}^T)$ for the desired observer characteristic polynomial:

$$
p_o^*(s) = \prod_{i=1}^n (s - \beta_i) = s^n + \gamma_{n-1}s^{n-1} + \dots + \gamma_1 s + \gamma_0
$$

Substituting $\mathbf{K}_{\text{dual}} = \mathbf{L}^T$, $\mathcal{R}_{\text{dual}} = \mathcal{O}^T$, and $\mathbf{A}_{\text{dual}} = \mathbf{A}^T$ into $\mathbf{K}_{\text{dual}} = \mathbf{e}_n^T \mathcal{R}_{\text{dual}}^{-1} p_o^*(\mathbf{A}_{\text{dual}})$ gives:

$$
\mathbf{L}^T = \mathbf{e}_n^T (\mathcal{O}^T)^{-1} p_o^*(\mathbf{A}^T)
$$

where $\mathbf{e}_n^T = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \dots & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$. Now recall two basic linear algebra rules for matrix powers and inverses under transposition:
1. The inverse of a transpose equals the transpose of the inverse: $(\mathcal{O}^T)^{-1} = (\mathcal{O}^{-1})^T$.
2. Because $((\mathbf{A}^T)^k) = (\mathbf{A}^k)^T$, any matrix polynomial satisfies $p_o^*(\mathbf{A}^T) = (\mathbf{A}^T)^n + \gamma_{n-1}(\mathbf{A}^T)^{n-1} + \dots + \gamma_0\mathbf{I}_n = \left(p_o^*(\mathbf{A})\right)^T$.

Substituting these two identities into the expression for $\mathbf{L}^T$ and taking the transpose of the entire equation reverses the order of the three factors ($(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{Z})^T = \mathbf{Z}^T\mathbf{Y}^T\mathbf{X}^T$):

$$
\mathbf{L} = \left[ \mathbf{e}_n^T (\mathcal{O}^{-1})^T \left(p_o^*(\mathbf{A})\right)^T \right]^T = \left(\left(p_o^*(\mathbf{A})\right)^T\right)^T \left((\mathcal{O}^{-1})^T\right)^T (\mathbf{e}_n^T)^T
$$

Simplifying the double transposes yields the **Dual Ackermann Observer Formula**:

$$
\boxed{\mathbf{L} = p_o^*(\mathbf{A}) \mathcal{O}^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = p_o^*(\mathbf{A}) \mathbf{q}_n}
$$

Here $\mathbf{e}_n = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \dots & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}^T \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 1}$, and $\mathbf{q}_n = \mathcal{O}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_n \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 1}$ is the **last column** of the inverse observability matrix $\mathcal{O}^{-1}$.

We can also verify this formula directly by column iteration on $\mathbf{A}_o = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$: because $\mathcal{O}\mathbf{q}_n = \mathbf{e}_n$, the column vector $\mathbf{q}_n$ satisfies $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{q}_n = 0$, $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q}_n = 0, \dots, \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{n-2}\mathbf{q}_n = 0$, and $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{n-1}\mathbf{q}_n = 1$. Multiplying $\mathbf{q}_n$ on the left by successive powers of $\mathbf{A}_o = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ shows that $\mathbf{A}_o^k \mathbf{q}_n = \mathbf{A}^k \mathbf{q}_n$ for $k = 1, \dots, n-1$, while at power $k = n$ we get $\mathbf{A}_o^n \mathbf{q}_n = \mathbf{A}_o(\mathbf{A}^{n-1}\mathbf{q}_n) = \mathbf{A}^n\mathbf{q}_n - \mathbf{L}(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{n-1}\mathbf{q}_n) = \mathbf{A}^n\mathbf{q}_n - \mathbf{L}$. Summing weighted by the coefficients of $p_o^*(s)$ gives $p_o^*(\mathbf{A}_o)\mathbf{q}_n = p_o^*(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{q}_n - \mathbf{L}$. Since $p_o^*(\mathbf{A}_o) = \mathbf{0}$ by the Cayley-Hamilton theorem, $\mathbf{L} = p_o^*(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{q}_n$ follows immediately.

### Dual Ackermann Algorithm

To evaluate the Dual Ackermann formula rapidly by hand or in embedded C++ without inverting the full $n \times n$ matrix $\mathcal{O}$ or computing full $n \times n$ matrix powers:

1. **Verify Complete Observability:** Assemble $\mathcal{O} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C} \\ \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} \\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{n-1} \end{bmatrix}$ and check $\det(\mathcal{O}) \neq 0$.
2. **Solve for the Last Column $\mathbf{q}_n$:** Instead of computing all $n$ columns of $\mathcal{O}^{-1}$, solve the single linear system $\mathcal{O}\mathbf{q}_n = \mathbf{e}_n$:
   $$
   \mathcal{O} \mathbf{q}_n = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
3. **Expand the Desired Observer Polynomial:** Multiply out $p_o^*(s) = \prod_{i=1}^n (s - \beta_i) = s^n + \gamma_{n-1}s^{n-1} + \dots + \gamma_1 s + \gamma_0$.
4. **Propagate Column Vectors from the Right:** Instead of forming $\mathbf{A}^2, \dots, \mathbf{A}^n$ as full matrices, multiply the $n \times 1$ column vector $\mathbf{q}_n$ sequentially on the left by $\mathbf{A}$:
   $$
   \mathbf{v}_0 = \mathbf{q}_n, \quad \mathbf{v}_1 = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}_0, \quad \mathbf{v}_2 = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}_1, \quad \dots, \quad \mathbf{v}_n = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}_{n-1}
   $$
   Summing these $n + 1$ column vectors yields the observer gain directly:
   $$
   \mathbf{L} = \mathbf{v}_n + \gamma_{n-1}\mathbf{v}_{n-1} + \dots + \gamma_1\mathbf{v}_1 + \gamma_0\mathbf{v}_0
   $$

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **System / Sensor Matrix** | $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ | $(\mathbf{A}^T, \mathbf{C}^T)$ (primal $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{C})$) |
| **Closed-Loop Matrix** | $\mathbf{A}_{\text{cl}} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$ | $\mathbf{A}_o = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ |
| **Kalman Matrix** | $\mathcal{R} = [\mathbf{B} \;\; \mathbf{A}\mathbf{B} \dots \mathbf{A}^{n-1}\mathbf{B}]$ | $\mathcal{O} = [\mathbf{C}; \;\; \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}; \dots; \;\; \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{n-1}]$ |
| **Basis Solve** | $\mathbf{t}_n^T \mathcal{R} = \mathbf{e}_n^T$ (last row of $\mathcal{R}^{-1}$) | $\mathcal{O}\mathbf{q}_n = \mathbf{e}_n$ (last column of $\mathcal{O}^{-1}$) |
| **Ackermann Formula** | $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{e}_n^T \mathcal{R}^{-1} p^*(\mathbf{A})$ | $\mathbf{L} = p_o^*(\mathbf{A}) \mathcal{O}^{-1} \mathbf{e}_n$ |

### Key Proofs

- **Eigenvalue Transpose Invariance:** $\det(s\mathbf{I} - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})) = \det(s\mathbf{I} - (\mathbf{A}^T - \mathbf{C}^T\mathbf{L}^T))$ maps observer pole placement identically to dual controller pole placement.
- **Product Reversal:** Because $(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{Z})^T = \mathbf{Z}^T\mathbf{Y}^T\mathbf{X}^T$, transposing the dual controller Ackermann equation places $p_o^*(\mathbf{A})$ on the **left** of $\mathcal{O}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_n$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistakes">
1. **Matrix-Vector Product Order Inversion:** Controller Ackermann places $\mathbf{e}_n^T\mathcal{R}^{-1}$ on the left of $p^*(\mathbf{A})$, whereas observer Ackermann places $p_o^*(\mathbf{A})$ on the **left** of $\mathbf{q}_n = \mathcal{O}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_n$. Writing $\mathcal{O}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_n p_o^*(\mathbf{A})$ fails dimensionally and algebraically.
2. **Omitting $\mathbf{I}_n$ on the Constant Term:** Write $\gamma_0 \mathbf{I}_n$ inside $p_o^*(\mathbf{A})$, never a scalar $\gamma_0$.
3. **Picking the Last Row Instead of Last Column:** $\mathbf{q}_n = \mathcal{O}^{-1}\mathbf{e}_n$ is the **last column** of $\mathcal{O}^{-1}$, not the last row.
</Callout>

<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. **Kalman observability matrix and rank check:**
   Multiply $\mathbf{C}$ by $\mathbf{A}$ to form the second row of $\mathcal{O}$:
   $$
   \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -3 & -4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Stacking $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}$ vertically gives the lower-triangular observability matrix:
   $$
   \mathcal{O} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C} \\ \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det(\mathcal{O}) = (1)(2) - (0)(1) = 2 \neq 0
   $$
   Since $\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O}) = 2 = n$, the pair $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{C})$ is completely observable.

2. **Last column $\mathbf{q}_2$ of $\mathcal{O}^{-1}$:**
   Solve $\mathcal{O}\mathbf{q}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}^T$ directly by forward substitution:
   $$
   \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} q_{21} \\ q_{22} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \implies \begin{cases} q_{21} = 0 \\ q_{21} + 2q_{22} = 1 \end{cases} \implies \mathbf{q}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

3. **Target observer polynomial for $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = -6$:**
   Expanding the repeated linear factor gives:
   $$
   p_o^*(s) = (s - (-6))^2 = (s + 6)^2 = s^2 + 12s + 36
   $$

4. **Matrix polynomial $p_o^*(\mathbf{A})$ and Dual Ackermann evaluation:**
   First compute $\mathbf{A}^2$:
   $$
   \mathbf{A}^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -3 & -4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -3 & -4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - 6 & 2 - 8 \\ -3 + 12 & -6 + 16 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -5 & -6 \\ 9 & 10 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Summing $\mathbf{A}^2 + 12\mathbf{A} + 36\mathbf{I}_2$ yields:
   $$
   p_o^*(\mathbf{A}) = \begin{bmatrix} -5 & -6 \\ 9 & 10 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 12 & 24 \\ -36 & -48 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 36 & 0 \\ 0 & 36 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 43 & 18 \\ -27 & -2 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Multiplying $\mathbf{q}_2$ on the left by $p_o^*(\mathbf{A})$ selects half of the second column of $p_o^*(\mathbf{A})$:
   $$
   \mathbf{L} = p_o^*(\mathbf{A}) \mathbf{q}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 43 & 18 \\ -27 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 9 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

5. **Closed-loop observer error verification:**
   Forming $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ gives:
   $$
   \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -3 & -4 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 9 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -8 & 2 \\ -2 & -4 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Computing its characteristic polynomial confirms the target eigenvalues:
   $$
   \det\left(s\mathbf{I}_2 - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\right) = \det\begin{bmatrix} s + 8 & -2 \\ 2 & s + 4 \end{bmatrix} = (s + 8)(s + 4) + 4 = s^2 + 12s + 36 = (s + 6)^2 \quad \checkmark
   $$

A third-order single-output system is described by:

$$
\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ -6 & -11 & -6 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
$$

1. Construct the observability matrix $\mathcal{O}$ and extract the last column $\mathbf{q}_3$ of $\mathcal{O}^{-1}$.
2. The desired observer poles are assigned at $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = \beta_3 = -10$. Determine the target polynomial $p_o^*(s) = (s + 10)^3$.
3. Compute the matrix powers $\mathbf{A}^2, \mathbf{A}^3$ and evaluate the dual Ackermann gain vector $\mathbf{L} = p_o^*(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{q}_3$.
4. Form the observer error matrix $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ and verify directly that its characteristic polynomial matches $p_o^*(s)$.

<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. **Observability matrix and last column $\mathbf{q}_3$:**
   Because the first two rows of $\mathbf{A}$ shift the unit entry of $\mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ one column to the right on each multiplication:
   $$
   \mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Stacking these three rows shows that the observability matrix is the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix:
   $$
   \mathcal{O} = \mathbf{I}_3 \implies \mathcal{O}^{-1} = \mathbf{I}_3 \implies \mathbf{q}_3 = \mathcal{O}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

2. **Target observer polynomial for $\beta_1 = \beta_2 = \beta_3 = -10$:**
   Expanding the cube $(s + 10)^3$ gives:
   $$
   p_o^*(s) = (s + 10)^3 = s^3 + 3(10)s^2 + 3(10^2)s + 10^3 = s^3 + 30s^2 + 300s + 1000
   $$

3. **Right-to-left column vector propagation for $\mathbf{L} = p_o^*(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{q}_3$:**
   Rather than forming full $3 \times 3$ matrices $\mathbf{A}^2$ and $\mathbf{A}^3$, we propagate the column vector $\mathbf{q}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}^T$ sequentially on the left by $\mathbf{A}$:
   $$
   \mathbf{q}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
   \mathbf{A}\mathbf{q}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ -6 & -11 & -6 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ -6 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   $$
   \mathbf{A}^2\mathbf{q}_3 = \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q}_3) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ -6 & -11 & -6 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ -6 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -6 \\ -11(1) - 6(-6) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -6 \\ 25 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   $$
   \mathbf{A}^3\mathbf{q}_3 = \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{A}^2\mathbf{q}_3) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ -6 & -11 & -6 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -6 \\ 25 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -6 \\ 25 \\ -6(1) - 11(-6) - 6(25) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -6 \\ 25 \\ -90 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Combining these four column vectors with the coefficients of $p_o^*(s) = s^3 + 30s^2 + 300s + 1000$ yields $\mathbf{L}$:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \mathbf{L} &= \mathbf{A}^3\mathbf{q}_3 + 30(\mathbf{A}^2\mathbf{q}_3) + 300(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{q}_3) + 1000\mathbf{q}_3 \\
   &= \begin{bmatrix} -6 \\ 25 \\ -90 \end{bmatrix} + 30\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -6 \\ 25 \end{bmatrix} + 300\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ -6 \end{bmatrix} + 1000\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \\
   &= \begin{bmatrix} -6 + 30 + 0 + 0 \\ 25 - 180 + 300 + 0 \\ -90 + 750 - 1800 + 1000 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 24 \\ 145 \\ -140 \end{bmatrix}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

4. **Direct closed-loop verification:**
   Subtracting $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ from $\mathbf{A}$ modifies the first column of $\mathbf{A}$:
   $$
   \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ -6 & -11 & -6 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 24 & 0 & 0 \\ 145 & 0 & 0 \\ -140 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -24 & 1 & 0 \\ -145 & 0 & 1 \\ 134 & -11 & -6 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Expanding $\det(s\mathbf{I}_3 - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}))$ along the first row gives:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \det\begin{bmatrix} s + 24 & -1 & 0 \\ 145 & s & -1 \\ -134 & 11 & s + 6 \end{bmatrix} &= (s + 24)\left[s(s + 6) - (-1)(11)\right] - (-1)\left[145(s + 6) - (-1)(-134)\right] \\
   &= (s + 24)(s^2 + 6s + 11) + (145s + 870 - 134) \\
   &= (s^3 + 30s^2 + 155s + 264) + (145s + 736) \\
   &= s^3 + 30s^2 + 300s + 1000 = (s + 10)^3 \quad \checkmark
   \end{aligned}
   $$
