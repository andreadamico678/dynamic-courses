## Introduction

Up to this point, you designed the state-feedback controller $\mathbf{K}$ assuming true physical states $\mathbf{x}(t)$, and you designed the Luenberger observer $\mathbf{L}$ as a standalone estimator. When you actually wire them together on a drone or industrial robot—replacing the unmeasured true state with the observer estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ inside $\mathbf{u}(t) = -\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{v}(t)$—the plant and observer form a tightly cross-coupled $2n$-dimensional loop. Could the observer's transient estimation errors shift or destabilize the controller's carefully placed eigenvalues?

The **Separation Principle** delivers the culminating structural guarantee of linear systems theory: under a simple similarity change of basis to $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{e})$ coordinates, the $2n \times 2n$ closed-loop matrix becomes **block upper-triangular**, proving that the $n$ controller eigenvalues and $n$ observer eigenvalues remain **100% decoupled**. That theorem is the mathematical license behind modular ROS 2 autonomy stacks where estimation and control teams tune their nodes independently.

### Coupled Controller and Observer

Consider an unstable scalar plant $\dot{x}(t) = x(t) + u(t)$ ($a = 1, b = 1$) with measured output $y(t) = x(t)$ ($c = 1$). Because the open-loop pole sits in the right half-plane at $\lambda = +1 > 0$, the unforced plant is exponentially unstable. Suppose we design the state-feedback gain $k = 4$ to place the closed-loop controller eigenvalue at $a - bk = 1 - 4 = -3$, and we design the Luenberger observer gain $l = 9$ to place the observer error eigenvalue at $a - lc = 1 - 9 = -8$.

Now connect the observer output $\hat{x}(t)$ directly into the feedback law, $u(t) = -4\hat{x}(t)$, and feed that same control input $u(t)$ into the observer $\dot{\hat{x}}(t) = \hat{x}(t) + u(t) + 9(y(t) - \hat{x}(t))$. Because $y(t) = x(t)$ and $u(t) = -4\hat{x}(t)$, both differential equations couple $x(t)$ and $\hat{x}(t)$ together:

$$
\dot{x}(t) = x(t) - 4\hat{x}(t), \qquad \dot{\hat{x}}(t) = \hat{x}(t) - 4\hat{x}(t) + 9\left(x(t) - \hat{x}(t)\right) = 9x(t) - 12\hat{x}(t)
$$

Writing this interconnected system in the composite state vector $\begin{bmatrix} x(t) & \hat{x}(t) \end{bmatrix}^T$ produces a fully populated $2 \times 2$ matrix:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x}(t) \\ \dot{\hat{x}}(t) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -4 \\ 9 & -12 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x(t) \\ \hat{x}(t) \end{bmatrix}
$$

At first glance, every entry of $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -4 \\ 9 & -12 \end{bmatrix}$ is non-zero, and neither $-3$ nor $-8$ appears on its diagonal. However, switch coordinates from $(x, \hat{x})$ to $(x, e)$ using the estimation error $e(t) = x(t) - \hat{x}(t)$, so that $\hat{x}(t) = x(t) - e(t)$:

$$
\dot{x}(t) = x(t) - 4\left(x(t) - e(t)\right) = -3x(t) + 4e(t)
$$

$$
\dot{e}(t) = \dot{x}(t) - \dot{\hat{x}}(t) = \left(x(t) - 4\hat{x}(t)\right) - \left(9x(t) - 12\hat{x}(t)\right) = -8\left(x(t) - \hat{x}(t)\right) = -8e(t)
$$

Assembling these two equations into matrix form eliminates the lower-left entry and makes the closed-loop system **upper-triangular**:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{x}(t) \\ \dot{e}(t) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -3 & 4 \\ 0 & -8 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x(t) \\ e(t) \end{bmatrix}
$$

Because the determinant of an upper-triangular matrix is simply the product of its diagonal entries, the closed-loop characteristic polynomial is $(s + 3)(s + 8) = s^2 + 11s + 24$, and the eigenvalues sit at $-3$ and $-8$—completely unchanged by the $4e(t)$ coupling term!

Finally, we can view the combined observer + feedback law ($\dot{\hat{x}} = -12\hat{x} + 9y$, $u = -4\hat{x}$) as a single classical transfer-function block mapping sensor measurement $Y(s)$ to control action $U(s) = -C(s)Y(s)$. Taking the Laplace transform gives $(s + 12)\hat{X}(s) = 9Y(s)$, so $U(s) = -4\hat{X}(s) = -\frac{36}{s + 12}Y(s)$, where the standalone **dynamic compensator** is:

$$
C(s) = \frac{k l}{s - (a - bk - lc)} = \frac{(4)(9)}{s - (1 - 4 - 9)} = \frac{36}{s + 12}
$$

Notice that the internal pole of the standalone compensator $C(s)$ sits at $s = -12$, whereas closing the loop around the plant $G(s) = \frac{1}{s - 1}$ places the closed-loop poles at $1 + C(s)G(s) = 0 \implies (s - 1)(s + 12) + 36 = s^2 + 11s + 24 = (s + 3)(s + 8) = 0$.

<Divider label="2 · Theory" />

Interconnecting a state-feedback controller $\mathbf{K}$ with a Luenberger observer $\mathbf{L}$ creates a $2n$-dimensional output-feedback system whose spectral structure appears coupled at first glance. This section forms the composite $(\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}})$ equations, applies the error-coordinate similarity transformation to prove the Separation Principle, and derives the standalone dynamic compensator transfer function $C(s)$.

### Interconnected State Equations

In Part VII ([Pole Placement in Canonical Form](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/07-state-feedback/01-pole-placement-controllable-canonical-form/)) we designed the state-feedback gain matrix $\mathbf{K} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ assuming full state measurement, and in Chapter 27 ([Luenberger Observer Architecture](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/08-observers-compensation/01-luenberger-state-observer-architecture/)) we designed the observer gain matrix $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p}$ to reconstruct the state estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ from sensor measurements $\mathbf{y}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^p$.

In a physical output-feedback control loop, we interconnect these two blocks by replacing the unmeasured true state $\mathbf{x}(t)$ inside the control law with its real-time Luenberger estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$:

$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}(t), \qquad \mathbf{y}(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}(t) \\
\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}(t) + \mathbf{L}\left(\mathbf{y}(t) - \mathbf{C}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)\right) \\
\mathbf{u}(t) = -\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{v}(t)
\end{cases}
$$

Here $\mathbf{v}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^m$ is an external reference input. Substituting $\mathbf{u}(t) = -\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{v}(t)$ and $\mathbf{y}(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}(t)$ into both differential equations eliminates the intermediate variables $\mathbf{u}(t)$ and $\mathbf{y}(t)$:

$$
\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}(t)
$$

$$
\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}(t) + (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}(t)
$$

Stacking the physical plant state $\mathbf{x}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and the software observer state $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ into a single $2n$-dimensional composite state vector yields the interconnected closed-loop state-space representation:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) \\ \dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A} & -\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} \\ \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} & \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} \end{bmatrix}}_{\overline{\mathbf{A}}} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t) \\ \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B} \\ \mathbf{B} \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{v}(t)
$$

In these $(\mathbf{x}, \hat{\mathbf{x}})$ coordinates, the $2n \times 2n$ closed-loop matrix $\overline{\mathbf{A}}$ has non-zero coupling blocks $-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$ in the upper right and $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ in the lower left, making it appear as though $\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{L}$ interact to shift all $2n$ eigenvalues.

### The Separation Principle

To uncover the true spectral structure of $\overline{\mathbf{A}}$, apply an invertible [similarity transformation](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/01-state-models/02-coordinate-basis-similarity-transformations/) that replaces the observer state $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ with the observer estimation error $\mathbf{e}(t) = \mathbf{x}(t) - \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t) \\ \mathbf{e}(t) \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I}_n & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{I}_n & -\mathbf{I}_n \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t) \\ \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) \end{bmatrix}
$$

Notice that multiplying $\mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}$ by itself gives $\mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}^2 = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I}_n & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{I}_n \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{I}_{2n}$, so $\mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}$ is involutory ($\mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}^{-1} = \mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}$, equivalently $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{e}(t)$). Let us compute the transformed dynamics in $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{e})$ coordinates both via differential equations and via direct block-matrix multiplication:

1. **Plant Error Coupling:** Substituting $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{e}(t)$ into the control law $\mathbf{u}(t) = -\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{e}(t)) + \mathbf{v}(t)$ rewrites $\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ as the ideal full-state feedback dynamics plus a transient error injection $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{e}(t)$:
   $$
   \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\left(\mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{e}(t)\right) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}(t) = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{e}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}(t)
   $$
2. **Autonomous Error Dynamics:** Subtracting $\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t)$ from $\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ cancels both $-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ and $+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}(t)$ identically:
   $$
   \dot{\mathbf{e}}(t) = \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) - \dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\left(\mathbf{x}(t) - \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)\right) = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\mathbf{e}(t)
   $$

Stacking these two vector equations (or computing $\mathbf{A}_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}\overline{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}^{-1}$ and $\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B} \\ \mathbf{B} \end{bmatrix}$) produces the **block upper-triangular** closed-loop system:

$$
\boxed{\begin{bmatrix} \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) \\ \dot{\mathbf{e}}(t) \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} & \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t) \\ \mathbf{e}(t) \end{bmatrix} + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B} \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}}} \mathbf{v}(t), \qquad \mathbf{y}(t) = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t) \\ \mathbf{e}(t) \end{bmatrix}}
$$

Because $\mathbf{A}_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}\overline{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}^{-1}$ is a similarity transformation of $\overline{\mathbf{A}}$ and its lower-left $n \times n$ block is identically $\mathbf{0}$, the determinant of $s\mathbf{I}_{2n} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}$ factors cleanly into the product of its two $n \times n$ diagonal block determinants:

$$
\boxed{\det(s\mathbf{I}_{2n} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}) = \det\left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\right) \cdot \det\left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\right) = p_c^*(s) \cdot p_o^*(s)}
$$

This factorization establishes three foundational results known collectively as the **Separation Principle**:

1. **Complete Eigenvalue Decoupling:** The $2n$ eigenvalues of the interconnected closed-loop system are the exact union of the $n$ state-feedback controller eigenvalues and the $n$ Luenberger observer eigenvalues:
   $$
   \sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}) = \sigma(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}) \cup \sigma(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})
   $$
   Inserting the observer into the state-feedback loop does *not* move a single controller eigenvalue, and closing the controller loop does *not* move a single observer eigenvalue. You can synthesize $\mathbf{K}$ (via Chapter 25) and $\mathbf{L}$ (via Chapter 28) completely independently.
2. **Reference-to-Output Transfer Function Invariance:** Look at the input and output matrices in $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{e})$ coordinates: $\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{B} \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}$ has zero entry into the error dynamics $\dot{\mathbf{e}}$, and $\mathbf{C}_{\text{total}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C} & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}$ reads only $\mathbf{x}$. By [Kalman Canonical Decomposition](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/06-kalman-structure/03-kalman-canonical-subspace-decomposition/), the $n$ estimation error modes $\mathbf{e}(t)$ are completely **uncontrollable** from the reference command $\mathbf{v}(t)$! Consequently, all $n$ observer poles $p_o^*(s)$ cancel identically in the closed-loop transfer function matrix from $\mathbf{V}(s)$ to $\mathbf{Y}(s)$:
   $$
   \mathbf{W}_{\text{CL}}(s) = \mathbf{C}_{\text{total}}(s\mathbf{I}_{2n} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{\text{total}} = \mathbf{C}\left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\right)^{-1}\mathbf{B}
   $$
   For zero initial estimation error $\mathbf{e}(0) = \mathbf{0}$, the forced response to any command $\mathbf{v}(t)$ is *100% identical* to ideal full-state feedback.
3. **Transient Coupling via $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{e}(t)$:** Whenever $\mathbf{e}(0) = \mathbf{x}(0) - \hat{\mathbf{x}}(0) \neq \mathbf{0}$, the off-diagonal block $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$ injects the decaying error $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}e^{(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})t}\mathbf{e}(0)$ as a forcing disturbance into $\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)$. Choosing observer poles $2\text{–}5\times$ faster than controller poles ensures that $\mathbf{e}(t)$ vanishes before it can noticeably deflect the physical trajectory $\mathbf{x}(t)$.

### Dynamic Compensator Transfer Function

In classical frequency-domain control (Part V), a feedback controller is represented as a transfer function $C(s)$ placed in a loop from measured plant output $\mathbf{Y}(s)$ to control input $\mathbf{U}(s)$. Our state-space observer-controller pair is an $n$-th order dynamical system with internal state $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$, driven by $\mathbf{y}(t)$ (with $\mathbf{v}(t) = \mathbf{0}$) and producing output $\mathbf{u}(t)$:

$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) = \underbrace{(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})}_{\mathbf{A}_{\text{comp}}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{L}\mathbf{y}(t) \\
\mathbf{u}(t) = -\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)
\end{cases}
$$

Applying the unilateral Laplace transform with zero initial observer state $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(0) = \mathbf{0}$ (as in [Transfer Function Matrix Invariants](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/02-laplace-resolvent/04-transfer-function-matrix-invariants/)) gives:

$$
\left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\right)\hat{\mathbf{X}}(s) = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{Y}(s) \implies \hat{\mathbf{X}}(s) = \left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\right)^{-1}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{Y}(s)
$$

Substituting $\hat{\mathbf{X}}(s)$ into the feedback equation $\mathbf{U}(s) = -\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{X}}(s)$ yields the negative-feedback relationship $\mathbf{U}(s) = -\mathbf{C}_{\text{comp}}(s)\mathbf{Y}(s)$, where the **$n$-th order dynamic compensator** transfer function (written $C(s)$ for SISO loops) is:

$$
\boxed{C(s) = \mathbf{K}\left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\right)^{-1} \mathbf{L}}
$$

Note carefully that the poles of the standalone compensator $C(s)$ are the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}_{\text{comp}} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$—which are *neither* the controller poles $\sigma(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})$ *nor* the observer poles $\sigma(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})$. Only when the negative-feedback loop $\mathbf{U}(s) = -C(s)\mathbf{Y}(s)$ is closed around the physical plant $\mathbf{Y}(s) = \mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I}_n - \mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{U}(s)$ do the $2n$ closed-loop poles snap to $p_c^*(s) \cdot p_o^*(s) = 0$.

<Callout type="bridge" title="Modular Autonomy Architecture">

Autonomous mobile robots and drones running ROS 2 (such as PX4 or Nav2) implement this mathematical boundary directly across separate software nodes:

1. **State Estimation Node:** The estimator (`robot_localization` / EKF) subscribes to `/imu/data`, `/wheel_odometry`, and `/gps/fix` to compute $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ using $(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})$, independent of trajectory tracking gains.
2. **Controller Node (`controller_server`):** Subscribes to `/odometry/filtered` ($\hat{\mathbf{x}}$) and commands $\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, independent of which sensors produced the estimate.

Because the composite error matrix is block upper-triangular, an asymptotically stable estimator and an asymptotically stable controller guarantee overall closed-loop stability across decoupled nodes.

</Callout>

| Component | State / Matrix Expression | Spectrum / Dimension |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Controller Gain $\mathbf{K}$** | $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$ | $n$ controller eigenvalues $p_c^*(s)$ |
| **Observer Gain $\mathbf{L}$** | $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ | $n$ observer eigenvalues $p_o^*(s)$ |
| **Composite $(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{e})$ System** | $\mathbf{A}_{\text{total}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} & \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} \end{bmatrix}$ | $2n$ eigenvalues: $p_c^*(s) \cdot p_o^*(s)$ |
| **Dynamic Compensator** | $C(s) = \mathbf{K}(s\mathbf{I} - (\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}))^{-1}\mathbf{L}$ | $n$-th order negative-feedback block ($\mathbf{U} = -C\mathbf{Y}$) |

### Compensator Synthesis Procedure

1. Verify $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ is controllable (or stabilizable) and $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{C})$ is observable (or detectable).
2. Design $\mathbf{K}$ independently to place $\sigma(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})$ at desired controller poles.
3. Design $\mathbf{L}$ independently to place $\sigma(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})$ roughly $2\text{–}5\times$ faster than controller poles.
4. Assemble $\mathbf{A}_{\text{comp}} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ and form $C(s) = \mathbf{K}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{comp}})^{-1}\mathbf{L}$.

### Key Proofs

- **Block-Triangular Similarity:** Transforming $\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} \\ \hat{\mathbf{x}} \end{bmatrix} \mapsto \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} \\ \mathbf{e} \end{bmatrix}$ eliminates $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ from the bottom-left block because $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}$ cancels in $\dot{\mathbf{x}} - \dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$.
- **Characteristic Factorization:** The determinant of any block upper-triangular matrix equals the product of its diagonal block determinants, giving $\det(s\mathbf{I}_{2n} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}) = p_c^*(s) p_o^*(s)$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistakes">
1. **Compensator vs. Closed-Loop Poles:** The eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}_{\text{comp}} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ are the *open-loop poles of $C(s)$* (which can even be unstable!), **not** the closed-loop poles $\sigma(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}) \cup \sigma(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})$.
2. **Wrong Error Transformation Sign:** Define $\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{x} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}$ so $\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{e}$, giving upper-right block $+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$ (not $-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$).
3. **Negative Feedback Sign:** Because $\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, $C(s)$ includes the minus sign in $\mathbf{U}(s) = -C(s)\mathbf{Y}(s)$.
</Callout>

   The open-loop characteristic polynomial of $\mathbf{A}$ is:
   $$
   p(s) = \det(s\mathbf{I}_2 - \mathbf{A}) = \det\begin{bmatrix} s & -1 \\ 0 & s + 2 \end{bmatrix} = s(s + 2) = s^2 + 2s \implies a_1 = 2, \; a_0 = 0

   For target closed-loop controller eigenvalues $\mu_{1,2} = -2 \pm j2$, the desired polynomial is:
   $$
   p_c^*(s) = (s + 2 - j2)(s + 2 + j2) = (s + 2)^2 + 2^2 = s^2 + 4s + 8 \implies \alpha_1 = 4, \; \alpha_0 = 8
   $$
   Because $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B})$ is already in Controllable Canonical Form, each gain entry is the direct coefficient difference:
   $$
   k_1 = \alpha_0 - a_0 = 8 - 0 = 8, \qquad k_2 = \alpha_1 - a_1 = 4 - 2 = 2 \implies \mathbf{K} = \begin{bmatrix} 8 & 2 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

2. **Observer gain $\mathbf{L}$ synthesis:**
   For target observer eigenvalues $\beta_{1,2} = -10, -10$, the desired observer polynomial is:
   $$
   p_o^*(s) = (s + 10)^2 = s^2 + 20s + 100
   $$
   Forming the observer error matrix $\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$ gives:
   $$
   \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} l_1 & 0 \\ l_2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -l_1 & 1 \\ -l_2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Expanding its characteristic polynomial and matching to $s^2 + 20s + 100$ yields:
   $$
   \det\begin{bmatrix} s + l_1 & -1 \\ l_2 & s + 2 \end{bmatrix} = (s + l_1)(s + 2) + l_2 = s^2 + (l_1 + 2)s + (2l_1 + l_2)
   $$
   $$
   l_1 + 2 = 20 \implies l_1 = 18, \qquad 2(18) + l_2 = 100 \implies l_2 = 64 \implies \mathbf{L} = \begin{bmatrix} 18 \\ 64 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

3. **Dynamic Compensator Matrix:**
   First compute the two $2 \times 2$ outer products $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}$:
   $$
   \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 8 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 8 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} 18 \\ 64 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 18 & 0 \\ 64 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
   $$
   Subtracting both outer products from $\mathbf{A}$ yields the internal compensator dynamics matrix:
   $$
   \mathbf{A}_{\text{comp}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 8 & 2 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 18 & 0 \\ 64 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -18 & 1 \\ -72 & -4 \end{bmatrix}
   $$

4. **Compensator Transfer Function:**
   Form the compensator resolvent matrix and its determinant:
   $$
   s\mathbf{I}_2 - \mathbf{A}_{\text{comp}} = \begin{bmatrix} s + 18 & -1 \\ 72 & s + 4 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det(s\mathbf{I}_2 - \mathbf{A}_{\text{comp}}) = (s + 18)(s + 4) + 72 = s^2 + 22s + 144
   $$
   Inverting $s\mathbf{I}_2 - \mathbf{A}_{\text{comp}}$ via the $2 \times 2$ adjugate formula and multiplying by $\mathbf{K}$ on the left and $\mathbf{L}$ on the right gives:
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   C(s) &= \frac{1}{s^2 + 22s + 144} \begin{bmatrix} 8 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} s + 4 & 1 \\ -72 & s + 18 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 18 \\ 64 \end{bmatrix} \\
   &= \frac{1}{s^2 + 22s + 144} \begin{bmatrix} 8(s + 4) - 144 & 8 + 2(s + 18) \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 18 \\ 64 \end{bmatrix} \\
   &= \frac{1}{s^2 + 22s + 144} \begin{bmatrix} 8s - 112 & 2s + 44 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 18 \\ 64 \end{bmatrix} \\
   &= \frac{18(8s - 112) + 64(2s + 44)}{s^2 + 22s + 144} = \frac{(144s - 2016) + (128s + 2816)}{s^2 + 22s + 144} \\
   &= \frac{272s + 800}{s^2 + 22s + 144} = \frac{16(17s + 50)}{s^2 + 22s + 144}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

<Exercise client:load id="ex-29-2" title="Exercise 29.2 — Separation Spectrum Invariance" level="Exam Standard" hint="Substitute $\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{e}$ into $\dot{\mathbf{x}}$ and $\dot{\mathbf{e}} = \dot{\mathbf{x}} - \dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ to verify the zero lower-left block.">

Consider a linear plant $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C})$ controlled via an asymptotic Luenberger observer $\dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} = \mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{L}(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{C}\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ with state feedback $\mathbf{u} = -\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}$.

1. Write down the combined $2n$-dimensional state-space equations in the composite state vector $\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} \\ \hat{\mathbf{x}} \end{bmatrix}$.
2. Apply the state transformation $\mathbf{z} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} \\ \mathbf{e} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{I} & -\mathbf{I} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} \\ \hat{\mathbf{x}} \end{bmatrix}$ and derive the block upper-triangular closed-loop system matrix:
   $$
   \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} & \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} \end{bmatrix}
   $$
3. Prove that the closed-loop characteristic polynomial factors as:
   $$
   \det(s\mathbf{I}_{2n} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}) = \det(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})) \cdot \det(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}))
   $$
   confirming that controller and observer poles can be designed independently.
4. Explain the physical role of the non-zero off-diagonal block $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$: if $\mathbf{e}(0) \neq \mathbf{0}$, how does the estimation error transient perturb the actual physical state trajectory $\mathbf{x}(t)$ even though the eigenvalues are decoupled?

<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. **Composite State Equations:**
   Substituting the feedback control law $\mathbf{u}(t) = -\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ and the output measurement $\mathbf{y}(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}(t)$ into the plant and observer differential equations gives:
   $$
   \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)
   $$
   $$
   \dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{L}\left(\mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{C}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)\right) = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}(t) + (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)
   $$
   Stacking these into $2n \times 2n$ block-matrix form yields:
   $$
   \begin{bmatrix} \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) \\ \dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A} & -\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} \\ \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} & \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t) \\ \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) \end{bmatrix}
   $$

2. **Separation Coordinate Basis:**
   Define $\mathbf{e}(t) = \mathbf{x}(t) - \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$, so that $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{e}(t)$. The similarity transformation matrix $\mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{I}_n & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{I}_n & -\mathbf{I}_n \end{bmatrix}$ satisfies $\mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}^{-1} = \mathbf{T}_{\text{sep}}$. Substituting $\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{e}(t)$ into $\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ gives:
   $$
   \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\left(\mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{e}(t)\right) = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{e}(t)
   $$
   Differentiating $\mathbf{e}(t) = \mathbf{x}(t) - \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ cancels $-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)$ identically:
   $$
   \dot{\mathbf{e}}(t) = \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) - \dot{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}(t) = \left(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)\right) - \left(\mathbf{A}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t) + \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{x}(t) - \hat{\mathbf{x}}(t))\right) = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\mathbf{e}(t)
   $$
   Assembling both equations into block-matrix form yields the block upper-triangular matrix $\mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}$:
   $$
   \begin{bmatrix} \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) \\ \dot{\mathbf{e}}(t) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} & \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}(t) \\ \mathbf{e}(t) \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}} \mathbf{z}(t)
   $$

3. **Spectrum factorization proof:**
   Because similarity transformations preserve determinants and the lower-left $n \times n$ block of $s\mathbf{I}_{2n} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}$ is $\mathbf{0}$, the block determinant factors into the product of the two diagonal block determinants:
   $$
   \det(s\mathbf{I}_{2n} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}) = \det\begin{bmatrix} s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}) & -\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K} \\ \mathbf{0} & s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C}) \end{bmatrix} = \det\left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\right) \cdot \det\left(s\mathbf{I}_n - (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})\right)
   $$
   Setting $\det(s\mathbf{I}_{2n} - \mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}) = 0$ proves that the closed-loop spectrum is the disjoint union:
   $$
   \sigma(\mathbf{A}_{\text{total}}) = \sigma(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}) \cup \sigma(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})
   $$
   confirming the **Separation Principle**: $\mathbf{K}$ and $\mathbf{L}$ can be synthesized independently without shifting each other's eigenvalues.

4. **Role of Coupling $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}$:**
   While the estimation error $\mathbf{e}(t) = e^{(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})t}\mathbf{e}(0)$ evolves autonomously, the physical plant state satisfies the forced differential equation:
   $$
   \dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{e}(t)
   $$
   Whenever the observer starts with a nonzero initial estimation error $\mathbf{e}(0) \neq \mathbf{0}$, the term $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{e}(t)$ acts as an endogenous decaying disturbance input that deflects the physical trajectory $\mathbf{x}(t)$ away from the ideal full-state feedback response $e^{(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K})t}\mathbf{x}(0)$. Only when $\mathbf{e}(0) = \mathbf{0}$ (or after $\mathbf{e}(t)$ has decayed to zero at rate $e^{(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{L}\mathbf{C})t}$) does the plant follow the pure state-feedback trajectory.
