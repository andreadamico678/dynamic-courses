## Introduction

In real control tuning, you rarely just ask whether a single fixed plant is stable—you want to know how far you can push a tunable controller gain $K$ before the closed-loop hardware shakes itself unstable. Turn gain too low and a drone feels sluggish; push gain past $K_{\text{crit}}$ and poles cross the imaginary axis into sustained oscillation or divergence.

Applying the Routh array symbolically in $K$ gives exact algebraic inequalities bounding the safe operating window $(K_{\min}, K_{\max})$ and reveals the exact **ultimate oscillation frequency** $\omega_{\text{osc}}$ at the stability edge—the analytical foundation behind industrial PID Ziegler-Nichols tuning.

Expanding the open-loop denominator $D(s) = (s+1)(s^2 + 6s + 8) = s^3 + 7s^2 + 14s + 8$ with $N(s) = 1$, the closed-loop transfer function is:

$$W_{\text{CL}}(s) = \frac{K G(s)}{1 + K G(s)} = \frac{K}{s^3 + 7s^2 + 14s + (8 + K)}$$

Its denominator is the parameterized characteristic polynomial $p(s, K) = s^3 + 7s^2 + 14s + (8 + K) = 0$. Building the Routh table symbolically in $K$ gives row $s^1$ entry $b_1(K) = \frac{7(14) - 1(8 + K)}{7} = \frac{90 - K}{7}$ and row $s^0$ entry $c_1(K) = 8 + K$:

$$
p(s, K) = s^3 + 7s^2 + 14s + (8 + K) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\begin{array}{c|ll}
s^3 & 1 & 14 \\
s^2 & 7 & 8 + K \\
s^1 & \frac{90 - K}{7} & 0 \\
s^0 & 8 + K & 0
\end{array}
$$

For strict Hurwitz stability, every entry in Column 1 must remain strictly positive: $\frac{90 - K}{7} > 0 \implies K < 90$ and $8 + K > 0 \implies K > -8$. Intersecting these inequalities gives the exact stabilizing gain window $\boxed{-8 < K < 90}$ ($0 < K < 90$ for positive physical gains). Physically, dropping below $K = -8$ pushes a real pole across $s = 0$ into the RHP (monotonic divergence), whereas exceeding $K_{\text{crit}} = 90$ pushes a complex conjugate pair across the imaginary axis (oscillatory instability).

At the upper boundary $K_{\text{crit}} = 90$, row $s^1$ vanishes identically while row $s^2$ is $[\alpha_1, \alpha_2] = [7, \; 8 + 90] = [7, \; 98]$. Solving the auxiliary equation $A(s) = 0$ locates the imaginary-axis poles and the exact sustained oscillation frequency:

$$A(s) = 7s^2 + 98 = 0 \implies s = \pm j\sqrt{\frac{98}{7}} = \pm j\sqrt{14} \implies \omega_{\text{osc}} = \sqrt{14} \approx 3.74\;\text{rad/s}$$

### Parameterized Characteristic Polynomial

For a unity negative-feedback loop with fixed plant $G(s) = N(s)/D(s)$ and proportional gain $K$, the closed-loop transfer function is:

$$W_{\text{CL}}(s) = \frac{K G(s)}{1 + K G(s)} = \frac{K N(s)}{D(s) + K N(s)}$$

Assuming $N(s)$ and $D(s)$ share no common factors (in particular, no unstable pole-zero cancellations that would hide an RHP mode), the closed-loop poles are the roots of the **parameterized characteristic polynomial**:

$$\boxed{p(s, K) = D(s) + K N(s) = a_n(K) s^n + \dots + a_1(K) s + a_0(K) = 0}$$

Notice that varying $K$ moves the closed-loop poles continuously through the complex plane: at $K = 0$ they sit at the open-loop poles $D(s) = 0$, and as $|K|$ increases they migrate toward the zeros or infinity, potentially crossing into the RHP.

### Critical Gain and Oscillation Frequency

By [Routh-Hurwitz Stability Criterion](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/04-stability-routh/03-routh-hurwitz-tabular-array-construction/), $p(s, K)$ is strictly Hurwitz if and only if every first-column entry $c_{k,1}(K)$ is strictly positive. Whenever a first-column entry is a rational expression $f(K)/g(K)$, its denominator $g(K)$ is the pivot of the row immediately above—which is already required to be strictly positive ($g(K) > 0$) by its own Column 1 inequality. Consequently, $f(K)/g(K) > 0$ simplifies directly to $f(K) > 0$. Intersecting all resulting inequalities yields the stabilizing gain interval $K \in (K_{\min}, K_{\max})$.

As $K$ reaches a boundary $K_{\text{crit}}$, closed-loop poles can cross the imaginary axis in two distinct ways:
1. **Real Pole Crossing at $s = 0$:** When the constant term $a_0(K) = 0$, $s = 0$ becomes a root of $p(s, K) = 0$, marking a static stability boundary.
2. **Complex Pair Crossing at $\pm j\omega_{\text{osc}}$:** When a complex conjugate pair crosses the $j\omega$-axis, row $s^1$ vanishes identically while row $s^2$ remains non-zero (Singular Case 2 from [Singular Routh Tables](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/04-stability-routh/04-singular-routh-table-zero-elements/)). Denoting row $s^2$ evaluated at $K = K_{\text{crit}}$ explicitly as $[\alpha_1(K_{\text{crit}}),\; \alpha_2(K_{\text{crit}})]$ (kept distinct from row $s^{n-2}$ entries $b_1, b_2$ so the notation holds for any polynomial degree $n \ge 3$), the quadratic auxiliary polynomial is:

$$A(s) = \alpha_1(K_{\text{crit}}) s^2 + \alpha_2(K_{\text{crit}}) = 0$$

Substituting $s = j\omega_{\text{osc}}$ gives $(j\omega_{\text{osc}})^2 = -\omega_{\text{osc}}^2$, which yields the exact **sustained oscillation frequency**:

$$-\alpha_1(K_{\text{crit}})\omega_{\text{osc}}^2 + \alpha_2(K_{\text{crit}}) = 0 \implies \boxed{\omega_{\text{osc}} = \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_2(K_{\text{crit}})}{\alpha_1(K_{\text{crit}})}}}$$

Because a complex pair $\pm j\omega_{\text{osc}}$ is always a degree-2 factor, this auxiliary equation always lives in row $s^2$ right above the vanishing $s^1$ row, regardless of the total order $n$ of the plant.

<Divider label="3 · Summary" />

| Stability Feature | Algebraic Condition | Design Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lower Bound** | $a_0(K) > 0$ (and $a_i(K) > 0$) | Minimum gain before a real pole crosses $s = 0$ |
| **Upper / Interior Bounds** | All Column 1 pivots $c_{k,1}(K) > 0$ | Safe operating interval $(K_{\min}, K_{\max})$ |
| **Critical Oscillation** | $A(s) = \alpha_1 s^2 + \alpha_2 = 0$ at $K_{\text{crit}}$ | Marginal frequency $\omega_{\text{osc}} = \sqrt{\alpha_2 / \alpha_1}$ |

### Tuning Bounds Procedure

1. Expand $p(s, K) = D(s) + K N(s) = 0$ and collect powers of $s$ with leading coefficient $a_n(K) > 0$.
2. Enforce Stodola's necessary bounds $a_i(K) > 0$ for all $i = 0, \dots, n$.
3. Build the symbolic Routh table down to row $s^0$ as functions of $K$.
4. Solve $c_{k,1}(K) > 0$ for every row and intersect the intervals to obtain $(K_{\min}, K_{\max})$.
5. At $K = K_{\text{crit}}$, substitute $K_{\text{crit}}$ into row $s^2$ ($[\alpha_1, \alpha_2]$) to compute $\omega_{\text{osc}} = \sqrt{\alpha_2 / \alpha_1}$.

### Key Proofs

- **Auxiliary Frequency Formula:** Setting $A(j\omega_{\text{osc}}) = \alpha_1(K_{\text{crit}})(j\omega_{\text{osc}})^2 + \alpha_2(K_{\text{crit}}) = 0$ yields $\omega_{\text{osc}} = \sqrt{\alpha_2 / \alpha_1}$.
- **Denominator Sign Simplification:** Each pivot denominator $g(K)$ appears in an earlier Column 1 inequality $g(K) > 0$, keeping fraction positivity equivalent to numerator positivity $f(K) > 0$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistakes">

1. **Wrong Auxiliary Row:** For $n \ge 4$, never grab row $s^{n-2}$ blindly; always read $[\alpha_1, \alpha_2]$ from row $s^2$ immediately above the vanishing $s^1$ row.
2. **Dropping Lower Bounds:** Always include $a_0(K) > 0$ and all Stodola conditions when intersecting inequalities.
3. **Silent RHP Cancellations:** Always check that $N(s)$ and $D(s)$ share no unstable common factors before trusting $p(s, K) = 0$ alone.

</Callout>

1. Derive the closed-loop characteristic polynomial $p(s, K) = 0$.
2. Construct the complete Routh table in terms of $K$.
3. Determine the exact interval of $K > 0$ that guarantees closed-loop asymptotic stability.
4. Calculate $K_{\text{crit}}$ and the sustained oscillation frequency $\omega_{\text{osc}}$ at the stability boundary.

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. Closed-loop characteristic polynomial:
   $$D(s) = s(s + 3)(s + 5) = s^3 + 8s^2 + 15s$$
   $$p(s, K) = D(s) + K N(s) = s^3 + 8s^2 + 15s + 10K = 0$$
2. Symbolic Routh array:
   Computing row $s^1$ gives $b_1(K) = \frac{8(15) - 1(10K)}{8} = \frac{120 - 10K}{8} = 15 - 1.25K$, and row $s^0$ gives $c_1(K) = \frac{b_1(10K) - 8(0)}{b_1} = 10K$:
   $$
   \begin{array}{c|ll}
   s^3 & 1 & 15 \\
   s^2 & 8 & 10K \\
   s^1 & \frac{120 - 10K}{8} = 15 - 1.25K & 0 \\
   s^0 & 10K & 0
   \end{array}
   $$
3. Asymptotic stability positivity conditions:
   $$
   \begin{cases}
   15 - 1.25K > 0 \implies K < 12 \\
   10K > 0 \implies K > 0
   \end{cases}
   \implies \boxed{0 < K < 12}
   $$
4. Critical gain and sustained oscillation frequency:
   At $K_{\text{crit}} = 12$, row $s^1$ vanishes and row $s^2$ gives:
   $$8s^2 + 120 = 0 \implies s = \pm j\sqrt{15} \implies \omega_{\text{osc}} = \sqrt{15} \approx 3.87\text{ rad/s}$$

</details>

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. Stodola's condition on $p(s, K) = s^4 + 4s^3 + 7s^2 + 6s + K$: all coefficients $> 0 \implies K > 0$.
2. Full Routh table construction:
   * Row $s^4$: $1, \quad 7, \quad K$
   * Row $s^3$: $4, \quad 6, \quad 0 \xrightarrow{\div 2} [2, \; 3, \; 0]$
   * Row $s^2$: $b_1 = \frac{2(7) - 1(3)}{2} = 5.5, \quad b_2 = K \implies [5.5, \; K]$
   * Row $s^1$: $c_1 = \frac{5.5(3) - 2K}{5.5} = \frac{16.5 - 2K}{5.5} \implies \left[\frac{16.5 - 2K}{5.5}, \; 0\right]$
   * Row $s^0$: $K$
3. First-column inequalities and stability range:
   $$
   \begin{cases}
   16.5 - 2K > 0 \implies K < 8.25 \\
   K > 0
   \end{cases}
   \implies \boxed{0 < K < 8.25}
   $$
   Thus $K_{\max} = 8.25 = \frac{33}{4}$.
4. Pole locations at $K = 8.25$:
   Auxiliary polynomial from row $s^2$:
   $$A(s) = 5.5s^2 + 8.25 = 0 \implies s^2 = -1.5 \implies s_{1,2} = \pm j\sqrt{1.5} \approx \pm j1.225$$
   Quotient polynomial:
   $$Q(s) = \frac{s^4 + 4s^3 + 7s^2 + 6s + 8.25}{s^2 + 1.5} = s^2 + 4s + 5.5 = 0 \implies s_{3,4} = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{4^2 - 4(1)(5.5)}}{2} = -2 \pm j\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2} \approx -2 \pm j1.225$$

</details>
