## Introduction

Before tuning performance, bandwidth, or energy efficiency, control engineering enforces one non-negotiable requirement: **stability**. An unstable system amplifies tiny disturbances or sensor noise exponentially until actuators saturate or mechanical hardware fails.

Part IV provides a complete toolkit for diagnosing stability from both inside and outside the system. You start by finding stationary operating equilibria ($\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{0}$), classify internal state stability rigorously through the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}$ (including tricky repeated roots on the imaginary axis), master the algebraic **Routh-Hurwitz** table to test high-degree or symbolic polynomials without factoring, and conclude by contrasting internal state stability with external **BIBO** input-output boundedness.

### Practical Real-World Perspective

- **Flight Trim & Hover:** An aircraft cruising at constant altitude or a rocket balancing upright operates at a trim equilibrium $(\mathbf{x}_e, \mathbf{u}_0)$. Perturbation dynamics $\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_e$ let linear stability theorems govern small deviations around that trim point.
- **PID Gain Tuning Limits:** Turning up proportional controller gain $K$ on a robotic arm makes tracking snappier until you cross $K_{\text{crit}}$ and the arm breaks into violent oscillation. Routh's array predicts the exact maximum gain $K_{\max}$ and chatter frequency $\omega_{\text{osc}}$ analytically before turning on power.
- **Hidden Instability Trap:** An internal thermal or structural resonance can diverge silently while a poorly placed sensor reports bounded output—why comparing internal Lyapunov stability against external BIBO stability prevents catastrophic blind spots.
