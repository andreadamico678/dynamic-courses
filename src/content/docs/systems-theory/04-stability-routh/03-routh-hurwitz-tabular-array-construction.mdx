## Introduction

Checking stability via eigenvalues sounds simple until you face a 4th-, 5th-, or higher-order characteristic polynomial—or worse, a polynomial containing symbolic controller gains like $K$ or physical parameters like mass $m$ where closed-form root formulas do not exist. Engineers needed a fast algebraic test that detects right-half-plane roots *without ever solving $p(s) = 0$*.

The **Routh-Hurwitz criterion** turns polynomial coefficients into a compact numerical table. Just by checking sign changes down the first column, you count the exact number of unstable roots in seconds—a staple method both in embedded parameter checks and hand-written control exams.

<Divider label="1 · Example" />

Determine the root distribution of the third-order characteristic polynomial:

$$p(s) = s^3 + 2s^2 + 4s + 12$$

1. **Stodola Check:** All four coefficients $[1, \; 2, \; 4, \; 12]$ are present and strictly positive, so the polynomial passes the quick necessary screen.
2. **Build the Routh Array:** Place the even-power and odd-power coefficients alternately into the first two rows ($s^3$ and $s^2$), padding any missing trailing columns with $0$:

   $$
   \begin{array}{c|ll}
   s^3 & 1 & 4 \\
   s^2 & 2 & 12 \\
   s^1 & b_1 & 0 \\
   s^0 & c_1 & 0
   \end{array}
   $$

   Each new entry is computed from the two rows immediately above using the cross-multiplication pattern in the figure below (pivot times upper-right minus upper-left times lower-right, divided by the pivot):

   $$
   b_1 = \frac{(2)(4) - (1)(12)}{2} = \frac{8 - 12}{2} = -2, \qquad c_1 = \frac{(-2)(12) - (2)(0)}{-2} = \frac{-24}{-2} = 12
   $$

   Because $b_1 = -2$ is negative while $c_1 = 12$ returns to positive, the first column is $[+1, \; +2, \; -2, \; +12]^T$.
3. **Count Sign Changes:** Walking down the first column from $s^3$ to $s^0$, the sign flips twice ($+2 \to -2$ and $-2 \to +12$). Therefore, $n_{\text{RHP}} = 2$ and $n_{\text{LHP}} = 3 - 2 = 1$: the system is **unstable** with two right-half-plane roots, even though every polynomial coefficient was positive.

### Characteristic Polynomial Root Counting

In [Lyapunov Internal State Stability](/dynamic-courses/systems-theory/04-stability-routh/02-lyapunov-spectral-internal-stability/), we established that an LTI system is internally asymptotically stable if and only if every root of its characteristic polynomial $p(s)$ lies strictly in the open left-half complex plane ($\operatorname{Re}(s_k) < 0$). While quadratic roots ($n \le 2$) factor by the quadratic formula, the Abel-Ruffini theorem proves no general radical formula exists for $n \ge 5$, and symbolic parameters make direct root-finding impossible. In 1877, Edward John Routh devised a purely algebraic **tabular array** that counts how many roots lie in the open right-half plane ($\operatorname{Re}(s_k) > 0$) directly from polynomial coefficients.

<Callout type="bridge" title="Terminology Bridge: Matrix Eigenvalues vs. Polynomial Roots">

Earlier we classified stability using **eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}$** (tracking $m_a$ and $m_g$). The Routh-Hurwitz criterion operates on a **scalar polynomial** $p(s) = a_n s^n + \dots + a_0$ (even without a state matrix $\mathbf{A}$, such as a closed-loop denominator $D(s) + K N(s)$). Routh-Hurwitz counts the real-part signs of all **roots** of $p(s)$, but **cannot** distinguish $m_g$ from $m_a$ for repeated roots on the imaginary axis.

</Callout>

### Stodola Necessary Condition

Consider a real degree-$n$ polynomial normalized so that its leading coefficient is strictly positive ($a_n > 0$; if $a_n < 0$, multiply $p(s)$ by $-1$ without changing its roots):

$$p(s) = a_n s^n + a_{n-1} s^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 s + a_0 = 0, \quad a_n > 0$$

By definition, $p(s)$ is **strictly Hurwitz** if and only if all $n$ roots satisfy $\operatorname{Re}(s_k) < 0$. Any real polynomial with strictly LHP roots factors completely into linear LHP terms $(s + \alpha_k)$ with $\alpha_k > 0$ and complex-conjugate LHP quadratic terms $(s^2 + 2\zeta_m \omega_{n,m} s + \omega_{n,m}^2)$ with $\zeta_m > 0$ and $\omega_{n,m} > 0$, scaled by $a_n > 0$:

$$
p(s) = a_n \prod_{k} (s + \alpha_k) \prod_{m} \left(s^2 + 2\zeta_m \omega_{n,m} s + \omega_{n,m}^2\right)
$$

Because every factor contains only strictly positive coefficients, expanding their product can only add positive numbers—it can never produce a zero or negative coefficient. This proves **Stodola's necessary condition**:

$$\boxed{a_i > 0 \quad \forall i = 0, 1, \dots, n \quad (\text{given } a_n > 0)}$$

If any coefficient $a_k = 0$ (a missing power of $s$) or $a_k < 0$, the polynomial immediately fails strict Hurwitz stability. For $n = 1$ ($a_1 s + a_0 = 0$) and $n = 2$ ($a_2 s^2 + a_1 s + a_0 = 0$, whose roots are $\frac{-a_1 \pm \sqrt{a_1^2 - 4a_2 a_0}}{2a_2}$), $a_i > 0$ is both necessary and sufficient. For $n \ge 3$, however, it is **necessary only**: a stable LHP factor can mask the negative term of an unstable quadratic, as in $s^3 + s^2 + 2s + 8 = (s+2)(s^2 - s + 4)$, which has all positive coefficients yet RHP roots $s = +0.5 \pm j\frac{\sqrt{15}}{2}$.

### Routh Tabular Algorithm

When Stodola's condition holds and no first-column pivot vanishes (the regular case), populate rows $s^n$ and $s^{n-1}$ with alternating coefficients and compute each subsequent row from the two rows immediately above (treating missing right-hand entries as $0$):

$$
\begin{array}{c|llll}
s^n & a_n & a_{n-2} & a_{n-4} & \dots \\
s^{n-1} & a_{n-1} & a_{n-3} & a_{n-5} & \dots \\
s^{n-2} & b_1 & b_2 & b_3 & \dots \\
s^{n-3} & c_1 & c_2 & c_3 & \dots \\
\vdots & \vdots & & & \\
s^0 & a_0 & & &
\end{array}
$$

Each entry in row $s^{n-2}$ is the negative $2 \times 2$ determinant of the first column and column $(j+1)$ divided by the pivot $a_{n-1}$:

$$\boxed{b_1 = \frac{a_{n-1} a_{n-2} - a_n a_{n-3}}{a_{n-1}}}, \qquad \boxed{b_2 = \frac{a_{n-1} a_{n-4} - a_n a_{n-5}}{a_{n-1}}}$$

Repeating the exact same rule one row lower gives row $s^{n-3}$ from rows $s^{n-1}$ and $s^{n-2}$:

$$c_1 = \frac{b_1 a_{n-3} - a_{n-1} b_2}{b_1}, \qquad c_2 = \frac{b_1 a_{n-5} - a_{n-1} b_3}{b_1}$$

Notice that multiplying or dividing any complete row by a **strictly positive scalar** $k > 0$ preserves all signs in that row and leaves every subsequent cross-product ratio invariant, because $\frac{(k p) x - y (k q)}{k p} = \frac{p x - y q}{p}$. This lets you clear fractions cleanly by hand without altering the result. By **Routh's criterion**:

1. $p(s)$ is strictly Hurwitz ($\operatorname{Re}(s_k) < 0$ for all $k$) **if and only if** all $(n + 1)$ entries in the first column are strictly positive.
2. The number of open right-half-plane roots $n_{\text{RHP}}$ equals the **number of sign changes** in the first column from $s^n$ down to $s^0$; in the regular case (no imaginary-axis roots), $n_{\text{LHP}} = n - n_{\text{RHP}}$.

For low-order monic polynomials with $a_i > 0$, evaluating the first column directly yields closed-form Hurwitz inequalities:
* **3rd-order** ($s^3 + a_2 s^2 + a_1 s + a_0$): Row $s^1$ is $b_1 = \frac{a_2 a_1 - a_0}{a_2} > 0$, which simplifies (since $a_2 > 0$) to $\boxed{a_2 a_1 > a_0}$.
* **4th-order** ($s^4 + a_3 s^3 + a_2 s^2 + a_1 s + a_0$): Row $s^2$ is $[b_1, b_2] = \left[\frac{a_3 a_2 - a_1}{a_3},\; a_0\right]$, and row $s^1$ is $c_1 = \frac{b_1 a_1 - a_3 a_0}{b_1} > 0$. Requiring $b_1 > 0$ and $b_1 a_1 > a_3 a_0$ gives $\frac{a_3 a_2 - a_1}{a_3} a_1 > a_3 a_0$, or $\boxed{a_3 a_2 a_1 > a_1^2 + a_3^2 a_0}$.

In differential-drive mobile robots, a permanent-magnet DC motor ($J, b, L, R$) under PI speed control $C(s) = K_p + K_i/s$ yields a 3rd-order characteristic polynomial. Applying $a_2 a_1 > a_0$ directly bounds the maximum integral gain $K_i$ before armature current oscillations destabilize the H-bridge driver.

</Callout>

<Divider label="3 · Summary" />

| Polynomial Degree | Necessary Condition | Sufficient Hurwitz Condition (given $a_n > 0$) | RHP Root Count |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Degree 1** ($a_1 s + a_0$) | $a_1, a_0 > 0$ | Same ($a_0 > 0$) | $0$ if $a_0 > 0$ |
| **Degree 2** ($s^2 + a_1 s + a_0$) | $a_1, a_0 > 0$ | Same ($a_1 > 0, \; a_0 > 0$) | $0$ if $a_1, a_0 > 0$ |
| **Degree 3** ($s^3 + a_2 s^2 + a_1 s + a_0$) | $a_2, a_1, a_0 > 0$ | $a_2 a_1 > a_0$ | Sign changes in Col 1 |
| **Degree 4** ($s^4 + a_3 s^3 + a_2 s^2 + a_1 s + a_0$) | $a_3, a_2, a_1, a_0 > 0$ | $a_3 a_2 a_1 > a_1^2 + a_3^2 a_0$ | Sign changes in Col 1 |

### Construction Procedure

1. Normalize $a_n > 0$ (multiply $p(s)$ by $-1$ if $a_n < 0$) and check Stodola's condition ($a_i > 0$ for all $i$).
2. Populate row $s^n$ and row $s^{n-1}$ with alternating coefficients of $p(s)$.
3. Compute rows $s^{n-2}$ down to $s^0$ via cross-multiplication, optionally scaling any row by $k > 0$.
4. Count sign changes down Column 1 from $s^n$ to $s^0$ to obtain $n_{\text{RHP}}$ and $n_{\text{LHP}} = n - n_{\text{RHP}}$.

### Key Proofs

- **Stodola Necessity:** Expanding a product of strictly positive linear and quadratic LHP factors produces strictly positive coefficients $a_i > 0$.
- **Positive Scaling Invariance:** Multiplying row $s^k$ by $k > 0$ scales its pivot and entries identically, leaving subsequent cross-product ratios $(k p \cdot x - y \cdot k q)/(k p)$ invariant.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common Mistakes">

1. **Treating Stodola as Sufficient:** Positive coefficients never guarantee stability for $n \ge 3$; always build the Routh array or check the Hurwitz inequalities.
2. **Negative Row Scaling:** Never multiply a Routh row by a negative scalar; flipping row signs corrupts the sign-change count.
3. **Missing Powers of $s$:** If any power $s^k$ is absent ($a_k = 0$), write `0` explicitly in the alternating rows—never shift later coefficients left.

</Callout>

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. Stodola necessary condition:
   For $p(s) = s^3 + 2s^2 + 4s + 12$, the coefficients $[1, \; 2, \; 4, \; 12]$ are all strictly positive with no missing terms.
2. Complete Routh table construction:
   * Row $s^3$: $1, \quad 4$
   * Row $s^2$: $2, \quad 12$
   * Row $s^1$: $b_1 = \frac{2(4) - 1(12)}{2} = \frac{-4}{2} = -2 \implies [-2, \; 0]$
   * Row $s^0$: $c_1 = \frac{-2(12) - 2(0)}{-2} = 12 \implies [12, \; 0]$
   $$
   \begin{array}{c|ll}
   s^3 & +1 & 4 \\
   s^2 & +2 & 12 \\
   s^1 & -2 & 0 \\
   s^0 & +12 & 0
   \end{array}
   $$
3. First column elements and signs:
   $$\text{Column 1} = \begin{bmatrix} +1 \\ +2 \\ -2 \\ +12 \end{bmatrix} \implies \begin{bmatrix} + \\ + \\ - \\ + \end{bmatrix}$$
4. Root distribution and stability:
   * Transition $+2 \to -2$: 1st sign change.
   * Transition $-2 \to +12$: 2nd sign change.
   With **2 sign variations**, $n_{\text{RHP}} = 2$ and $n_{\text{LHP}} = 3 - 2 = 1$; the system is **unstable**.

</details>

</Exercise>

<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. Stodola check:
   For $p(s) = s^4 + 2s^3 + 3s^2 + 4s + 5$, all coefficients $[1, 2, 3, 4, 5]$ are strictly positive.
2. Tabular array construction:
   * Row $s^4$: $1, \quad 3, \quad 5$
   * Row $s^3$: $2, \quad 4, \quad 0 \xrightarrow{\div 2} [1, \; 2, \; 0]$
   * Row $s^2$: $b_1 = \frac{1(3) - 1(2)}{1} = 1, \quad b_2 = \frac{1(5) - 1(0)}{1} = 5 \implies [1, \; 5]$
   * Row $s^1$: $c_1 = \frac{1(2) - 1(5)}{1} = -3 \implies [-3, \; 0]$
   * Row $s^0$: $d_1 = \frac{-3(5) - 1(0)}{-3} = 5 \implies [5, \; 0]$
   $$
   \begin{array}{c|lll}
   s^4 & +1 & 3 & 5 \\
   s^3 & +2 \;(\to +1) & 4 \;(\to 2) & 0 \\
   s^2 & +1 & 5 & 0 \\
   s^1 & -3 & 0 & 0 \\
   s^0 & +5 & 0 & 0
   \end{array}
   $$
3. First column signs: $\text{Column 1} = [+1, \; +1, \; +1, \; -3, \; +5]^T$ has **2 sign changes** ($+1 \to -3$ and $-3 \to +5$).
4. Root distribution and conclusion:
   $n_{\text{RHP}} = 2$ and $n_{\text{LHP}} = 4 - 2 = 2$; the system is **unstable**.

</details>
