## Intuition and Physical Meaning
An LTI system is internally asymptotically stable if and only if every root of its characteristic polynomial $p(s)$ lies strictly in the open left-half complex plane ($\operatorname{Re}(s_k) < 0$). For degree $n = 1$ or $n = 2$, finding the roots directly is trivial. For $n \ge 3$, symbolic root formulas become unwieldy, and for $n \ge 5$, the Abel-Ruffini theorem proves that no general closed-form radical formula exists.

In 1877, Edward John Routh devised a purely algebraic **tabular array** that counts how many roots lie in the open right-half plane ($\operatorname{Re}(s_k) > 0$) using only the polynomial coefficients—without ever factoring $p(s)$.

<Callout type="bridge" title="Terminology Bridge: Matrix Eigenvalues vs. Polynomial Roots">

In previous chapters we classified stability using the **eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}$** (with algebraic multiplicity $m_a$ and geometric multiplicity $m_g$). The Routh-Hurwitz criterion operates on a **scalar polynomial** $p(s) = a_n s^n + \dots + a_0$ (even when no state matrix $\mathbf{A}$ is given, such as a closed-loop transfer denominator $D(s) + K N(s)$). Because $p(s)$ is scalar, Routh-Hurwitz determines the real-part sign of every **root** of $p(s)$ but **cannot** distinguish $m_g$ from $m_a$ for repeated roots on the imaginary axis.

</Callout>

## Formal Definition and Derivation (Oral Core)

Consider a real polynomial of degree $n$ normalized so that its leading coefficient is strictly positive ($a_n > 0$):
$$p(s) = a_n s^n + a_{n-1} s^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 s + a_0 = 0, \quad a_n > 0$$

A polynomial $p(s)$ is **strictly Hurwitz** (asymptotically stable) if and only if all $n$ of its roots satisfy $\operatorname{Re}(s_k) < 0$.

### Derivation / Oral Proof: Stodola's Necessary Condition

If all roots of $p(s)$ lie in the open left-half plane, $p(s)$ factors into a product of linear terms $(s + \alpha_k)$ with $\alpha_k > 0$ and quadratic terms $(s^2 + 2\zeta_m \omega_{n,m} s + \omega_{n,m}^2)$ with $\zeta_m > 0, \omega_{n,m} > 0$, scaled by $a_n > 0$. Expanding a product of factors whose coefficients are all strictly positive cannot produce a zero or negative coefficient. Therefore, a **necessary** condition for strict Hurwitz stability (Stodola's rule) is that every coefficient is strictly positive:
$$\boxed{a_i > 0 \quad \forall i = 0, 1, \dots, n \quad (\text{given } a_n > 0)}$$

If any $a_k = 0$ (missing power of $s$) or $a_k < 0$ (sign flip), $p(s)$ is **immediately unstable or at best marginally stable**—there is no need to build the Routh table.

<Callout type="warning" title="Exam Pitfall: Stodola Is Necessary, NOT Sufficient for $n \ge 3$">

For $n = 1$ and $n = 2$, $a_i > 0$ is both necessary and sufficient. For $n \ge 3$, positive coefficients **do not** guarantee stability. For example, $p(s) = s^3 + s^2 + 2s + 8 = (s + 2)(s^2 - s + 4)$ has all positive coefficients $[1, 1, 2, 8]$, yet its complex roots are $s = +0.5 \pm j\frac{\sqrt{15}}{2}$ in the open right-half plane.

</Callout>

### Routh's Stability Criterion

Assuming Stodola's condition holds and no row pivot vanishes (the regular case):
1. A polynomial $p(s)$ is strictly Hurwitz ($\operatorname{Re}(s_k) < 0$ for all $k$) **if and only if** all $(n+1)$ entries in the first column of the Routh table are strictly positive.
2. The number of roots in the open right-half plane ($n_{\text{RHP}}$) equals the **number of sign changes** in the first column.
3. In the regular case (no zeros in the first column), there are no roots on the imaginary axis, so $n_{\text{LHP}} = n - n_{\text{RHP}}$.

## Operational Algorithm (Written Exam Core)

### Step 1: Check Stodola's Necessary Condition
Verify $a_n > 0$ (if $a_n < 0$, multiply $p(s)$ by $-1$) and confirm every $a_i > 0$ for $i = 0, \dots, n$.

### Step 2: Populate the First Two Rows
Arrange the coefficients in alternating order across row $s^n$ and row $s^{n-1}$:
$$\begin{array}{c|llll}
s^n & a_n & a_{n-2} & a_{n-4} & \dots \\
s^{n-1} & a_{n-1} & a_{n-3} & a_{n-5} & \dots \\
s^{n-2} & b_1 & b_2 & b_3 & \dots \\
s^{n-3} & c_1 & c_2 & c_3 & \dots \\
\vdots & \vdots & & & \\
s^0 & a_0 & & &
\end{array}$$

### Step 3: Compute Subsequent Rows via Cross-Multiplication
Each entry in row $s^{n-2}$ is computed from the two rows immediately above using the leading pivot $a_{n-1}$ ([the figure](#fig-routh-pattern)):
$$\boxed{b_1 = \frac{a_{n-1} a_{n-2} - a_n a_{n-3}}{a_{n-1}}}, \qquad \boxed{b_2 = \frac{a_{n-1} a_{n-4} - a_n a_{n-5}}{a_{n-1}}}$$
and similarly for row $s^{n-3}$ using pivot $b_1$:
$$c_1 = \frac{b_1 a_{n-3} - a_{n-1} b_2}{b_1}, \qquad c_2 = \frac{b_1 a_{n-5} - a_{n-1} b_3}{b_1}$$
Missing entries to the right are treated as $0$. Any entire row may be multiplied or divided by a **strictly positive scalar** ($k > 0$) to clear fractions without affecting the first-column signs.

### Step 4: Count Sign Changes in the First Column
Read the first column from $s^n$ down to $s^0$. Every sign flip ($+ \to -$ or $- \to +$) counts as one RHP root. For standard low-order monic polynomials with $a_i > 0$, this yields the analytical Hurwitz inequalities:
* **3rd-order** ($s^3 + a_2 s^2 + a_1 s + a_0$): $\boxed{a_2 a_1 > a_0}$
* **4th-order** ($s^4 + a_3 s^3 + a_2 s^2 + a_1 s + a_0$): $\boxed{a_3 a_2 a_1 > a_1^2 + a_3^2 a_0}$

## Worked Exam Archetype and Pitfalls

### Worked Example: Third-Order Characteristic Polynomial

Determine the root distribution of:
$$p(s) = s^3 + 2s^2 + 4s + 12$$

1. **Stodola Check:** $[1, 2, 4, 12]$ are all strictly positive.
2. **Routh Table:**
   $$\begin{array}{c|ll}
   s^3 & 1 & 4 \\
   s^2 & 2 & 12 \\
   s^1 & b_1 & 0 \\
   s^0 & c_1 & 0
   \end{array}$$
   $$b_1 = \frac{(2)(4) - (1)(12)}{2} = \frac{-4}{2} = -2, \qquad c_1 = \frac{(-2)(12) - (2)(0)}{-2} = 12$$
3. **First Column Inspection:** $[+1,\; +2,\; -2,\; +12]^T$ exhibits **2 sign changes** ($+2 \to -2$ and $-2 \to +12$).
   Therefore, $n_{\text{RHP}} = 2$ and $n_{\text{LHP}} = 3 - 2 = 1$; the system is **unstable**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Exam Pitfall: Scaling a Row by a Negative Number">

Never multiply a Routh row by a negative scalar! Multiplying by $-1$ flips the sign of that first-column entry and corrupts the sign-change count. Only strictly positive scalars ($k > 0$) preserve stability.

</Callout>

<Callout type="bridge" title="SDU Robotics Bridge: DC Motor PI Speed Loop Tuning">

In differential-drive mobile robots, a permanent-magnet DC motor ($J, b, L, R$) under PI speed control $C(s) = K_p + K_i/s$ yields a 3rd-order characteristic polynomial. Applying $a_2 a_1 > a_0$ directly bounds the maximum integral gain $K_i$ before armature current oscillations destabilize the H-bridge driver.

</Callout>

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. Stodola necessary condition:
   For $p(s) = s^3 + 2s^2 + 4s + 12$, the coefficients are $[1, \; 2, \; 4, \; 12]$.
   All coefficients are strictly positive ($> 0$) and no term is missing. The necessary condition for stability is satisfied.
2. Complete Routh table construction:
   * Row $s^3$: $1, \quad 4$
   * Row $s^2$: $2, \quad 12$
   * Row $s^1$:
     $$b_1 = \frac{2(4) - 1(12)}{2} = \frac{8 - 12}{2} = \frac{-4}{2} = -2$$
     Row $s^1$: $-2, \quad 0$
   * Row $s^0$:
     $$c_1 = \frac{-2(12) - 2(0)}{-2} = 12$$
     Row $s^0$: $12, \quad 0$
   Tabular array:
   $$\begin{array}{c|ll}
   s^3 & +1 & 4 \\
   s^2 & +2 & 12 \\
   s^1 & -2 & 0 \\
   s^0 & +12 & 0
   \end{array}$$
3. First column elements and signs:
   $$\text{Column 1} = \begin{bmatrix} +1 \\ +2 \\ -2 \\ +12 \end{bmatrix} \implies \begin{bmatrix} + \\ + \\ - \\ + \end{bmatrix}$$
4. Root distribution and stability:
   * Transition $+2 \to -2$: 1st sign change.
   * Transition $-2 \to +12$: 2nd sign change.
   There are exactly **2 sign variations** in the first column.
   * Roots in the open right-half plane: $n_{\text{RHP}} = 2$.
   * Roots in the open left-half plane: $n_{\text{LHP}} = 3 - 2 = 1$.
   The polynomial is strictly **unstable** (not asymptotically stable).

</details>

Consider the 4th-order characteristic polynomial:

1. Verify Stodola's necessary condition.
2. Construct the full Routh tabular array, using positive row-scaling where helpful.

<details className="dc-solution">
<summary>Reveal Worked Solution</summary>

1. Stodola check:
   For $p(s) = s^4 + 2s^3 + 3s^2 + 4s + 5$, all coefficients $[1, 2, 3, 4, 5]$ are strictly positive. The necessary condition holds.
2. Tabular array construction:
   * Row $s^4$: $1, \quad 3, \quad 5$
   * Row $s^3$: $2, \quad 4, \quad 0$. Dividing the row by $2$ simplifies subsequent calculations: $[1, \; 2, \; 0]$.
   * Row $s^2$:
     $$b_1 = \frac{1(3) - 1(2)}{1} = 1, \quad b_2 = \frac{1(5) - 1(0)}{1} = 5 \implies [1, \; 5]$$
   * Row $s^1$:
     $$c_1 = \frac{1(2) - 1(5)}{1} = -3 \implies [-3, \; 0]$$
   * Row $s^0$:
     $$d_1 = \frac{-3(5) - 1(0)}{-3} = 5 \implies [5, \; 0]$$
   Completed Routh array:
   $$\begin{array}{c|lll}
   s^4 & +1 & 3 & 5 \\
   s^3 & +2 \;(\to +1) & 4 \;(\to 2) & 0 \\
   s^2 & +1 & 5 & 0 \\
   s^1 & -3 & 0 & 0 \\
   s^0 & +5 & 0 & 0
   \end{array}$$
3. First column signs:
   $$\text{Column 1} = \begin{bmatrix} +1 \\ +1 \\ +1 \\ -3 \\ +5 \end{bmatrix}$$
   * $+1 \to +1 \to +1$: 0 changes.
   * $+1 \to -3$: **1st sign change**.
   * $-3 \to +5$: **2nd sign change**.
4. Root distribution and stability conclusion:
   Because there are exactly 2 sign changes in the first column:
   * $n_{\text{RHP}} = 2$ (a pair of complex conjugate poles in the open right-half plane).
   * $n_{\text{LHP}} = 4 - 2 = 2$ (two poles in the open left-half plane).
   The system is **unstable**.

</details>

</Exercise>
