## Introduction

Differential equations in the time domain require solving coupled derivatives and convolution integrals—hard by hand whenever system order exceeds $n = 2$. Part II shifts the entire problem into the **complex frequency domain ($s$-domain)**, where differentiation $\frac{d}{dt}$ becomes simple multiplication by $s$ and an entire system of linear differential equations collapses into linear matrix algebra.

You will follow a complete two-way bridge: first converting signals and state equations from time $t$ into rational functions of $s$ via the **State Resolvent Matrix** $(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}$, and then decomposing those rational fractions into simple terms so you can read off natural decay rates, oscillation frequencies, and steady-state behavior directly.

### Practical Real-World Perspective

- **Embedded Flight & Motor Diagnostics:** Measuring a motor's step response in the lab tells you its transfer function $W(s)$. Partial fraction residues instantly separate a fast electrical current transient from a slow mechanical rotor inertia mode.
- **Hidden Internal Faults:** Two wildly different physical circuits or mechanical linkages can share the exact same external transfer function $\mathbf{W}(s) = \mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{D}$ if an internal mode cancels before reaching the sensor. Part II reveals the exact distinction between internal matrix eigenvalues and external transfer-function poles.
