## Introduction

Solving coupled differential equations and convolution integrals directly in the time domain becomes algebraic guesswork once system order exceeds $n = 2$. Part II moves the entire problem into the **complex frequency domain** ($s$-domain), turning differentiation $\frac{d}{dt}$ into multiplication by $s$ and coupled state equations into linear matrix algebra.

Chapters 4–7 build a two-way operational bridge: converting time signals and initial conditions into rational functions of $s$, inverting the **State Resolvent Matrix** $(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}$, decomposing fractions via residues, and mapping internal matrices $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}, \mathbf{D})$ to external transfer functions $\mathbf{W}(s)$.

### Running Example

Across Part II, we carry forward second- and third-order state-space dynamics in the $s$-domain—converting initial-energy ODEs and Frobenius companion matrices $\mathbf{A}$ through the resolvent:

$$\mathbf{\Phi}(s) = (s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}, \qquad \mathbf{W}(s) = \mathbf{C}(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{D}$$

Chapter 4 handles $t = 0^-$ initial conditions via Laplace theorems, Chapter 5 recovers time modes from residues, Chapter 6 inverts $(s\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{A})$ via its adjugate, and Chapter 7 exposes pole-zero cancellation inside $\mathbf{W}(s)$.

### Real-World Applications

- **Motor & Airframe Diagnostics:** Partial fraction residues immediately isolate fast electrical transients from slower mechanical structural modes.
- **Hidden Internal Modes:** Two distinct physical circuits or linkages can share the exact same $\mathbf{W}(s)$ if an internal eigenvalue cancels before reaching the sensor.
