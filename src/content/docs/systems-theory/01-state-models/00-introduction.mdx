## Introduction

Before analyzing stability or designing controllers, an engineer must choose a mathematical language to describe how a physical system evolves over time. High-level input-output differential equations break down as soon as a system has multiple actuators, multiple sensors, or internal energy-storing elements whose internal behavior matters even when the output appears stationary.

Part I establishes the **state-space representation**. Instead of tracking derivatives up to order $n$ of a single output, you pack the system's instantaneous memory—positions, velocities, capacitor voltages, inductor currents—into a state vector $\mathbf{x}(t)$ governed by first-order equations $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}$.

### Running Example

Across Chapters 1–3, we analyze a horizontal **coupled two-cart mechanical bench** of masses $m_1, m_2$, coupling spring $k$, wall damper $B_1$, and actuator force $u(t)$, assigning positions and velocities $\mathbf{x} = [p_1, \dot{p}_1, p_2, \dot{p}_2]^T \in \mathbb{R}^4$:

$$\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}(t) + \mathbf{B}u(t), \qquad y(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{x}(t) + Du(t)$$

Chapter 1 derives $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}, D)$ from Newton's laws, Chapter 2 shows how coordinate transformations $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{T}\mathbf{z}$ diagonalize coupled dynamics while preserving physical eigenvalues, and Chapter 3 collapses interconnected block diagrams into equivalent transfer operators $W(s)$.

### Real-World Applications

- **Suspension & Robotics:** Choosing position and velocity states lets one matrix model predict structural oscillation regardless of whether sensors measure position, velocity, or spring stretch.
- **Coordinate Frames:** Rotating IMU coordinates from body frame to Earth frame leaves physical system modes and input-output behavior completely invariant.
- **Modular Feedback Architectures:** Cascades of motor drivers, actuators, sensors, and feedback loops simplify systematically via block diagram algebra.
